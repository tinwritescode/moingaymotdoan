# Luke 11 — Teach Us to Pray

## Story

One day Jesus was praying in a certain place. When He finished, one of His disciples said to Him, "Lord, teach us to pray, just as John taught his disciples." He told them: when you pray, say, "Father, hallowed be Your name. Your kingdom come. Give us each day our daily bread. And forgive us our sins, for we also forgive everyone who sins against us. And lead us not into temptation" (11:1–4). Luke's Lord's Prayer is leaner than Matthew 6:9–13. Early copies preserve this concise wording, while many later manuscripts filled in phrases from Matthew such as "in heaven" and "Your will be done."

Then He told them: suppose one of you has a friend and goes to him at midnight saying, "Friend, lend me three loaves of bread, because a friend of mine on a journey has come to me and I have nothing to set before him." From inside the man replies: do not bother me; the door is already locked and my children are with me in bed; I cannot get up and give you anything. I tell you, even though he will not get up and give him anything out of friendship, yet because of his shameless persistence he will get up and give him as much as he needs (11:5–8). In ancient villages, an entire family slept together in one room; opening the door woke everybody up. Yet even that sleepy neighbor gets up—and God is no reluctant sleeper; if a neighbor still helps, how much more will God!

"Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and the door will be opened to you." Everyone who asks receives, everyone who seeks finds, and to everyone who knocks the door will be opened. Which father among you, if his son asks for a fish, will give him a snake instead—or if he asks for an egg, will give him a scorpion? If you then, though you are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, "how much more will your Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!" (11:9–13). Matthew records "good gifts" (Matthew 7:11); Luke names "the Holy Spirit." The finest gift our Father can give is His own presence. Some later copies add "bread or a stone" in verse 11, absent from the oldest manuscripts.

Jesus was driving out a demon that was mute; when the demon left, the mute man spoke, and the crowds marveled. Yet some of them said, "By Beelzebul, the prince of the demons, He drives out demons." Others, to test Him, demanded a sign from heaven. Knowing their thoughts, He said: every kingdom divided against itself is laid waste, and a divided household falls. If Satan is divided against himself, how can his kingdom stand? If I drive out demons by Beelzebul, by whom do your own people drive them out? "But if I drive out demons by the finger of God, then the kingdom of God has come upon you" (11:14–20; Exodus 8:19). "The finger of God" was the exact phrase Egyptian magicians exclaimed when bested by Moses' miracles; Jesus reclaims it here.

When a strong man, fully armed, guards his own palace, his goods are safe. Yet when someone stronger attacks and overpowers him, he strips away the armor in which the man trusted and divides up his plunder. Whoever is not with Me is against Me, and whoever does not gather with Me scatters. When an impure spirit comes out of a person, it roams through waterless places looking for rest; finding none, it says: I will return to the house I left. Arriving to find it swept clean and put in order, it goes and brings seven other spirits more wicked than itself, and they move in—leaving that person's final state worse than the first (11:21–26). A swept house left empty is never safe. There is no neutral ground: a liberated heart needs a new Owner inside.

As He spoke, a woman in the crowd raised her voice: "Blessed is the womb that bore You, and blessed are the breasts that nursed You!" He replied, "Blessed rather are those who hear the word of God and obey it" (11:27–28). He is not taking away His mother's honor—Mary herself was blessed precisely as one who believed God's word (1:45)—yet opens that supreme blessing to everyone who hears and obeys.

As the crowds swelled, He declared, "This is a wicked generation. It demands a sign, but none will be given it except the sign of Jonah." Just as Jonah became a sign to the Ninevites, so will the Son of Man be to this generation. At the judgment the Queen of the South will rise up and condemn them, because she traveled from the ends of the earth to listen to Solomon's wisdom—"and now One greater than Solomon is here." The men of Nineveh will rise up and condemn them too, because they repented at Jonah's preaching—"and now One greater than Jonah is here" (11:29–32; 1 Kings 10:1–10; Jonah 3:5). Both witnesses are Gentiles: outsiders responded wholeheartedly to far less light, whereas this generation holds far greater light and still demands proof.

Nobody lights a lamp and hides it in a cellar or under a basket, but puts it on a stand so those entering see the light. "Your eye is the lamp of your body": when your eye is healthy, your whole body is full of light; when it is bad, your body is full of darkness. See to it then that the light within you is not darkness. If your whole body is full of light with no dark part, it will shine completely as when a lamp's bright beam illuminates you (11:33–36). Light already shines right in front of them; the question is whether our eyes open to receive it.

As soon as He finished speaking, a Pharisee invited Him to dine, and Jesus went in and reclined at table. Astonished to see He had not first washed before the meal, the Pharisee marveled. The Lord told him: you Pharisees clean the outside of cup and dish, while inside you are full of greed and wickedness. "You fools! Did not the One who made the outside make the inside as well?" Give what is inside to the poor, and everything will be clean for you. Woe to you Pharisees, because you tithe mint, rue, and every garden herb, yet neglect justice and the love of God; you should practice the latter without neglecting the former. Woe to you, because you love the front seats in synagogues and greetings in marketplaces. Woe to you, because you are like unmarked graves that people walk over without knowing it (11:37–44; Numbers 19:16). Stepping on a grave brought ritual defilement—so the people priding themselves on spotless purity quietly defile everyone around them without anyone noticing.

One of the experts in the Law answered, "Teacher, when You say these things, You insult us as well." Jesus replied: woe to you experts in the Law too, because you load people down with unbearable burdens while refusing to lift a finger yourselves. Woe to you, because you build tombs for the prophets your ancestors killed—testifying and approving your ancestors' deeds! Therefore God's wisdom declared: I will send them prophets and apostles, some of whom they will kill and persecute, so this generation will be held accountable for the blood of all the prophets shed since the foundation of the world, "from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zechariah, who was killed between the altar and the sanctuary." Woe to you experts in the Law, because you have taken away the key of knowledge: you yourselves did not enter, and you hindered those trying to enter (11:45–52; Genesis 4:8; 2 Chronicles 24:20–22). Abel is the first martyr in Scripture's opening book; Zechariah is the last martyr in Chronicles, the closing book of the Hebrew Bible—spanning their entire Bible from cover to cover.

When He left there, the scribes and Pharisees began opposing Him fiercely and pelting Him with questions on many subjects, lying in wait to catch Him in something He might say (11:53–54). A dinner that began with an invitation ends in a conspiracy.

## Key Takeaways

**Prayer means approaching a generous Father, knocking boldly for His finest gift: the Holy Spirit.** Jesus gives a compact model prayer, adds the midnight neighbor story, and appeals to ordinary fatherhood to prove our heavenly Father gladly gives the Holy Spirit to anyone who asks (11:1–13).

**Nobody can sit on the fence once God's kingdom arrives.** When Jesus drives out demons "by the finger of God," the Stronger One has defeated darkness (11:20–22). Whoever is not with Him stands against Him, and a cleaned-out life left unoccupied invites darkness straight back in (11:23–26).

**Plenty of light is already shining; the issue is whether our eyes open or keep demanding stunts.** Nineveh repented for Jonah and Sheba's queen crossed continents for Solomon, while this generation brushes past One far greater standing right before them (11:29–36).

**Outward polish paired with inner neglect of justice and love is deadly hypocrisy.** Six woes over dinner expose scrubbing exterior cups and counting herb leaves while harboring greed, pride, and crushing burdens on others (11:37–52).

## Deeper Meaning

**"The finger of God" echoes Exodus liberation.** Egyptian sorcerers themselves admitted Moses' miracles sprang from "the finger of God" (Exodus 8:19). Reclaiming that phrase (11:20), Jesus announces an even greater deliverance from spiritual bondage.

**Jonah and the Queen of the South highlight Gentiles welcoming God's light.** Both Nineveh (Jonah 3:5) and Sheba's queen (1 Kings 10:1–9) stood outside Israel's covenant yet listened humbly and turned toward truth (11:29–32)—shaming those holding Scripture in hand who stay hardhearted.

**Promising the Holy Spirit points straight toward Pentecost.** Where Matthew speaks generally of "good things" (Matthew 7:11), Luke explicitly names the Holy Spirit (11:13), paving the road to the outpouring of the Spirit in Acts (Acts 1:8; Acts 2:1–4).

**"From the blood of Abel to the blood of Zechariah" spans the whole Old Testament.** In the Hebrew canon's order, Abel falls first in Genesis (Genesis 4:8) and Zechariah the priest is stoned inside the temple court in the final book, 2 Chronicles (2 Chronicles 24:20–21)—summing up centuries of rejecting God's messengers (11:51).

**Neglecting justice and love echoes the Old Testament prophets.** Jesus affirms tithing while restoring true priorities (11:42), carrying forward Micah's timeless word: act justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with your God (Micah 6:8).

## Notes & Nuances

**Luke's Lord's Prayer (11:2–4) is shorter than Matthew's (6:9–13).** Opening simply with "Father" and five concise petitions, the difference reminds us that Jesus offered a living template to guide prayer rather than a rigid magic chant.

**The midnight friend's attitude in 11:8 carries two complementary angles.** The Greek word literally means "shamelessness": many translations hear the persistent shamelessness of the friend knocking at midnight, while scholars of Middle Eastern village honor hear the homeowner rising to avoid bringing *shame* on himself and the village by refusing hospitality. Either way, the punchline shines clear: if a reluctant neighbor comes through, how much more our gracious Father!

**Verses 11:27–28 never belittle Mary.** When a bystander praises Mary's biological motherhood, Jesus lifts everyone to a deeper blessedness: hearing and keeping God's word—the exact faithfulness Mary herself modeled in chapter 1 (1:45).

**The six woes (11:39–52) are no blanket attack on Jews or every Pharisee.** After all, a Pharisee invited Jesus to dinner (11:37), and Jesus explicitly affirms tithing (11:42). His fire targets religious hypocrisy—polishing public piety while ignoring justice and the poor—a temptation threatening believers in every age.

## What Commentators Say

**Tertullian**, the early third-century theologian of Carthage, famously called the Lord's Prayer (11:2–4) "a summary of the whole gospel." Inside just a few short petitions, Christ anchors every essential of life as God's child: hallowing the Father, longing for His reign, trusting Him for daily bread, practicing forgiveness, and asking protection from temptation. *(On Prayer, chapter 1)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: calling the Lord's Prayer a summary of the whole gospel appears in chapter 1 of Tertullian's On Prayer -->

**Cyril of Alexandria**, fifth-century bishop, lingers on Luke's distinctive promise in 11:13 that the Father gives the Holy Spirit to those who ask. Every genuine blessing flows inside the Spirit Himself, Cyril explains, since possessing the Spirit means possessing God's own life and presence within us. *(Homilies on Luke)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: confident in Cyril's theological reflection on Luke 11:13 naming the Holy Spirit; exact homily number unverified -->

**John Calvin**, the sixteenth-century French Reformer, reflects on 11:27–28 that Mary's supreme honor was never carrying Christ merely in her physical womb, but conceiving Him inside her heart through faith and obedience—a high blessing open to everyone who hears and keeps God's word. *(Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists, on Luke 11)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: contrasting physical conception with conceiving Christ through faith is Calvin's celebrated point, echoing Augustine, on Luke 11:27–28 -->

**Kenneth Bailey**, an American scholar who lived decades in the Middle East, illuminates the midnight friend (11:5–8) through village hospitality honor: hosting a late-arriving traveler was the shared honor of the whole village. The neighbor gets up to uphold communal honor rather than out of mere annoyance—assuring disciples that God will uphold His own faithful character even more eagerly when His children call. *(Poet and Peasant, 1976; Jesus Through Middle Eastern Eyes, 2008)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: interpreting anaideia through Middle Eastern village hospitality and honor is a hallmark contribution of Kenneth Bailey -->

**The Mishnah**, the Jewish oral law code compiled around AD 200, devotes extensive rulings to pre-meal handwashing and purifying the outside versus inside of cups and bowls. That legal backdrop explains why the Pharisee host blinked when Jesus skipped handwashing (11:38)—and sharpens Jesus' challenge that inner cleansing through mercy and generosity comes first (11:39–41). *(Mishnah, Yadayim 1; Kelim)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: Mishnah Yadayim 1 governs handwashing before meals and Kelim addresses ritual purity of vessels -->
