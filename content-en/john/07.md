# John 7 — The Feast of Tabernacles and Living Water

## Story

Afterward Jesus moved about in Galilee, choosing not to travel openly in Judea because the Jewish leaders there were trying to kill Him. As the Feast of Tabernacles drew near, His brothers urged Him: leave here and go to Judea so your disciples may see the works you do; nobody who wants to be known acts in secret; since you do these things, show yourself to the world. For even His own brothers did not believe in Him (7:1–5).

Jesus answered, "My time has not yet come, though for you any time is right." The world cannot hate you, yet it hates Me because I testify that its works are evil. Go on up to the festival yourselves; I am not yet going up to this festival, because My time has not yet fully come. Saying this, He stayed behind in Galilee. Yet once His brothers had gone up for the feast, He also went up—not publicly, but privately (7:6–10).

During the festival the Jewish leaders looked for Him, asking, "Where is that man?" Across the crowds there was widespread muttering about Him. Some said, "He is a good man." Others countered, "No, he deceives the people." Yet no one spoke openly about Him for fear of the Jewish authorities (7:11–13).

Halfway through the festival, Jesus went up to the temple courts and began teaching. The Jewish listeners marveled: how does this man know the Scriptures so deeply when he has never been formally trained? Jesus replied: My teaching is not My own, but comes from the One who sent Me. Anyone willing to do God's will shall know whether My teaching comes from God or whether I speak on My own authority. Did not Moses give you the law? Yet none of you keeps the law. Why are you trying to kill Me? The crowd snapped back, "You have a demon! Who is trying to kill you?" (7:14–20).

Jesus replied: I did one work, and you are all astonished. Moses gave you circumcision—though it actually came from the patriarchs—and you circumcise a boy on the Sabbath so the law of Moses will not be broken. Why then are you furious with Me for making a man's entire body well on the Sabbath? "Stop judging by mere appearances, and instead judge rightly." (7:21–24). He refers back to the paralyzed man healed at Bethesda in chapter 5.

Some residents of Jerusalem remarked: isn't this the man they are trying to kill? Look, he is speaking publicly and they say nothing to him; could the authorities actually have concluded he is the Christ? Yet we know where this man comes from, whereas when the Christ appears, nobody will know where He comes from. Jesus cried out loudly in the temple courts: you know Me, and you know where I come from! Still, I have not come on My own; the One who sent Me is true, and you do not know Him, whereas I know Him because I am from Him and He sent Me. They tried to seize Him, yet no one laid a hand on Him because His hour had not yet come. Even so, many in the crowd put faith in Him, asking: when the Christ arrives, could He perform more signs than this man has done? (7:25–31).

Hearing the crowd whispering such things about Him, the Pharisees and chief priests dispatched temple guards to arrest Him. Jesus told them: I am with you only a little longer, and then I am returning to the One who sent Me. You will look for Me and not find Me, and where I am you cannot come. The Jewish leaders puzzled among themselves: where does he plan to go that we cannot find him? Will he go to our people scattered among the Greeks and teach the Greeks? (7:32–36).

On the last and greatest day of the festival, Jesus stood up and cried aloud: if anyone thirsts, let him come to Me and drink! Whoever believes in Me, just as Scripture has said, "streams of living water will flow from within him." By this He meant the Spirit, whom those trusting in Him were later to receive; up to that point the Spirit had not yet been given, because Jesus had not yet been glorified (7:37–39). Throughout Tabernacles, priests drew water from the Pool of Siloam and poured it beside the altar as a prayer for rain. Right inside that water ceremony, Jesus invites thirsty souls to Himself.

Hearing His words, some in the crowd said, "Surely this man is the Prophet." Others insisted, "This is the Christ." Still others objected: how can the Christ come from Galilee? Does not Scripture say the Christ comes from David's offspring and from Bethlehem, the village where David lived? So the crowd split over Him. Some wanted to arrest Him, yet nobody laid hands on Him (7:40–44). They did not realize He had in fact been born in Bethlehem.

The temple guards returned empty-handed to the chief priests and Pharisees, who demanded, "Why didn't you bring him in?" The officers replied, "No one has ever spoken the way this man speaks!" The Pharisees retorted: have you been deceived too? Have any of the rulers or Pharisees believed in him? As for this mob that knows nothing of the law, they are accursed! Nicodemus—who had visited Jesus earlier and belonged to their own council—spoke up: does our law condemn someone without first hearing him and finding out what he has done? They shot back: are you from Galilee too? Look into it, and you will see that no prophet rises from Galilee. Then everyone went home (7:45–53). The man who crept in by night in chapter 3 now speaks up, even if only a single sentence. Verse 7:53 also opens the episode of the woman caught in adultery, told in the next chapter.

## Key Takeaways

**Jesus refuses fame and family pressure, moving strictly by His Father's "hour."** Even His own brothers do not yet believe, prodding Him to display wonders in Jerusalem. Rejecting the pursuit of spotlight, Jesus goes up quietly, and no enemy can touch Him before His appointed hour arrives (7:1–10; 7:30).

**Willingness to obey comes before recognizing the truth.** When listeners puzzle over how an uncredentialed teacher knows Scripture so deeply, Jesus roots His teaching in the Father who sent Him. Discernment depends not on academic credentials, but on a heart ready to do God's will (7:14–18).

**Inside a festival praying for rain, Jesus announces Himself as the spring of living water.** On the climactic day of Tabernacles, He stands and invites every thirsty person to come and drink, promising streams of living water—the Holy Spirit—once He enters His glory (7:37–39).

**Jesus exposes deep divisions in human hearts.** Listening to the exact same message, guards sent to arrest Him return marveling that "no one ever spoke like this man," Nicodemus ventures a plea for fair hearing, and religious elites despise the crowd while dismissing Jesus out of regional prejudice (7:40–52).

## Deeper Meaning

**Tabernacles recalls the wilderness journey and water from the rock.** Mosaic law commanded Israel to dwell in booths for seven days to remember God leading them out of Egypt and sustaining them across forty years, including drawing water from the desert rock (Leviticus 23:42–43; Exodus 17:6). Stepping into the center of that feast, Jesus shows Himself as the fulfillment of every ancient symbol.

**Prophetic promises of living streams and the Spirit.** Verse 7:38 gathers not a single isolated verse, but a whole stream of prophetic promises: God pledging to "pour water on the thirsty land" and "pour out My Spirit on your offspring" (Isaiah 44:3), along with visions of living waters streaming from beneath the Jerusalem temple threshold (Ezekiel 47:1; Zechariah 14:8).

**The sharp irony over Messiah's birthplace in Bethlehem.** The crowd doubts Jesus because Scripture foretells the Messiah rising from David's line in "Bethlehem Ephrathah" (Micah 5:2), while they assume He hails solely from Galilee (7:41–42). Readers feel the sting of irony: they wield a prophecy already fulfilled in Jesus to reject Him because they never bother to check the facts.

**Mosaic fairness voiced by Nicodemus.** Asking the council whether the law condemns anyone before hearing him out (7:51), Nicodemus appeals to the bedrock requirement of impartial justice laid on Israel's judges (Deuteronomy 1:16–17). The man who came under darkness in chapter 3 begins stepping toward the light.

## Notes & Nuances

**Saying "I am not yet going up" in 7:8 and then going privately in 7:10 was not deception.** Some ancient manuscripts read "not going up," while others read "not yet going up." Either way, the context makes clear that Jesus refuses to join the public festival caravan to stage a publicity display as His brothers urged; afterward He goes quietly on His own terms in step with His Father's timing.

**Scholars discuss two ways of punctuating 7:37–38.** In the traditional punctuation ("Whoever believes in Me, rivers of living water will flow from within him"), the Spirit pours forth through the believer to refresh others. In the alternative punctuation favored by many modern translations, Jesus calls out, "Let anyone who is thirsty come to Me, and let the one who believes in Me drink," and the next line declares that rivers of living water flow from within Christ Himself.

**"The Spirit had not yet been given" in 7:39 points ahead to Pentecost.** That note does not deny the Spirit's active presence throughout the Old Testament. John means the new-covenant outpouring—wide, abiding, and indwelling every believer—begins only after Jesus dies, rises, and returns to the Father.

**Verse 7:53 opens the story of the woman caught in adultery.** The earliest Greek manuscripts of John lack the entire section 7:53 and 8:1–11 at this spot, though later chapter divisions left its opening line attached to chapter 7. Its textual history receives closer attention in the Notes & Nuances section of chapter 8.

## What Commentators Say

**The Mishnah**, Jewish oral law compiled around AD 200, describes in vivid detail the daily water-drawing ritual across the seven days of Tabernacles: each morning a priest carried a golden pitcher down to the Pool of Siloam, marched back to the temple amid trumpets and joyous singing, and poured the water beside the altar in prayer for rain and thanksgiving. **John Calvin**, sixteenth-century Reformer, exposed the biting irony of 7:52 when Pharisee leaders snap at Nicodemus that "no prophet arises from Galilee." Priding themselves on mastery of Scripture, they forgot immediately that Jonah hailed from Galilee—proving how anger and pride can blind even learned scholars. *(Commentary on the Gospel according to John, on chapter 7)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: clearly recalled Calvin pointing out the Pharisees' blunder in forgetting Jonah at 7:52; exact wording in original not checked -->

**Raymond Brown**, twentieth-century American Catholic New Testament scholar, favored the punctuation that identifies Christ Himself as the source of the "streams of living water" in 7:38. Linking the image to the new temple in Ezekiel 47, he saw the promise visibly fulfilled in chapter 19 when a soldier pierces Jesus' side on the cross and blood and water immediately pour out. *(The Gospel According to John, Anchor Bible, vol. 1, 1966)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: reading Christ as the fountain of living water linked to 19:34 is a hallmark of Brown's commentary -->
