# John 14 — The Way to the Father

## Story

Still at that final meal, Jesus speaks to His anxious disciples: "Let not your hearts be troubled; believe in God, believe also in Me." In My Father's house are many dwelling places; if it were not so, I would have told you. I am going to prepare a place for you. And when I have gone and prepared a place for you, I will come back and welcome you to Myself, so that where I am you may be too. You know where I am going, and you know the way (14:1–4). They have just heard that He will be betrayed and that Peter will deny Him; He begins with comfort.

Thomas asked, "Lord, we do not know where You are going; how can we know the way?" Jesus answered, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life; no one comes to the Father except through Me." If you had known Me, you would have known My Father also; from now on you do know Him and have seen Him (14:5–7). Thomas asks for directions; Jesus answers by giving Himself.

Philip said, "Lord, show us the Father, and that is enough for us." Jesus replied: Philip, have I been with you so long and still you do not know Me? "Whoever has seen Me has seen the Father." How can you say, "Show us the Father"? Do you not believe that I am in the Father and the Father is in Me? The words I speak to you I do not speak on My own authority; rather, the Father abiding in Me performs His works. Believe Me when I say that I am in the Father and the Father is in Me; or else believe on account of the works themselves (14:8–11).

Truly, whoever believes in Me will also do the works that I do—and even greater works than these, because I am going to the Father. Whatever you ask in My name, I will do it, so that the Father may be glorified in the Son. If you ask Me anything in My name, I will do it (14:12–14).

"If you love Me, you will keep My commandments." And I will ask the Father, and He will give you another Helper to be with you forever—the Spirit of truth. The world cannot receive Him because it neither sees Him nor knows Him; you know Him, because He dwells with you and will be in you. "I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you." In a little while the world will see Me no longer, yet you will see Me; because I live, you also will live. On that day you will realize that I am in My Father, you are in Me, and I am in you. Whoever holds My commandments and keeps them is the one who loves Me; My Father will love that person, and I too will love him and disclose Myself to him (14:15–21). The Master is leaving, yet His followers will not be left stranded like orphans.

Judas—not Iscariot—asked, "Lord, how is it that You will disclose Yourself to us and not to the world?" Jesus answered: if anyone loves Me, he will keep My word; My Father will love him, and We will come to him and make Our home with him. Whoever does not love Me does not keep My words; and the word you hear is not Mine, but comes from the Father who sent Me. I have told you these things while still staying with you. Yet the Helper, the Holy Spirit whom the Father will send in My name, will teach you everything and bring to your remembrance all that I have said to you (14:22–26). He reveals Himself not through a public spectacle for the world, but by coming to live within the heart that loves Him.

"Peace I leave with you; My peace I give to you; not as the world gives do I give to you." Let your hearts neither be troubled nor afraid. You heard Me tell you, "I am going away, and I will come back to you." If you loved Me, you would rejoice that I am going to the Father, because the Father is greater than I. I have told you now before it happens, so that when it does happen you may believe. I will no longer talk much with you, for the ruler of this world is coming; he has no claim on Me. Yet so that the world may know I love the Father and act exactly as the Father commanded Me, "Rise, let us go from here." (14:27–31). He bestows His peace on the very night He is about to be arrested.

## Key Takeaways

**Comfort begins with the promise of a home.** The disciples have just been stunned by warnings of departure, betrayal, and denial. Jesus does not hand them a lecture on dying; He speaks of the Father's house with plenty of rooms and promises to return for them, "so that where I am you may be also" (14:1–3). Heaven's deepest treasure is not architecture, but being with Him.

**Jesus does not merely point out a route; He is the Way.** Thomas wants a roadmap. Jesus replies, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life" (14:6). Reaching God does not happen by mastering a map, but by walking with a Person. And to know what God is like, look straight at Jesus: whoever has seen Him has seen the Father (14:7–11).

**The Master departs, yet leaves no orphans behind.** Jesus promises to ask the Father for another Helper, the Spirit of truth, who will stay forever (14:16–17), teaching them and calling to mind everything He spoke (14:26). Better still, both Father and Son come to dwell within whoever loves Him (14:23). His physical absence opens the door to an even closer presence.

**Love for Christ shows itself in keeping His word.** Three times across this chapter Jesus ties love directly to obedience (14:15; 14:21; 14:23). Here love is far more than passing sentiment—it is trust strong enough to live out what He teaches.

**A peace unlike anything the world hands out.** The world offers peace only when troubles pause. Jesus gives peace on the very evening of His arrest, with "the ruler of this world" closing in (14:27; 14:30). His peace springs not from calm circumstances, but from knowing He is doing His Father's will (14:31).

## Deeper Meaning

**Philip asks for what Moses once asked.** Moses pleaded, "Please show me Your glory," and God replied, "No one may see My face and live" (Exodus 33:18; Exodus 33:20). Philip now asks to see the Father. Jesus' reply—"Whoever has seen Me has seen the Father" (14:9)—unpacks what John stated at the Gospel's start: no one has ever seen God, yet the only Son "has made Him known" (John 1:18). Paul names Him "the image of the invisible God" (Colossians 1:15).

**The Father's house and dwelling place.** The earlier time Jesus spoke of "My Father's house," He meant the temple (John 2:16), the place God dwelt among His people as promised long ago: "I will make My dwelling among you" (Leviticus 26:11). The exact Greek noun translated "dwelling places" in verse 2 reappears in verse 23 when Jesus says Father and Son will make their "home" with the believer (14:2; 14:23). God's home is not merely waiting in heaven later; He makes His home inside those who love Him today.

**The Spirit placed within the heart.** Ezekiel heard God pledge, "I will put My Spirit within you," empowering His people to walk in His statutes (Ezekiel 36:27). Jesus announces that very fulfillment: the Spirit of truth "will be in you," and those who love Him keep His word (14:17; 14:23). The Spirit accomplishes inwardly what stone tablets could never achieve from outside.

**"Another" Helper implies a first one.** The Greek word rendered Helper or Comforter (*Parakletos*) describes someone summoned alongside to assist—like an advocate in court. John applies the exact same term to Jesus Himself: "We have an Advocate with the Father" (1 John 2:1). For three years Jesus stood beside the disciples; the Holy Spirit arrives to carry on that ministry permanently.

**Followers of "the Way."** In Acts, the earliest believers are called followers of "the Way"—the exact Greek word used here (Acts 9:2). Hebrews explains that Jesus opened "a new and living way" into God's presence right through His own body (Hebrews 10:20). The road to the Father runs straight through the cross.

**A greeting of peace transformed into a gift.** Jews greeted and parted from one another with wishes of peace, echoing the priestly benediction: "The LORD lift up His countenance upon you and give you peace" (Numbers 6:26). Jesus does more than wish peace; He imparts His own peace (14:27)—a peace Paul says "surpasses all understanding" (Philippians 4:7).

## Notes & Nuances

**Does "no one comes to the Father except through Me" exclude everyone else?** Few lines in John stir more discussion. The historic conviction of most Christians is that salvation exists nowhere outside Jesus, echoing Peter's declaration that "there is no other name" (Acts 4:12). Some theologians hold that anyone God saves is saved through Christ's work even if they never heard His story clearly, leaving judgment to God's mercy. Others read the verse as an insider confession of faith rather than a verdict on other faiths. Whatever one's theological nuance, remember the setting: Jesus spoke these words to comfort frightened disciples, never as a club to beat outsiders.

**"Many rooms" does not mean separate estates.** Older English translations rendered this "mansions," which once meant simply dwelling places, though modern ears hear luxury palaces. The imagery is of many rooms inside one shared Father's house—a large family living together. Nor does it rank heavenly statuses; it assures them there is plenty of room for all whom He brings home.

**Does "the Father is greater than I" make the Son inferior?** In antiquity, Arians cited verse 28 to argue the Son is less than truly God. Early Church Fathers answered in two ways: some linked the phrase to Jesus' humbled human state on earth; others explained that because the Father is the source from whom the Son eternally proceeds, the Father is greater in relational order rather than in divine essence. Both readings keep verse 28 anchored beside "I and the Father are one" (John 10:30) and "Whoever has seen Me has seen the Father" (14:9).

**What are "greater works" and praying "in My name"?** "Greater works" refers less to flashier miracles than to broader reach: after Pentecost, roughly three thousand people believe in a single day (Acts 2:41), surpassing anything seen during Jesus' earthly ministry. And praying "in My name" is never a magic closing tag appended to prayers; it means asking in harmony with who Jesus is and what He wills, "so that the Father may be glorified in the Son" (14:13).

**Why does Jesus say "Rise, let us go from here" and then keep speaking for three more chapters?** After verse 31, Jesus continues teaching through chapters 15 and 16 and prays across chapter 17 before finally departing in chapter 18 (18:1). Some suggest He spoke while walking toward the Kidron Valley; others imagine Him rising yet pausing before leaving the room. Many scholars think chapters 15–17 represent additional farewell teachings from the same tradition woven into the discourse during the Gospel's composition.

## What Commentators Say

**Augustine**, bishop of Hippo in North Africa around AD 400, unpacked verse 6 with a beloved contrast he repeated often: Christ as God is the homeland toward which we travel; Christ as man is the road along which we walk. **John Chrysostom**, fourth-century archbishop of Constantinople, asked what the "greater works" of verse 12 meant and pointed straight to Acts: Peter's passing shadow healed the sick (Acts 5:15), something Jesus Himself did not do during His earthly life. Rather than making the apostles superior to their Master, Chrysostom saw this as proof that Christ's active power grows even mightier after His return to the Father. *(Homilies on the Gospel of John)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: recalled Chrysostom citing Peter's shadow to illustrate verse 12; exact homily number not recalled -->

**Martin Luther**, sixteenth-century German Reformer, preached an extended series on John 14–16. Addressing Philip's request, he warned that anyone trying to climb straight up to probe God's bare majesty while bypassing Christ will inevitably fall. We must begin down low, with the man Jesus lying in a manger and dying on a cross; right there we see the Father's heart. *(Sermons on the Gospel of St. John, chapters 14–16)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: this is a classic Luther theme appearing across his John 14 sermons; exact phrasing here not verified -->

**Raymond Brown**, twentieth-century American Catholic New Testament scholar, spotlighted the abrupt transition at verse 31 where Jesus announces departure yet speaks for three more chapters. He proposed that chapter 14 once stood as a complete farewell discourse, to which chapters 15–17—parallel teachings from the same community—were later joined, explaining why several themes recur across chapters 14 and 16. *(The Gospel According to John, Anchor Bible, vol. 2, 1970)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: Brown's theory of layered farewell discourses pivoting on 14:31 is well known -->

**Lesslie Newbigin**, British missionary bishop who served decades in India, reflected on verse 6 for readers living amid religious pluralism. He stressed that the verse never claims Christianity as an institution is "the way."
