# Isaiah 45 — King Cyrus and the One and Only Savior

## Story

Chapter 44 ended on an astonishing personal name: the LORD calling Cyrus "My shepherd" who will rebuild Jerusalem and lay the temple's foundation (44:28). Entering chapter 45, the LORD speaks directly to this Persian monarch, calling him "His anointed." God takes him by his right hand to subdue nations, strip kings of their armor, and swing open double gates that will never stay bolted shut. He marches ahead leveling rugged terrain, shattering bronze doors, snapping iron bars, and handing over hidden treasures stored in dark vaults—so Cyrus knows it is the LORD calling him by name (45:1–3).

God spells out why He summons Cyrus "though you do not know Me": first, "for the sake of Jacob My servant and Israel My chosen one," and broader still, so from the rising of the sun to its setting everyone knows "there is no other; apart from Me there is no God." He closes this address with a sweeping claim of absolute sovereignty: "I form the light and create the darkness; I bring prosperity and create calamity" (45:4–7).

Suddenly the oracle bursts into song calling heaven and earth to join in: skies above, shower down righteousness; clouds, rain it down; earth below, open wide so salvation and righteousness sprout up together—all created by the LORD Himself (45:8).

Immediately God's tone sharpens toward grumblers inside His own people resenting His plan to use a pagan foreign monarch. "Woe to him who quarrels with his Maker—one clay pot among many!" Does wet clay have any business quizzing the potter shaping it, "What are you making?" or sneering that its maker has no skill? Does a child have any right cross-examining father and mother for giving birth to them? (45:9–10).

The LORD, Holy One of Israel, challenges critics presuming to instruct Him about His children and the work of His hands. His own hands shaped the earth, formed humanity, stretched out the skies, and marshaled every starry host. He Himself raises Cyrus up in righteousness and straightens all his roads so he rebuilds God's city and releases His exiles "without price or reward" (45:11–13).

The oracle turns tenderly to humiliated Jerusalem: Egypt's wealth, Cush's merchandise, and towering Sabeans will march over in chains, bowing down before God's people to confess: "Surely God is with you, and there is no other; there is no other God." Stunned by such an unexpected rescue, an exclamation breaks in—perhaps the prophet's or Israel's awed voice: "Truly You are a God who hides Himself, O God of Israel, the Savior!" Then God sets two destinies side by side: all idol-makers trudge off together in disgrace, whereas Israel receives "everlasting salvation," never again to bear shame or reproach (45:14–17).

The LORD reaffirms Himself as clear, trustworthy Creator. He formed heaven and established earth firmly—"He did not create it to be empty, but formed it to be inhabited." Never has He muttered secretly in gloomy corners like pagan oracles, nor ever told Jacob's offspring to seek Him in vain; He speaks truth and declares what is right (45:18–19).

At the chapter's close, a courtroom opens for "the fugitives from the nations" surviving the collapse of empires. God calls them near to reason together: folks hauling carved wooden idols and praying to gods powerless to save know nothing at all. The LORD alone foretold history from long ago, and apart from Him stands no righteous God and Savior (45:20–21). Out of that verdict rings Scripture's widest invitation: "Turn to Me and be saved, all the ends of the earth; for I am God, and there is no other!" By Himself the LORD swears an unbreakable oath: "Before Me every knee will bow; by Me every tongue will swear." People will come acknowledging that righteousness and strength dwell solely in the LORD; raging opponents will come to shame, while all Israel's offspring stand justified and glory in Him (45:22–25).

## Key Takeaways

**God rules all of history—even through people who do not know Him.** King Cyrus never worshiped the LORD, yet God called him by name and pushed open imperial gates before him (45:1–5). God's sovereignty never stops at the sanctuary door; world-shaking political turns move right inside His hands.

**There is only one true God.** Across this single chapter tolls the refrain "apart from Me there is no God" (45:5; 45:6; 45:14; 45:18; 45:21; 45:22). The cosmos is not a tug-of-war between rival twin deities of light and dark; one sole Creator made heaven and earth and governs every turning event.

**God refuses to be boxed into human scripts.** Many exiles expected rescue via a dashing Davidic warrior-king, certainly not a Persian outsider. Clay arguing with its potter (45:9) reminds us that real trust lets God act freely in His higher wisdom.

**God is simultaneously surprising and transparent.** Verse 45:15 calls Him "a God who hides Himself" because His methods outstrip human prediction, yet 45:19 insists He has "not spoken in secret." His promises stay crystal clear; only His unexpected paths of fulfillment leave us speechless.

**Salvation swings open wide to every nation on earth.** Insisting there is only one God never narrows compassion—it widens it! Precisely because one Creator made everyone, He stands as the lone Savior inviting earth's farthest corners to turn to Him and live (45:22).

## Deeper Meaning

**Cyrus's historical decree fulfilling prophecy.** Once Persia took Babylon in 539 BC, Scripture records the LORD stirring King Cyrus's spirit to issue his proclamation sending Judeans home to rebuild Jerusalem's temple and funding the journey (Ezra 1:1–4; 2 Chronicles 36:22–23). Freeing exiles "without price or reward" (45:13) fits his real repatriation policy cleanly.

**The potter metaphor entering Paul's letter to Rome.** Explaining God's sovereign freedom in saving both Israel and Gentiles, the apostle Paul borrows clay questioning its potter from 45:9 alongside Jeremiah's potter's shop (Jeremiah 18:1–6): "Will what is formed say to him who formed it, 'Why did you make me like this?'" (Romans 9:20–21).

**Every knee bowing at the name of Jesus.** In 45:23, the LORD Himself swears every knee will bow before Him. Paul quotes it regarding God's judgment seat (Romans 14:11)—and even more astonishingly, in Philippians applies that exclusive Yahweh oath directly to Jesus Christ: at Jesus' name every knee bows and every tongue confesses Him as Lord (Philippians 2:9–11). The earliest Christians thereby confessed Jesus belongs inside the sole divine identity of Israel's God.

**Earth never created to stay empty.** Saying God did not create earth "to be empty" (45:18) repeats the exact Hebrew term (*tohu*) describing the "formless and void" world before creation (Genesis 1:2). Invoking creation's purpose right amid Judah's ruined cities guarantees exile will never hold the final word.

**The God who never speaks in secret.** Saying "I have not spoken in secret" (45:19) echoes powerfully as Jesus answers the high priest before His crucifixion: He taught openly in synagogues and the temple, saying nothing in secret (John 18:20).

## Notes & Nuances

**Why call a foreign monarch "His anointed"?** The Hebrew term for "anointed" is *Mashiach* (Messiah, Greek *Christos*), normally reserved for Davidic kings or Israel's priests. Cyrus is the sole Gentile in the entire Old Testament handed this title (45:1). It never suggests he is the ultimate Redeemer, only that God set him apart for one distinct assignment: liberating Israel's exiles.

**"Create calamity" in 45:7 never implies God authors moral evil.** In Hebrew, the word pairs directly opposite "prosperity/peace" (*shalom*), denoting historical disaster, warfare, or just judgment—exactly as used elsewhere in the prophets (Amos 3:6; Lamentations 3:38)—never moral wickedness. The line confronts Persian dualism (two rival deities of light and darkness), declaring one sole God rules over all events.

**The debate over naming Cyrus ahead of time.** Isaiah lived in the eighth century BC, more than 150 years before Cyrus. Traditional readers see direct supernatural prediction matching chapters 41–48's claim that God foretells tomorrow. Most modern scholars view chapters 40–55 as written by a late-exilic prophet right as Cyrus emerged, then united into Isaiah's book. Either way, the core proclamation stands untouched: the LORD rules empires and calls kings by name.

**Foreigners coming "in chains" (45:14) can easily be misread.** Pulled out of context, Egyptians, Cushites, and Sabeans marching over in shackles before Israel sounds like harsh subjugation. Yet the very same verse shows them coming to confess the sole true God, leading straight to 45:22's global invitation to every corner of earth to be saved. It uses dramatic ancient poetic idiom picturing total surrender to God's supreme majesty.

## What Commentators Say

**Josephus**, the first-century Jewish historian, recounts that when Cyrus read Isaiah's prophecy inscribed with his own name over two centuries earlier, he was amazed and eager to fulfill it, issuing his decree sending Jews home to rebuild the temple. Even if modern historians view this more as revered tradition than verifiable archive, it shows how deeply ancient Judaism prized Isaiah's Cyrus oracle. *(Antiquities of the Jews, Book 11, Chapter 1)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: Josephus's story of Cyrus reading Isaiah appears prominently in Antiquities Book 11, Chapter 1 and is widely cited -->

**The Aleinu prayer**, the daily liturgy concluding every Jewish worship service, cites the oath of Isaiah 45:23 directly, praying for the day every knee bows and every tongue swears allegiance to the one true God. Its liturgical role proves Jewish tradition reads chapter 45's climax as a universal hope embracing all humanity. *(Siddur, Aleinu prayer)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: the Aleinu prayer's quotation of Isaiah 45:23 is a foundational element of Jewish liturgy -->

**John Calvin**, the sixteenth-century Genevan Reformer, examines 45:7 closely to refute dualistic beliefs in rival equal gods of good and evil. "Calamity" denotes war, famine, and righteous affliction rather than moral sin, he clarifies; then on 45:9 he exposes human arrogance presuming to judge God's dealings by mortal yardsticks. *(Commentary on the Book of the Prophet Isaiah, on chapter 45, verses 7 and 9)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: recall Calvin interpreting 45:7 as historical calamity refuting dualism and his critique of pride in 45:9; not verified against ccel.org -->

**Charles Spurgeon**, the famed nineteenth-century English preacher, traced his very conversion to 45:22. In January 1850, a fifteen-year-old sheltered from a blizzard inside a tiny Colchester chapel heard a lay preacher repeat "Look unto Me, and be ye saved!"—reminding listeners that simply *looking* requires zero strength or scholarship. Instantly Spurgeon's crushing spiritual burden vanished. *(Autobiography, Volume 1; sermons on Isaiah 45, verse 22)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: Spurgeon's conversion through Isaiah 45:22 in Colchester is one of the most famous conversion accounts in Christian history -->

**Richard Bauckham**, British New Testament scholar, shows that Isaiah 40–55 defines the one true God by two sole prerogatives: creating all things and ruling history (45:12; 45:18). Because 45:23 is the Old Testament's peak monotheistic oath, Paul applying it directly to Jesus in Philippians 2:9–11 demonstrates that the earliest Church unmistakably included Jesus inside the unique divine identity of Israel's God. *(Jesus and the God of Israel, 2008)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: analyzing Isaiah 40–55's monotheism and Philippians 2's appropriation of Isaiah 45:23 is the central thesis of Bauckham's book -->
