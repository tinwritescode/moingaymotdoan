# Numbers 33 — Looking Back Over Forty Years and the Command to Enter Canaan

## Story

At the LORD's command, Moses records the complete travel log of Israel's forty-year trek from the day they marched out of Egypt until they stood beside the Jordan River. Forty-two camping stages unfold in chronological order across three distinct chapters of the journey (33:1–2).

Stage one recounts the rescue from Egypt to Mount Sinai. Israel marches out of Rameses on the fifteenth day of the first month—the morning after Passover—walking boldly out in full view of Egyptians burying their firstborn after God executed judgment on Egypt's gods. They cross the Red Sea, pass the bitter spring at Marah, camp beside the oasis of Elim with its twelve springs and seventy palm trees, endure thirst at Rephidim, and arrive in the Wilderness of Sinai (33:3–15).

Stage two stretches across nearly forty years roaming vast desert plains—from Sinai through almost twenty obscure stations mentioned nowhere else, back to Kadesh in the Wilderness of Zin, and on to Mount Hor on Edom's border. Here the brisk list pauses at a solemn milestone: Aaron the priest climbs Mount Hor at God's command and dies on the first day of the fifth month in the fortieth year after leaving Egypt, at 123 years old (33:16–39).

Stage three traces the final swing from Mount Hor around Edom and Moab, briefly noting the Canaanite king of Arad in the Negev, then crossing the Abarim mountains to spread camp along the plains of Moab beside the Jordan opposite Jericho (33:40–49).

Closing the old road, the LORD issues a clear mandate for the new chapter ahead once Israel crosses the Jordan into Canaan. They must drive out all the land's inhabitants, smash every carved stone, cast image, and hilltop shrine, and divide the land fairly by lot according to each tribe's population. God adds a stern warning: if they compromise and let those peoples remain, the survivors will become "barbs in your eyes and thorns in your sides," tormenting them constantly—until God eventually does to Israel what he planned to do to those nations (33:50–56).

## Key Takeaways

**Looking back over the journey reveals God's steady faithfulness.** The LORD himself ordered Moses to write down this travel diary (33:2). Across forty years and forty-two campfires—including long stretches in obscure, unnamed desert valleys—God remembered and guided every single footprint until he brought his people home.

**God's grace towers above human shame.** Read the itinerary carefully: it spotlights Passover deliverance, the palms of Elim, and Aaron's dignified passing, while quietly declining to rehash the golden calf or the Kadesh revolt. God does not erase history, yet neither does he weaponize old failures to crush a new generation stepping into the future.

**Entering the Promised Land requires a clean break from idolatry.** Commanding Israel to demolish carved stones, metal idols, and hilltop shrines (33:52) shows that Israel's deadliest danger inside Canaan was never enemy chariots, but the slow pull of local pagan worship.

**Halfway compromise turns into lifelong pain.** The vivid picture of "barbs in your eyes and thorns in your sides" (33:55) captures tolerated sin perfectly: manageable or convenient at first glance, yet turning into a throbbing wound that wears life down from inside.

## Deeper Meaning

**The life of faith is a pilgrim journey.** Israel's forty-two camping stops became one of Scripture's signature images of walking with God: his people never settle permanently at a temporary desert stop, always pressing toward the Promised Land. Deuteronomy calls Israel to remember the whole forty-year path God led them so they learn humility and live by every word from his mouth (Deuteronomy 8:2–3). Hebrews widens that horizon to every believer: we are strangers and exiles on earth, journeying home toward a better heavenly country (Hebrews 11:13–16).

**Verse 55's warning comes tragically true in Judges.** Once in Canaan, Israel failed to drive out the inhabitants, settled into coexistence, and drifted straight into worshiping their gods. The angel of the LORD repeated almost word for word the warning of Numbers 33:55: those peoples became thorns in their sides and snares plunging Israel into the downward spiral of Judges (Judges 2:1–3; Joshua 23:12–13).

**Wholehearted purity of heart in the New Testament.** Just as ancient Israel was charged to tear down physical idols to hold the land, New Testament believers are urged to put away every hidden idol of the heart—greed, sensual indulgence, worldly compromise—keeping their lives a clean temple wholly devoted to God (Colossians 3:5; 1 John 5:21).

## Notes & Nuances

**Why can so many campsites in chapter 33 no longer be located today?** Roughly half of the forty-two place names appear nowhere else in Scripture and remain uncertain archaeologically. That makes complete historical sense: nomadic travelers pitched cloth tents near minor desert springs or wadis without erecting stone fortresses, so across more than three millennia local names and faint camp traces vanished into the sand.

**Does the number forty-two hold symbolic shape?** Many scholars notice there are exactly forty-two stops from Rameses to Moab (six times seven). Moses likely selected key representative stops across forty years to craft an orderly, memorable summary—much as Matthew 1 arranges Jesus' genealogy into three neat sets of fourteen generations (forty-two in total).

**Understanding the command to drive out Canaan's peoples (33:52–56).** As with chapter 31, verse 52 raises serious questions about conquering Canaan. Notably, the core verb in verses 52 and 55 is *drive out* rather than indiscriminate slaughter, paired immediately with the reason: dismantling idolatrous cults bound up with child sacrifice and temple prostitution (Deuteronomy 18:9–12). Furthermore, verse 56 reveals God's complete impartiality: if Israel ever commits those exact sins, they will suffer the exact same expulsion from the land—precisely what unfolded centuries later in the Babylonian exile.
