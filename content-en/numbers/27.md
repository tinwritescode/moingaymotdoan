# Numbers 27 — Zelophehad's Daughters and Joshua's Commissioning

## Story

Immediately after the census preparing to divide the land, five sisters—Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah, daughters of Zelophehad from the tribe of Manasseh—step forward at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting. Standing before Moses, Eleazar the priest, the tribal leaders, and the entire congregation, they state their case clearly: their father died in the wilderness, not as a rebel in Korah's company, but for his own sin like the rest of the old generation, leaving no sons. They ask a bold, pointed question: why should their father's name vanish from his clan simply because he had no son? They request a rightful share of property among their father's brothers (27:1–4).

Moses neither brushes them aside nor rules off the cuff; he brings their case straight before the LORD. God's verdict is immediate: "The daughters of Zelophehad speak rightly." He instructs Moses to grant the five sisters their father's inheritance. Building on their plea, the LORD enacts a permanent inheritance statute for all Israel: when a man dies leaving no son, his estate passes to his daughter; if no daughter, to his brothers; if no brothers, to his father's brothers; and failing that, to his nearest kinsman in the clan (27:5–11).

Next, the LORD tells Moses to climb a peak in the Abarim range and look out across Canaan from afar. Once he has seen the land, he will be gathered to his people just like his brother Aaron. God recalls the reason: in the Wilderness of Zin, when the congregation quarreled over water, Moses and Aaron failed to obey fully and uphold God's holiness before the people's eyes (27:12–14).

Learning that his death is near and Canaan closed to him, Moses asks nothing for himself. Immediately he prays for the nation, addressing God as "the God of the spirits of all flesh," and begs him to appoint a leader over the community who will lead them out and bring them in so the LORD's congregation will not be "like sheep without a shepherd" (27:15–17).

The LORD answers by naming Joshua son of Nun—a man in whom the Spirit already dwells. God directs Moses to lay his hand on Joshua, present him before Eleazar the priest and the whole assembly, commission him, and invest him with part of Moses' own authority so all Israel will obey him. Unlike Moses who spoke directly with God, however, Joshua will stand before Eleazar the priest to inquire of the LORD's will through the *Urim* on the high priest's breastpiece, leading the nation out and back at God's word. Moses obeys completely, laying hands on Joshua and commissioning him before the whole congregation (27:18–23).

## Key Takeaways

**Real faith steps up to claim God's promise before seeing it.** When Zelophehad's five daughters petitioned for ancestral land, Israel still stood east of the Jordan—not an acre of Canaan had been conquered yet. They acted as though God's promise was already as good as done, and God himself commended their faith (27:4; 27:7).

**God's law listens attentively and champions the vulnerable.** Across patriarchal ancient cultures, women seldom held legal title to ancestral land. When five sisters spoke up courageously, Moses did not hide behind custom to turn them away; he carried their plea straight to God, and God reshaped precedent to safeguard their rightful claim (27:5–8).

**A true shepherd puts the flock ahead of personal disappointment.** At the very moment he received news of his approaching death, Moses' lone concern was not missing out on the land he had spent forty years trekking toward—it was guarding God's people from wandering like shepherdless sheep (27:16–17).

**Healthy succession happens openly and selflessly.** Moses neither clung to office until his dying breath nor maneuvered to pass power to his own sons. He laid hands publicly on God's chosen successor Joshua before the whole community, ensuring smooth unity after he was gone (27:18–23).

## Deeper Meaning

**Equal inheritance for men and women comes to full flower in Christ.** Zelophehad's five daughters pried open the doorway for women to inherit the Promised Land under the old covenant. In the New Testament, Paul heralds the complete breakthrough: in Christ Jesus there is neither Jew nor Greek, slave nor free, male nor female—all who belong to Christ are Abraham's seed and equal heirs according to the promise (Galatians 3:28–29).

**Joshua foreshadows the Good Shepherd.** Moses' plea that Israel not be left "like sheep without a shepherd" echoes straight into the Gospels: seeing weary, helpless crowds like sheep without a shepherd, Jesus feels deep compassion (Matthew 9:36; Mark 6:34) and declares himself the Good Shepherd who lays down his life for the sheep (John 10:11). Remarkably, Hebrew *Joshua* and Greek *Jesus* are the very same name, meaning *the LORD saves*. Moses—representing the Law—leads Israel only to the river's edge, while Joshua brings them across; even so, Jesus alone guides believers into the eternal rest that not even Joshua could permanently secure (Hebrews 4:8–9).

**Shared authority between civil leader and high priest.** Moses uniquely combined prophet, national leader, and direct mediator with God. With Joshua, authority divides cleanly: Joshua leads armies and governance while Eleazar seeks God's direction via the *Urim* (27:21). That balance checked unchecked power—until all three offices of Prophet, Priest, and King converged perfectly in Jesus Christ alone.

## Notes & Nuances

**The five sisters' story gets an important sequel.** Here God establishes their right to inherit; later, in chapter 36—the final chapter of Numbers—Manasseh's clan elders raise a practical complication: if the sisters marry outside their tribe, ancestral land could permanently transfer to another tribe. Chapters 27 and 36 thus bookend the closing third of Numbers, showing God's law both responsive to individual justice and attentive to long-term communal stability (36:1–12).

**Why did Moses impart only *some* of his authority to Joshua?** Verse 20 says "invest him with some of your authority," because Moses still had a brief season left to preach Deuteronomy, and no subsequent Old Testament prophet ever matched Moses' unique face-to-face intimacy with God (27:20; Deuteronomy 34:10). Even a gifted successor like Joshua held delegated authority and remained dependent on God's guidance through the priesthood.

**What was the *Urim*?** The *Urim* (paired with the *Thummim*, meaning *lights* and *perfections*) were two sacred objects kept inside the high priest's breastpiece (Exodus 28:30). Scripture never describes their exact shape or mechanism, though scholars widely view them as sacred lots yielding clear *yes* or *no* guidance from God in major national decisions.
