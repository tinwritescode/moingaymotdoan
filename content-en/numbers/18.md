# Numbers 18 — Duties and Provision for Priests and Levites

## Story

Answering Israel's terrified outcry at the end of chapter 17—fearing anyone nearing the Tabernacle must die—the LORD speaks directly to Aaron to draw clear lines of responsibility among the priests, the Levites, and the entire nation. First, God places a weighty charge squarely on Aaron, his sons, and the whole tribe of Levi: they will bear responsibility for any offenses against the sanctuary, while Aaron and his sons alone bear responsibility for offenses connected with the priesthood (18:1).

God directs Aaron to bring his brothers of the tribe of Levi near to assist him in caring for the Tabernacle. The Levites handle general duties of the Tent of Meeting, yet must never come near the sacred furnishings or the altar, lest both they and the priests die. Aaron's line alone exercises priestly office around the altar and inside the veil. Through this two-layered safeguard—Levites guarding the perimeter, priests ministering within—divine wrath will no longer break out upon Israel. God describes the priesthood itself as a gift he freely bestows upon Aaron (18:2–7).

Matched with heavy duty comes generous divine provision. The LORD tells Aaron that most offerings Israel brings that are not wholly burned—grain offerings, sin offerings, and guilt offerings—belong to Aaron and his sons to eat in a most holy place (18:8–10). Likewise, contributions, wave offerings, the choicest firstfruits of olive oil, new wine, and grain, and everything devoted wholly to the LORD belong to the priestly household so every ceremonially clean family member may enjoy them (18:11–14).

Every firstborn of human and beast likewise belongs to the LORD and is assigned to the priests. Human firstborn sons and firstborn of unclean animals are redeemed at one month old for five shekels of silver; firstborn cattle, sheep, and goats are never redeemed, but sacrificed—blood splashed on the altar, fat burned to the LORD, and meat given to the priests. God seals this pledge of provision as "an everlasting covenant of salt" (18:15–19).

Then the LORD speaks a profound word directly to Aaron: "You shall have no inheritance in their land, nor shall you have any portion among them; I am your portion and your inheritance among the Israelites" (18:20). The tribe of Levi likewise receives no farmland inheritance like the other tribes; instead, God grants them every tithe Israel brings, compensating them for serving the Tent of Meeting and standing in the gap so ordinary Israelites need not risk death approaching the sanctuary (18:21–24).

Finally, God relays through Moses one more statute specifically for the Levites: they themselves must never lose a grateful, giving heart. Out of the tithes they receive from Israel, the Levites must present a tenth—specifically "the very best" portion—to the LORD and deliver it to Aaron the priest. Once that choice tenth is offered, the remainder counts as their rightful wage, free for them and their families to eat anywhere without sin or profaning holy gifts (18:25–32).

## Key Takeaways

**Spiritual office is a weighty responsibility, never a trophy for ego.** Right after Korah grabbed for the priesthood imagining it a seat of prestige, God states Aaron's first duty bluntly: shoulder the liability of the sanctuary to guard the entire nation's life (18:1; 18:5). Those called to lead before God stand on the front line of accountability, never on a pedestal collecting perks.

**God generously sustains those who devote themselves wholly to his work.** Priests and Levites received no farmland to till like other tribes, yet God never left them destitute. Through sacred portions and the nation's tithes, he secured a reliable livelihood for them and their households so they could focus wholly on sanctuary service (18:8–24).

**God himself is the supreme inheritance of his servants.** Declaring "I am your portion and your inheritance" transforms what looked like deprivation—owning no acreage—into the highest imaginable treasure (18:20). Real estate can be lost or ruined, yet possessing the living God as one's heritage surpasses every earthly asset.

**Even those supported by offerings must cheerfully give God their best.** Though Levites lived off Israel's tithes, God required them to tithe from their own income—selecting "the very best" part—to honor him (18:26–29). Nobody, not even full-time servants of God, stands exempt from gratitude and generous giving.

## Deeper Meaning

**Paul applies this temple-support principle directly to the church.** Writing to Corinth, Paul invokes this exact sanctuary rule: those who serve in the temple get their food from the temple, and those who wait at the altar share in what is offered on the altar; in the same way, the Lord commanded that those who preach the gospel should earn their living from the gospel (1 Corinthians 9:13–14).

**The Levites' tithing illuminates the argument of Hebrews.** Hebrews cites Levi's legal right to collect tithes in order to contrast Aaron's order with the far loftier priesthood of Melchizedek and Jesus (Hebrews 7:5–10). Every priest of Aaron's line eventually died, whereas Jesus lives forever, able to save completely all who draw near to God through him (Hebrews 7:23–25).

**"The LORD is my inheritance" becomes every believer's song.** Not only Aaron and the Levites savor that joy. King David sings that the LORD himself is his chosen portion and cup (Psalms 16:5). In the New Testament, the whole church is named a "holy priesthood" and a royal priesthood (1 Peter 2:5; 2:9), sharing an imperishable heavenly inheritance in Christ (Ephesians 1:11).

## Notes & Nuances

**What does "a covenant of salt" mean (18:19)?** Before refrigeration, salt preserved food from rotting. Across the ancient Near East, solemnizing a treaty often included sharing a meal with salt to pledge unbroken loyalty. Thus "an everlasting covenant of salt" vividly denotes a pure, incorruptible pledge that will never spoil or be revoked (cf. Leviticus 2:13; 2 Chronicles 13:5).

**Why does verse 1 show God speaking directly to Aaron instead of through Moses?** Almost everywhere in Numbers, God speaks to Moses, or to Moses and Aaron jointly. Having the LORD address Aaron directly by himself (18:1; 18:8; 18:20) is strikingly rare—a public, unmistakable seal right after chapters 16–17 confirming that God himself personally authenticated and entrusted Aaron's priesthood.

**Is tithing still mandatory for Christians today?** The tithe in Numbers 18 belonged directly to Israel's Tabernacle economy supporting the landless tribe of Levi. In the New Testament, the apostles do not re-impose a fixed Mosaic tax, calling believers instead to give generously, cheerfully, and proportionally as God prospers them (2 Corinthians 9:6–7). Yet the chapter's living heartbeat endures: honoring God with our first and best while caring faithfully for those who labor in the gospel.

**Gordon Wenham**, British Old Testament scholar, shows that Numbers 18 directly answers the congregation's cry of terror at 17:12–13.
