# Proverbs 5 — Avoiding the Snare of Adultery and Treasuring Marital Faithfulness

## Story

Chapter 5 is a candid, deeply perceptive lecture from a father to his son about sexuality, faithfulness in marriage, and the devastating price of adultery. The speech moves through three stages: unmasking the deception of temptation, celebrating the rich joy of marital love, and reminding the son that every human step lies open before God.

The father opens by urging his son to listen closely so he can guard discretion and knowledge. Then he strips away the sweet veneer of the adulterous woman: at first "the lips of the forbidden woman drip honey, and her speech is smoother than oil; but in the end she is bitter as wormwood, sharp as a double-edged sword." Her feet go down to death and her steps lead straight to Sheol; she gives no thought to the path of life, wandering along crooked trails without even realizing it (5:1–6).

For that reason, the father does not tell his son to *stand nearby and test himself*; he commands him flatly to keep his road far from her and never go near the door of her house. If he gives in, he will surrender his honor, his prime years, and his hard-earned wealth to strangers. At the end of his life, when his body is wasted and he stands on the brink of public disgrace before the assembly, all that will remain is bitter, belated regret—groaning over how he hated discipline and ignored his teachers (5:7–14).

Then, at verse 15, the harsh warning shifts abruptly into warm, lyrical poetry celebrating married love. The father turns to the image of pure water: "Drink water from your own cistern, and running water from your own well." Do not let your precious springs spill out into the public streets for strangers; keep them exclusively for yourself and your wife. He pronounces a blessing on that home and urges his son to rejoice and be captivated always by the love of "the wife of your youth"—comparing her grace to a loving doe and a graceful deer. Why ever be intoxicated by an outsider's embrace when real love waits inside your own home (5:15–20)?

The final three verses pull back the curtain to reveal the watchful eyes of God. No affair is ever truly hidden, for a man's ways are in full view of the LORD, and He examines all his paths. In the end, the wicked person is trapped by his own wrongdoing—"the cords of his sin hold him fast"—and he dies for refusing correction (5:21–23).

## Key Takeaways

**Temptation is always sweet at the beginning and bitter at the end.** The image of dripping honey turning into bitter wormwood and a two-edged sword (5:3–4) exposes the universal pattern of sin: it promises instant pleasure while hiding long-term pain, ruin, and regret.

**Defeat temptation with distance, not overconfidence.** The counsel of verse 8 is thoroughly practical: do not even go near the door of her house. Wise people do not test their willpower along the edge of a cliff; they change direction while they are still far away.

**The Bible never treats marital intimacy as shameful or second-rate.** On the contrary, verses 15–19 use vivid, tender imagery to celebrate physical and emotional delight within marriage. The strongest defense against counterfeit love outside the home is cultivating a warm, faithful love inside it.

**Sin does not merely bring outside punishment; it binds the sinner from within.** Verse 22 pictures the wicked person tangled in the cords of his own sin. Careless choices that look like freedom gradually harden into habits and chains that imprison the one who chose them.

## Deeper Meaning

**Faithful love in marriage reflects the covenant between God and His people.** The phrase "the wife of your youth" (5:18) is the exact expression the prophet Malachi uses when rebuking marital betrayal and tying it directly to covenant faithfulness before God (Malachi 2:14–15). In the New Testament, Paul lifts faithful marital love even higher as a living picture of Christ's love for the Church (Ephesians 5:25–32).

**The all-seeing gaze of God (5:21) reappears in Hebrews.** Nothing in all creation is hidden from God's sight; everything is uncovered and laid bare before the eyes of Him to whom we must give account (Hebrews 4:13). To the unrepentant that truth is a sober warning, while to the upright it is deep comfort.

**Being bound by the cords of sin prepares for Christ's teaching on true liberation.** Verse 22 says sinners are held fast by their own cords. Jesus later declares that everyone who sins is a slave to sin, and only if the Son sets you free will you be free indeed (John 8:34–36).

## Notes & Nuances

**The images of a *cistern* and *well* (5:15–18) are traditional poetic metaphors for marital intimacy.** In the arid landscape of ancient Israel, a private household well was a priceless, refreshing, closely guarded source of life. The poet borrows that familiar imagery to speak both delicately and passionately about satisfaction within marriage, contrasting it with squandering affection and intimacy in the public streets.

**Why does Proverbs consistently speak from a father to a son about an adulterous woman?** In the ancient social setting, Proverbs was compiled first of all to train young men preparing for adulthood, marriage, and civic responsibility. Even so, its principles of marital faithfulness and fleeing temptation apply with complete equality to both men and women.
