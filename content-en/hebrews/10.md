# Hebrews 10 — The Once-for-All Sacrifice and the New Way Through the Curtain

## Story

The author wraps up his theology of Christ's priesthood with a decisive verdict. Since the law holds only "a shadow of the good things to come"—not the reality itself—its endless annual sacrifices can never make worshipers perfect. If they could, worshipers would have been cleansed once for all, felt no lingering guilt, and stopped sacrificing long ago. Instead, yearly sacrifices merely jogged the memory that sin still lingered, because "it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins" (10:1–4).

Accordingly, as Christ entered the world, He spoke the words of Psalm 40: sacrifices and offerings You did not desire, nor did burnt offerings and sin offerings under the law delight You, "but a body You prepared for Me"; and the Son declared, "Here I am... I have come to do Your will, O God." God thus sets aside the old sacrificial system to establish the new reality of complete obedience. By that very will we have been sanctified "through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all" (10:5–10; Psalms 40:6–8).

The writer sets two vivid postures side by side: day after day every old priest "stands ministering" and repeatedly offers the exact same sacrifices that can never erase sins; Christ, however, after offering "one sacrifice for sins for all time," "sat down at the right hand of God," waiting until His enemies become a footstool for His feet. By that single offering He has "perfected for all time" those being sanctified. The Holy Spirit confirms this in the new covenant promise: once God writes His laws on hearts and pledges, "Their sins and lawless acts I will remember no more," where sin has been completely forgiven, no further sin offering is ever needed (10:11–18; Psalms 110:1; Jeremiah 31:33–34).

From that towering theological summit, 10:19 pivots directly into everyday living—launching the practical second half of the letter. Because we now have "confidence to enter the Most Holy Place by the blood of Jesus," stepping along "a new and living way opened for us through the curtain, that is, His body," and because we have a great Priest over God's house, the author issues three calls: let us "draw near" with a sincere heart in full assurance of faith, consciences sprinkled clean and bodies washed pure; let us hold unswervingly to the hope we profess, for He who promised is faithful; and let us consider how to "spur one another on to love and good deeds," never neglecting to meet together as some have made a habit of doing, encouraging each other all the more as we see the Day approaching (10:19–25).

Immediately behind that warm appeal stands the letter's fourth stern warning. If we "deliberately go on sinning" after receiving the knowledge of the truth—meaning turning our backs to repudiate Christ—"no further sacrifice for sins remains," leaving only a terrifying expectation of judgment and raging fire that consumes God's adversaries. Anyone rejecting Moses's law died without mercy on the testimony of two or three witnesses; how much harsher punishment does someone deserve who has "trampled the Son of God underfoot," treated the covenant blood that sanctified him as common, and insulted the Spirit of grace! Scripture declares the Lord will avenge and judge His people: "It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God!" (10:26–31; Deuteronomy 17:6; 32:35–36).

Then the writer's voice softens, calling back memories of their own bold early days. Right after being enlightened, they endured "a great struggle with suffering": some were publicly mocked and abused, while others stood loyally beside persecuted brothers and sisters. They sympathized with prisoners and "joyfully accepted the confiscation of your property," knowing they owned a better, lasting inheritance. So do not throw away that "confidence"—it carries a rich reward. You need endurance so that after doing God's will you receive what was promised. Closing with Habakkuk's prophecy, he reminds them that in just a little while the Coming One will arrive without delay; My righteous one will live by faith, and if he shrinks back My soul takes no pleasure in him. And he concludes with steady trust: we are not "those who shrink back and are destroyed," but "those who have faith and preserve their souls" (10:32–39; Habakkuk 2:3–4).

## Key Takeaways

**What God truly seeks is wholehearted obedience, not endless ritual.** Thousands of slain animals across centuries were only shadows reminding humanity of unpaid sin debt. When Christ stepped into the world saying, "I have come to do Your will," He brought the sole sacrifice of eternal worth: a life and death of seamless obedience (10:1–10).

**Three practical calls flowing from the cross: faith, hope, and love.** In 10:22–24, the writer distills Christian living into three invitations—"let us": draw near to God in full faith, hold fast our unshakable hope, and watch out for one another to spark love. Deep faith never isolates believers into lonely corners; it draws us closer to God and pulls us closer to our church family (10:19–25).

**Dropping out of fellowship is the first sign of spiritual drift.** Urging believers "not to neglect meeting together" (10:25) is no cold attendance rule. Facing social ostracism and pressure, slipping away from the church leaves individuals exposed to temptation and slow erosion of faith. Gathering together is where believers keep one another's fire burning (10:24–25).

**Remember earlier courage so you do not quit halfway.** Under grinding, drawn-out pressure, we easily forget how bravely we once stood. The writer reminds them of days when they gladly lost property to visit imprisoned friends—not to boast of past deeds, but to recall that the heavenly treasure they once trusted still stands completely intact (10:32–36).

## Deeper Meaning

**The temple curtain torn in two.** Seeing Jesus open "a new and living way... through the curtain, that is, His body" (10:20) bridges two pivotal biblical moments: the thick curtain of Exodus 26:31–33 sealing off the Most Holy Place, and the moment Jesus breathed His last on the cross when the temple curtain ripped from top to bottom (Matthew 27:51; Mark 15:38). His body was torn open so the road straight home to God stands open forever.

**Standing versus sitting—the postures of two covenants.** Verses 10:11–12 restate the letter's defining contrast: old priests kept "standing" in nonstop service because sin remained uncleansed, while Christ "sat down at the right hand of God" (Psalms 110:1). Every anxious religious question about whether we have done enough to pay off guilt ends right beside that seated Christ.

**Habakkuk's prophecy unlocks chapter 11.** "My righteous one will live by faith" (10:38; Habakkuk 2:4) stands among the New Testament's great cornerstones—Paul builds Romans (Romans 1:17) and Galatians (Galatians 3:11) right on it. In Hebrews, the verse spotlights faithful perseverance while waiting, swinging open the door to chapter 11's celebrated gallery of faith.

## Notes & Nuances

**What does "deliberately go on sinning" mean in 10:26?** Tender-conscienced believers sometimes read this verse in panic, terrified that any known slip forfeits forgiveness forever. Yet 10:29 spells out unmistakably what "deliberately sinning" signifies here: decisively "trampling the Son of God underfoot," despising covenant blood, and repudiating Christ—open apostasy matching 6:4–6. Whoever throws away Christ's lone sacrifice naturally leaves no second sacrifice anywhere to cover their sin.

**Verse 10:5 quotes Psalms 40:6 from the Greek Septuagint.** Where the Hebrew text of Psalms 40:6 reads "my ears You have opened" (an image of attentive obedience), the ancient Greek Septuagint translates "a body You prepared for Me." Hebrews uses that widely read Greek version to emphasize that Christ carried out His obedience concretely in a real flesh-and-blood body offered on the cross (10:10).

**How are believers already "sanctified" (10:10) and still "being sanctified" (10:14)?** Verse 10:10 looks at finished status: through the cross, believers have been definitively set apart for God once for all. Verse 10:14 uses a present ongoing participle ("those who are being sanctified") pointing to daily spiritual growth. We stand completely secure in status in Christ while growing step by step to live out who we are.

## What Commentators Say

**John Calvin** notes on "not neglecting to meet together" (10:25) that because human hearts cool and wander so quickly, God established corporate church gatherings as a God-given safeguard for faith. Anyone pulling back from Christian community out of pride or inconvenience strips away the very strength God designed to hold them up. *(Commentary on the Epistle to the Hebrews, on chapter 10 verse 25)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: emphasizing corporate church fellowship as God's remedy against spiritual coldness is very clear in Calvin on Hebrews chapter 10 verse 25 -->

**John Owen** explains that "deliberately sinning" in 10:26 never refers to ordinary stumbles of believers who know God's law, but to a willful, final repudiation of the gospel after grasping the truth. He draws a parallel to Mosaic law, which provided sacrifices for sins of weakness yet offered zero sacrifice for defiant, high-handed contempt of God (Numbers 15:30). *(An Exposition of the Epistle to the Hebrews, on chapter 10 verses 26–29)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: linking Hebrews chapter 10 verse 26 to high-handed sin in Numbers chapter 15 verse 30 is a classic argument in John Owen -->

**Charles Spurgeon** preached on 10:12 ("sat down at the right hand of God") that the whole difference between human religion and the gospel boils down to two verbs: *standing* and *sitting*. Every self-salvation system keeps people standing endlessly without ever knowing if they have done enough; the gospel invites us to rest in the One who finished everything and sat down at God's right hand. *(Sermon on Hebrews chapter 10 verse 12, Metropolitan Tabernacle Pulpit)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: clearly recall Spurgeon contrasting standing and sitting in Hebrews chapter 10 verses 11–12; exact sermon number not verified -->
