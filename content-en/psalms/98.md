# Psalms 98 — Joyful Shouts Before the King Coming to Judge

## Story

This exuberant song in the *The LORD Reigns* series opens summoning everyone to sing a "new song" to the LORD because He has done marvelous things: His own right hand and holy arm have won the victory. Rather than keeping His salvation hidden in a corner, He has revealed His righteousness openly in the sight of the nations. He has remembered His steadfast love and faithfulness to the house of Israel, so that "all the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God" (98:1–3).

The middle section erupts into a worldwide symphony. The psalmist summons all the earth to shout joyfully, burst into jubilant song, and make music with harp, trumpets, and the blast of the ram's horn "before the LORD, the King" (98:4–6).

In the finale, creation itself joins the orchestra: let the sea and everything in it resound, the world join in, "let the rivers clap their hands; let the mountains sing together for joy before the LORD." The closing verse gives the reason for cosmic celebration: He is coming to judge the earth with righteousness and equity (98:7–9).

## Key Takeaways

**God's salvation is a victory won solely by His own hand.** Saying "His right hand and holy arm have gained Him the victory" underscores that nobody lent God a hand or earned His rescue—He stepped in Himself to save His people (98:1).

**Covenant faithfulness to Israel becomes saving news for earth's farthest corners.** God kept His loving devotion to Israel not to lock blessing inside one border, but so all the ends of the earth would witness His salvation (98:2–3).

**God's righteous reign brings total joy to humanity and creation alike.** When the true King arrives to govern fairly, rivers clap and mountains sing together (98:6–9).

## Deeper Meaning

**Mary's song and Simeon's blessing in Luke echo Psalm 98 directly.** Rejoicing over Jesus' coming birth, Mary sang that God performed mighty deeds with His arm and remembered His mercy toward Israel (Luke 1:51; 1:54). Soon after, aged Simeon cradled infant Jesus in the temple and declared his eyes had seen God's salvation prepared in the sight of all peoples (Luke 2:30–32)—mirroring verses 2 and 3 unmistakably (98:2–3).

**God revealing His righteousness to the nations opens the gospel theme of Romans.** Verse 2—the LORD making His salvation known and revealing His righteousness before the nations (98:2)—foreshadows Paul's banner statement in Romans: the gospel is God's power for salvation to everyone who believes, because in it the righteousness of God is revealed (Romans 1:16–17).

## Notes & Nuances

**Inspiration behind a beloved Christmas carol.** Hymnwriter Isaac Watts directly paraphrased verses 4 through 9 to write *Joy to the World*—turning fields, floods, rocks, hills, and plains rejoicing at the coming King into a timeless celebration of Christ's first and second comings (98:4–9).
