# Psalms 138 — The High Lord Still Regards the Lowly

## Story

Psalm 138 opens the final cluster of eight psalms attributed to David (Psalms 138–145). Right from the first line, the psalmist lifts up wholehearted and fearless thanksgiving: "I give You thanks with all my heart; before the gods I sing Your praises." Bowing toward the holy temple, he praises God's loving devotion and faithfulness, because God has exalted His promise, and on the very day he called out, "You answered me; You emboldened me and strengthened my soul" (138:1–3).

The horizon of the song suddenly widens from a private room of prayer to the whole earth. The psalmist believes that one day "all the kings of the earth will give You thanks, O LORD, when they hear the words of Your mouth," and they will sing of the ways of the Lord. The reason His glory is so astonishing lies in a wonderful paradox: "Though the LORD is on high, He attends to the lowly; but the proud He knows from afar" (138:4–6).

At the close, the psalmist returns to the real dangers ahead of him with calm confidence. Even when walking in the midst of trouble, he trusts that the Lord will preserve his life and stretch out His right hand to save him from the anger of his enemies. He finishes with a tender, deep assurance—"The LORD will fulfill His purpose for me"—and asks the God whose loving devotion endures forever not to abandon the works of His hands (138:7–8).

## Key Takeaways

**God answers prayer by strengthening us within, not only by changing our outward circumstances.** Verse 3 does not say God immediately erased every hardship; it says He *emboldened me and strengthened my soul*. Often the first miracle of answered prayer is fresh courage right inside the storm.

**The highest God stays closest to the humblest people.** Verse 6 captures the paradoxical heart of God throughout Scripture: He never measures human worth by rank or swagger. The proud He keeps at a distance, while He bends down to watch over the lowly.

**God never leaves His work half-done.** Verse 8 gives believers deep peace: the purpose God has begun in the lives of His children, He Himself will bring to completion.

## Deeper Meaning

**God will complete the work He began.** The assurance that "The LORD will fulfill His purpose for me" (138:8) resounds clearly in Paul's words to the church at Philippi: He who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus (Philippians 1:6).

**The God who opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble.** The principle in verse 6 is reaffirmed in the New Testament by both James and Peter (James 4:6; 1 Peter 5:5), and shines most completely in Jesus Himself—who, being in very nature God, humbled Himself and took the form of a servant (Philippians 2:6–8).

**Earth's kings submitting to Christ.** The vision of earthly rulers hearing God's words and giving Him glory (138:4–5) looks ahead to the day when the nations and the kings of the earth bring their splendor into the New Jerusalem (Revelation 21:24).

## Notes & Nuances

**"Before the gods" in verse 1.** The Hebrew word *elohim* ("gods") in the phrase "before the gods" can be understood in several ways: in defiance of the false deities of the nations (declaring that the Lord alone is real), in the presence of heavenly angels (as the ancient Greek Septuagint translates it), or before earthly rulers and judges (Psalms 82:1). Whichever nuance one chooses, the main point stands: the psalmist boldly honors God in front of every competing power.

## What Commentators Say

**Charles Spurgeon**, nineteenth-century English pastor, treasured verse 8 as a sure guarantee of the perseverance of the saints.
