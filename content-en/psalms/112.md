# Psalms 112 — Portrait of the Person Who Fears the LORD

## Story

Opening with *Hallelujah*, Psalm 112 picks up directly where Psalm 111 closed on the fear of the LORD: "Blessed is the man who fears the LORD, who greatly delights in His commandments!" The poem sketches that person's life and blessings: descendants flourishing in the land, a well-provided home, and righteousness enduring forever (112:1–3).

Even when dark seasons hit, "Light dawns in the darkness for the upright." This believer reflects God's very own nature—gracious, compassionate, and righteous—dealing generously, lending fairly without exploitation, conducting affairs justly so they remain unshaken, leaving a lasting good name (112:4–6).

One standout mark of the godly person is quiet calm amid upheaval: "He does not fear bad news; his heart is steadfast, trusting in the LORD." With a steady heart, they face adversaries unafraid while giving freely to others: "He has scattered abroad his gifts to the poor; his righteousness endures forever." Their honor stands lifted high, while the wicked watch furious, gnash their teeth, and watch all their selfish cravings melt away (112:7–10).

## Key Takeaways

**Worshipers gradually grow to resemble the God they adore.** In Psalm 111, God Himself is gracious, compassionate, and righteous forever (Psalms 111:3–4); right next door in Psalm 112, those identical words describe the person who fears Him (112:3–4; 112:9). Genuine faith reshapes character.

**Trusting the LORD cures panic over bad news.** Verses 7 and 8 never promise believers will never hear distressing news, yet assure us we need not crumble when hard news arrives because our hearts stand anchored in the LORD (112:7–8).

**Generosity toward the poor walks hand in hand with integrity.** Someone blessed by God never hoards wealth tightly, lending fairly and giving freely to those in need (112:5; 112:9).

## Deeper Meaning

**The apostle Paul quotes verse 9 to encourage Christian generosity (112:9).** Urging Corinthian believers to give cheerfully for needy Christians in Jerusalem, Paul cites this exact verse—"He has scattered abroad His gifts to the poor; His righteousness endures forever"—to show God abundantly supplies cheerful givers (2 Corinthians 9:8–9).

**Light breaking through darkness.** Light dawning amid shadow for the upright (112:4) reaches full brightness in Jesus, the light of the world whose followers never walk in darkness (John 8:12).

## Notes & Nuances

**An exact alphabetic twin to Psalm 111.** Mirroring Psalm 111 line for line, Psalm 112 comprises 22 crisp alphabetic lines from *Aleph* to *Tav*. Placed side by side, Psalm 111 portrays God's character and Psalm 112 portrays the human life shaped in His likeness.

**Promises of wealth and stability in verses 2 and 3 express general wisdom, not rigid guarantees (112:2–3).** Like Proverbs, this poem traces the ordinary healthy fruit of upright, diligent, generous living—while candidly naming *darkness* and *bad news* (112:4; 112:7), proving godly people still encounter storms rather than floating above hardship.

## What Commentators Say

**John Calvin**, sixteenth-century Reformer, highlights "who greatly delights in His commandments" in verse 1: someone who truly fears God never obeys grudgingly from dread of punishment, genuinely rejoicing in God's word.
