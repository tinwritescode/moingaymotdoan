# Psalms 111 — Praising God's Great Works and Eternal Covenant

## Story

Psalm 111 opens with a bright *Hallelujah* and a pledge to thank the LORD wholeheartedly in the council of the upright and the full assembly. The psalmist declares, "Great are the works of the LORD; they are pondered by all who delight in them"—radiant with splendor and majesty, matched by righteousness enduring forever (111:1–3).

He recalls unforgettable wonders God has made memorable: "The LORD is gracious and compassionate." Providing food for those who fear Him, He always remembers His covenant and demonstrated His mighty power by giving His people the inheritance of the nations (111:4–6).

Moving from saving history to the moral reliability of God's deeds and word, he affirms that everything God's hands do is faithful and just, all His precepts stand trustworthy forever. "He has sent redemption to His people" and ordained His covenant eternally; holy and awesome is His name (111:7–9).

The closing line distills the entire psalm into a timeless proverb: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom"; everyone who practices His precepts gains sound understanding, and His praise endures forever (111:10).

## Key Takeaways

**Loving God sparks eager curiosity into all He has made and done.** Verse 2 proves faith never fears study or careful inquiry: whoever delights in God's works—across creation and history—loves exploring them and praising Him all the more deeply (111:2).

**God does not save once and step away—He keeps covenant and feeds His own.** Redemption, daily bread, and trustworthy commands all flow from the exact same gracious, compassionate character (111:4–9).

**True wisdom starts with reverence and shows up in action.** In verse 10, biblical insight lives not merely in clever brains, but in obedient living: the wise person honors God and walks out His commands (111:10).

## Deeper Meaning

**Redeeming His people and His eternal covenant.** Verse 9 ("He has sent redemption to His people") rings directly inside Zechariah's song anticipating the Messiah: God has visited and redeemed His people, remembering His holy covenant (111:9; Luke 1:68; 1:72). In the New Testament, that eternal redemption is accomplished through Christ's blood (Hebrews 9:12; 13:20).

**The beginning of wisdom.** Verse 10 joins seamlessly with the keynote of Proverbs (111:10; Proverbs 1:7; 9:10), while the New Testament reveals that all treasures of wisdom and knowledge lie hidden in Christ (Colossians 2:3).

## Notes & Nuances

**The tightest alphabetic acrostic in the Psalter.** After the opening *Hallelujah*, the poem consists of exactly 22 crisp lines stepping straight through the 22 letters of the Hebrew alphabet (*Aleph* to *Tav*), making it wonderfully easy to memorize. Psalm 112 right next door uses the identical 22-line pattern to form a matched twin.
