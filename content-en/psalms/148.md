# Psalms 148 — All Creation in Chorus Praising Its Maker

## Story

Psalm 148 throws open the stage of praise to the widest horizon imaginable: the entire universe, from the highest heavens down to the ocean depths. The song unfolds in two balanced halves—the first ringing down from the skies, the second rising up from the earth.

The first half calls the heavenly choir to begin: "Praise the LORD from the heavens!" The psalmist calls the roll from angels and heavenly hosts down to sun, moon, all shining stars, the highest heavens, and the waters above the skies. The reason is unmistakable: "for He commanded and they were created," and He Himself established the lasting decrees by which they endure (148:1–6).

The second half turns downward to the earth, starting from its deepest floor and climbing upward: great sea creatures and all ocean depths, fire and hail, snow and clouds, "stormy winds that obey His word," mountains and hills, fruit trees and cedars, wild beasts and cattle, small creatures and flying birds (148:7–10).

The climax of the earthly choir is all humanity, without regard to rank or age: "kings of the earth and all peoples, princes and all rulers of the earth, young men and maidens, old and young together." Everyone is summoned to join in because "His name alone is exalted; His splendor is above the earth and the heavens." The closing verse narrows its loving focus to His covenant people—Israel, whom God has lifted up and kept as "a people near to Him" (148:11–14).

## Key Takeaways

**Nature is not something to worship—it is our partner in worshiping God.** Many ancient nations revered sun, moon, stars, and sea monsters as deities. Psalm 148 puts every one of them in its rightful place: they are fellow creatures spoken into being by God's command, existing to honor their Maker.

**Even the fiercest forces obey God's word.** Fire, hail, stormy winds, and deep-sea monsters are not chaotic powers outside God's control; they are *obeying His word* (148:7–8).

**Every class of humanity stands equal before God's throne.** From monarchs and judges to youth, elders, and little children (148:11–12), everyone takes a place side by side in the same choir exalting His name alone.

## Deeper Meaning

**All things created through and for Christ.** Verse 5 declares, "He commanded and they were created," meeting directly the New Testament confession about Jesus: all things in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, were created through Him and for Him (Colossians 1:16; John 1:3).

**The cosmic anthem before the throne and the Lamb.** The vision of heaven and earth singing in unison in Psalm 148 foreshadows the climactic scene in Revelation, where every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth, and on the sea lifts blessing to Him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb (Revelation 5:13).

**A people brought near by the blood of Christ.** The tender title in verse 14—"a people near to Him"—is echoed by Paul to describe New Testament salvation: Gentiles who once were far away have now been *brought near* in Christ Jesus through His blood (Ephesians 2:13).

## Notes & Nuances

**How do inanimate things praise God?** Mountains, snow, and cedar trees have no mouths to sing as humans do. In biblical poetry, non-human creation praises God simply by being what He designed it to be and obeying the order He established (148:6; 148:8)—while humanity serves as creation's conscious voice, putting that silent praise into words.

## What Commentators Say

**John Calvin**, sixteenth-century Reformer, observed that summoning lifeless things like snow, storms, and mountains to praise God sharply rebukes human ingratitude: if an unthinking cosmos still obeys and honors its Creator, then human beings endowed with reason and speech have no excuse for silence. *(Commentary on the Book of Psalms, on Psalm 148)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: recalls Calvin's characteristic argument that inanimate creation shames human ingratitude in Psalm 148 -->
