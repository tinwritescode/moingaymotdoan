# Psalms 99 — The Holy King Who Forgives and Disciplines

## Story

Closing the *The LORD Reigns* collection (Psalms 93–99), Psalm 99 opens with majesty that makes the entire planet shiver: "The LORD reigns; let the nations tremble! He is enthroned above the cherubim; let the earth quake!" Great in Zion and exalted over all peoples, He receives the first reverent refrain: "He is holy!" (99:1–3).

The second stanza clarifies the moral character of this mighty King: He loves justice, has established equity, and executes righteousness across Jacob. Worshipers are therefore invited to exalt the LORD and bow at His footstool, before the second refrain echoes: "He is holy!" (99:4–5).

The final stanza recalls three towering intercessors from Israel's history—Moses, Aaron, and Samuel—who called on the LORD's name and heard Him answer out of the pillar of cloud. The psalm pauses on a strikingly balanced portrait of God: "You were to them a forgiving God, yet an avenger of their misdeeds." Calling everyone to worship at His holy mountain, the song seals with its third and fullest refrain: "For the LORD our God is holy!" (99:6–9).

## Key Takeaways

**Holiness stands at the very center of God's reign.** Three times the psalm halts to proclaim *He is holy* (99:3; 99:5; 99:9). God's supreme power is inseparable from His absolute purity and justice.

**The exalted Holy One still listens to human intercessors.** Even though earth quakes before His throne above the cherubim, He spoke from the pillar of cloud with Moses, Aaron, and Samuel and answered their prayers on behalf of His people (99:1; 99:6–7).

**Forgiveness never trivializes sin's consequences.** Verse 8 captures God's gracious holiness in one breath: He genuinely forgave His people, yet still disciplined their wrongdoings to shape them in holiness (99:8).

## Deeper Meaning

**The threefold *holy* refrain echoes the seraphim in Isaiah and Revelation.** Proclaiming "He is holy!" three times (99:3; 99:5; 99:9) mirrors the *Holy, holy, holy* sung around the LORD's throne in Isaiah's vision (Isaiah 6:3) and by the four living creatures before heaven's throne in Revelation (Revelation 4:8).

**Ancient intercessors point forward to Jesus, the flawless Mediator.** Moses, Aaron, and Samuel stood in the gap praying for Israel (Exodus 32:11; Numbers 16:46–48; 1 Samuel 7:8–9), yet they themselves remained frail mortals needing pardon and correction. They foreshadow Jesus—our holy, sinless High Priest who always lives to intercede for His people (Hebrews 7:25–26).

## Notes & Nuances

**Three stanzas anchored by the *holy* refrain.** Psalm 99 divides cleanly into three sections (99:1–3; 99:4–5; 99:6–9), each concluding with a confession of God's holiness. *His footstool* (99:5) refers to the ark of the covenant and temple in Jerusalem (cf. 1 Chronicles 28:2).
