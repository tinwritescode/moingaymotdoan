# Psalms 117 — The Shortest Summons to All Nations

## Story

The briefest chapter in the entire Bible—just two verses long—Psalm 117 stretches wide enough to embrace the entire planet. Verse 1 sends a ringing call far beyond Israel's borders: "Praise the LORD, all you nations! Extol Him, all you peoples!" (117:1).

Verse 2 instantly supplies the reason every nation holds a seat in that choir: "For great is His loving devotion toward us, and the faithfulness of the LORD endures forever. Hallelujah!" (117:2).

## Key Takeaways

**Israel's God is no tribal deity—He is Lord of every nation.** Brief as this poem is, it holds the widest horizon in the entire *Hallel*: the LORD's immense covenant love and endless faithfulness invite every people on earth to praise Him together (117:1–2).

**Praise stands on two unshakable pillars: loving devotion and faithfulness.** Two cornerstone Hebrew terms in verse 2—*hesed* (loyal covenant love) and *emet* (unfailing truth)—sum up every reason humanity can trust and praise God forever.

## Deeper Meaning

**Paul quotes Psalm 117 to prove the gospel embraces Gentiles.** Explaining in Romans that Christ came not only to confirm promises to Israel, but also so the Gentiles might glorify God for His mercy, Paul quotes verse 1 right alongside other Old Testament witnesses (117:1; Romans 15:8–11).

**An overture to heaven's global choir.** Summoning every nation and people to extol God (117:1) comes to complete fulfillment in Scripture's final vision, where an uncountable multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and language stands praising before God's throne (Revelation 7:9–10).

## Notes & Nuances

**The Bible's shortest chapter standing right near its center.** Just two verses long, Psalm 117 sits right beside Psalm 118 and directly ahead of Psalm 119 (the Bible's longest chapter). Several ancient Hebrew manuscripts attach Psalm 117 directly to Psalm 116 or 118 as a brief festive refrain during Passover.
