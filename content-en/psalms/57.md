# Psalms 57 — Sheltered Beneath Your Wings and Singing at Dawn

## Story

The traditional heading links this psalm to David hiding from King Saul inside a dark cave (1 Samuel 22:1; 24:3). Surrounded by damp shadow and looming peril, David pleads twice for God's mercy because his soul takes refuge "in the shadow of Your wings... until the danger has passed." Crying out to God Most High—who fulfills His purpose for him—he trusts God will send "His loving devotion and His faithfulness" down from heaven to rescue him from fierce enemies like lions whose teeth are spears and arrows and whose tongues are sharp swords (57:1–4).

Right inside the crisis, a sweeping refrain of praise bursts forth: "Be exalted, O God, above the heavens; may Your glory cover all the earth!" (57:5).

David looks back at his enemies' traps: they spread a net for his feet and dug a pit across his path until his spirit nearly sank, yet in the end they tumbled straight into the very pit they dug. From there the psalm shifts completely into steady joy: "My heart is steadfast, O God, my heart is steadfast; I will sing and make music." He rouses his soul along with harp and lyre before sunrise to awaken the dawn with songs of thanks to the Lord among the nations (57:6–9).

The psalm closes by naming the reason for such joy—God's steadfast love reaches to the heavens and His faithfulness to the clouds—before repeating its soaring refrain exalting God above heavens and earth (57:10–11).

## Key Takeaways

**The safest shelter in calamity is not a stone cave, but the shadow of God's wings.** Like a chick tucked warmly under its mother's wing (57:1), God's protection is intimate and tender. A cave can hide the body, yet only God keeps the soul serene.

**Believers pray not only for personal safety, but for God's glory.** The twin refrains in verses 5 and 11 reveal David's highest priority amid danger: not merely his own escape, but seeing God's glory exalted over heaven and earth (57:5; 57:11).

**A steadfast heart births song even in darkness.** Still sitting inside the cave, David declares "My heart is steadfast" and wakes his instruments to greet the morning (57:7–8). Deep gladness depends not on smooth circumstances, but on anchoring the heart in God.

**God's grace is too big to keep private.** Having tasted loving devotion high as the heavens, David vows to sing God's praises across every nation and people (57:9–10).

## Deeper Meaning

**Taking shelter under God's wings echoes in Jesus' tender lament.** Just as David nestled beneath God's wings (57:1), Jesus later compared His love for Jerusalem to a hen longing to gather her chicks under her wings—grieving that they were unwilling (Matthew 23:37).

**Praising God among the nations anticipates the global mission of the gospel.** When David promises to sing among all peoples (57:9), the psalm's horizon stretches far beyond Israel's borders. In the New Testament, Paul draws on this same Old Testament chorus to show God always planned to welcome Gentiles into worshiping Him (Romans 15:9).

## Notes & Nuances

**The final five verses of Psalm 57 appear nearly verbatim as the opening of Psalm 108.** Reading Psalm 108 shows verses 1 through 5 matching Psalm 57's conclusion (57:7–11; Psalms 108:1–5), paired with the ending of Psalm 60. This reveals how ancient Israel freely wove treasured passages together for fresh worship gatherings.

**The phrase *my glory* in verse 8 refers to David's own soul or innermost self (57:8).** In Hebrew poetry, *glory* sometimes parallels *soul* (Psalms 16:9; 30:12). Summoning "my glory" to wake up means rousing every fiber of his inner being to praise God at daybreak.
