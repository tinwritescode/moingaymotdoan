# Psalms 10 — When God Seems Far Away

## Story

In sharp contrast to the exuberant thanksgiving opening Psalm 9, Psalm 10 begins with an aching question: "Why, O LORD, do You stand far off? Why do You hide in times of trouble?" (10:1). Before the psalmist stretches a grim scene of injustice: the wicked arrogantly hunt down the poor, boast of their own cravings, and revile the LORD (10:2–3).

The poem probes inside the mind of the wicked person. He tells himself, "There is no God," assuming God will never call him to account (10:4). Seeing his schemes succeed at every turn, he grows even more cocky, convinced misfortune will never touch him (10:5–6). His mouth overflows with curses, deceit, and oppression; he lurks in ambush like a lion in its den, waiting to pounce on the helpless and innocent (10:7–10). What galls the psalmist most is his smug muttering: "God has forgotten; He hides His face and never sees" (10:11).

At verse 12, the psalmist refuses to stay quiet any longer and cries aloud: "Arise, O LORD! Lift up Your hand, O God. Do not forget the helpless" (10:12–13). Then faith breaks through the clouds: in truth, God has seen every bit of trouble and grief; the victim entrusts himself to God, for He is the helper of the fatherless (10:14–15). The poem closes with a majestic affirmation: the LORD is King forever and ever, He hears the longing of the afflicted, and He will secure justice for the orphan and the oppressed (10:16–18).

## Key Takeaways

**Biblical atheism is above all a practical way of living without accountability to God.** The wicked say "There is no God" less out of philosophical argument than out of a desire to believe they will never answer to Him (10:4; 10:11; 10:13).

**Feeling that God is distant does not mean God has forgotten.** Early in the psalm the writer asks why God hides Himself (10:1), yet by the end he affirms, "You have seen it," and testifies that God hears even the unspoken longing of the humble (10:14; 10:17).

**God's eternal kingship is the hope of the vulnerable.** Because the LORD reigns forever, orphans and the oppressed are never abandoned to violent bullies who are merely "of the earth" (10:16; 10:18).

## Deeper Meaning

**The indictment of the wicked mouth in verse 7 is folded into Paul's universal diagnosis of human sin (10:7).** While proving that all humanity stands under sin, Paul quotes this very description of a mouth full of cursing and bitterness (Romans 3:14).

**The promise that God is *the helper of the fatherless* and champion of the oppressed comes alive in Jesus' ministry (10:14; 10:18).** He came to proclaim good news to the poor and set the oppressed free (Luke 4:18–19), declaring that the meek will inherit the earth (Matthew 5:5).

## Notes & Nuances

**Psalm 10 has no heading of its own in Hebrew because it originally formed one unit with Psalm 9.** The two share striking echoes—such as *the afflicted*, *times of trouble*, *arise*, and *mortal / man of the earth*—forming a paired sequence moving from thanksgiving (Psalm 9) to an honest cry amid ongoing injustice (Psalm 10).

**The apparent success of the wicked—*His ways are always prosperous*—raises a classic question about why evildoers thrive (10:5).** Scripture returns to explore this puzzle more deeply in Psalms 37 and 73.
