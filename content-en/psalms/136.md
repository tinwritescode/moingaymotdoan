# Psalms 136 — His Loving Devotion Endures Forever

## Story

Psalm 136 is composed as a grand responsive anthem: a leader sings the first half of each verse recounting what God has done, and the entire congregation answers in unison with the same refrain across all twenty-six verses: "His loving devotion endures forever."

The opening section calls God's people to give thanks to the One who is utterly supreme: the Lord is good, the God of gods, the Lord of lords, "He alone does great wonders" (136:1–4).

The second section lifts its eyes to the work of creation: "By His insight He made the heavens," spread out the earth upon the waters, and set the sun to rule the day and the moon and stars to govern the night (136:5–9).

The longest middle section rehearses the great story of the Exodus and the conquest of the Promised Land. God struck down the firstborn of Egypt, brought Israel out of slavery "with a strong hand and an outstretched arm," "divided the Red Sea in two" so Israel could pass through while Pharaoh's army was swept away, led His people through the wilderness, and defeated formidable rulers like Sihon and Og to give their land as an inheritance to His servant Israel (136:10–22).

The closing section brings that ancient story straight into the present and widens it to all creation: "He remembered us in our low estate," rescued us from our foes, and "gives food to every creature." The hymn ends with a final summons to give thanks to "the God of heaven" (136:23–26).

## Key Takeaways

**Creation and redemption spring from the same steadfast love.** People often think of creating the cosmos merely in terms of raw power, and defeating oppressors merely in terms of justice. Psalm 136 attaches the exact same refrain of *loving devotion* to both: every star in the sky and the path split through the Red Sea alike flow from God's unfailing covenant love.

**Repetition anchors truth deep in memory.** Singing the same line twenty-six times is not filler. In seasons of weakness or exile, that steady rhythm drives home one unshakable truth: circumstances shift, yet the Lord's faithful love remains forever.

**The God of the universe still remembers the lowly and feeds every living thing.** From the scale of the heavens and mighty empires, the psalm drops unexpectedly close in verses 23 and 25: God notices His people in their *low estate* and provides daily food for every creature.

## Deeper Meaning

**Lord of lords and love from which nothing can separate us.** The title "Lord of lords" (136:3) is given directly in the New Testament to the risen and glorified Christ (Revelation 17:14; Revelation 19:16). And the refrain *His loving devotion endures forever* reaches full bloom in Paul's confession that neither death nor life nor anything else in all creation can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:38–39).

**The One who feeds every living creature.** The praise in verse 25 that God gives food to every creature echoes in Jesus' Sermon on the Mount, where He points to the birds of the air that neither sow nor reap, yet the heavenly Father feeds them (Matthew 6:26).

## Notes & Nuances

**The Great Hallel in Jewish liturgy.** In Jewish tradition, Psalm 136 is known as the *Great Hallel*, often sung at Passover alongside Psalms 113–118. Its call-and-response structure shows that it was crafted to be sung aloud by a whole assembly rather than merely read in silence.

**The meaning of "loving devotion" (hesed).** The Hebrew word *hesed*, rendered *loving devotion* (or *steadfast love*), means far more than a passing feeling of pity. It describes loyal, covenant-keeping love that holds fast to its promises even when the other partner is weak or faltering.
