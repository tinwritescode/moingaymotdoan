# Psalms 107 — Four Portraits of Rescue by the God of Steadfast Love

## Story

Opening Book V—the final section of the Psalter—Psalm 107 answers directly the exile prayer closing Psalm 106. It bursts out with thanksgiving: "Give thanks to the LORD, for He is good; His loving devotion endures forever." Those whom the LORD redeemed from enemy hands and gathered home from east, west, north, and south are summoned to tell their story aloud (107:1–3).

The heart of the psalm paints four vivid scenes of distress, each carrying the exact same twin refrain—"Then they cried out to the LORD in their trouble, and He delivered them from their distress" followed by "Let them give thanks to the LORD for His loving devotion and His wonders to the sons of men."

The first portrait shows travelers lost in barren wasteland, unable to find an inhabited city, fainting from hunger and thirst. When they cry out, God leads them along a straight path to a settled city, satisfying thirsty souls and filling the hungry with good things (107:4–9).

The second shows prisoners sitting in darkness and the shadow of death, bound in iron chains because they rebelled against God's words and despised the counsel of the Most High. Collapsing with no one to help, they cry out—and God brings them out of gloom, snaps their chains, shatters bronze gates, and cuts through iron bars (107:10–16).

The third shows people gravely ill at death's doorstep because of their own foolish, sinful ways, loathing every kind of food. Crying out in affliction, they hear God send out His word to heal them and pull them back from the grave—prompting thank offerings and joyful songs recounting His deeds (107:17–22).

The fourth shows sailors venturing across deep seas for trade. God stirs up a furious gale lifting waves sky-high and plunging ships into troughs until seasoned mariners reel like drunkards and reach their wits' end. When they cry out, "He calmed the storm to a whisper, and the waves of the sea were hushed," guiding them safely into their longed-for harbor (107:23–32).

Following these four scenes, the poem surveys God's sovereign reversals across society and nature. Over human wickedness He turns rivers into desert and fertile fields into salt flats; yet He also turns wasteland into pools and parched ground into flowing springs so the hungry build cities, plant fields, and multiply. He pours contempt on arrogant princes while lifting the needy out of affliction and making their families thrive like flocks (107:33–41).

The final two verses call for reflection: the upright see it and rejoice while every wicked mouth falls shut; whoever is wise will pay close attention and "ponder the loving devotion of the LORD" (107:42–43).

## Key Takeaways

**God rescues both victims of hardship and people suffering self-inflicted ruin.** Among the four portraits, desert wanderers and storm-tossed sailors face raw danger, whereas prisoners and sick fools brought disaster on themselves through rebellion (107:11; 107:17). Yet the instant any of the four cry out, God listens and rescues every single one.

**Gratitude received must be spoken aloud.** Verse 2 urges the redeemed to "say so," and four times the psalm calls for public praise inside the assembly (107:2; 107:8; 107:15; 107:21; 107:31–32). Silent thankfulness is too quiet; telling what God has done strengthens everyone listening.

**True wisdom recognizes God's steadfast love weaving through life's reversals.** Verse 43 teaches that paying thoughtful attention to life's ups and downs leads wise hearts to one lasting realization: the LORD's covenant love stays steadily at work behind every turn (107:43).

## Deeper Meaning

**The One who hushes storm-tossed seas.** Panicked sailors rescued when God stills wind and waves (107:25–30) comes unmistakably alive on the Sea of Galilee when Jesus awakens in a swamped boat, rebukes wind and waves into glass calm, and leaves disciples marveling who He is that wind and sea obey Him (Mark 4:37–41)—a power Psalm 107 assigns solely to the LORD.

**Healer, Liberator, and Guide home.** All four portraits mirror Jesus' earthly mission: proclaiming good news to the poor, freeing captives, healing the sick, and guiding wandering exiles home to God's lasting city (Luke 4:18; Matthew 8:16–17; Hebrews 11:16).

**God's great reversals echoed in Mary's song.** Humbling proud princes while lifting the needy and filling the hungry (107:9; 107:40–41) rings out again in Mary's Magnificat celebrating the coming Savior (Luke 1:52–53).

## Notes & Nuances

**Masterful liturgical refrain structure.** Verses 4 through 32 unfold in four matching stanzas following an identical beat: desperate plight, cry and deliverance (107:6; 107:13; 107:19; 107:28), thanksgiving refrain (107:8; 107:15; 107:21; 107:31), and concrete detail of God's rescue. Worshipers likely sang it antiphonally during post-exilic thanksgiving festivals at the temple.

**Illness in verses 17 and 18 does not claim all sickness comes from personal sin (107:17–18).** Here the psalm pictures one specific group hurting themselves through reckless folly; elsewhere Scripture explicitly rejects equating all suffering with personal guilt (Job 1:8; John 9:2–3).
