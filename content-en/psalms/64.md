# Psalms 64 — Arrows of Wicked Speech and God's Righteous Arrow

## Story

David opens pleading for protection against a stealthy threat. He asks God to hear his complaint, guard his life from dread of the enemy, and hide him from secret plots hatched by evildoers. Their weapons are quieter than battlefield swords—they wield words: sharpening their tongues like blades and aiming bitter words like arrows shot from ambush straight at the innocent. They cheer one another on while laying hidden traps, smugly certain their conspiracy is airtight and no one will ever notice (64:1–6).

Just as the wicked congratulate themselves on their clever plot, a stunning reversal strikes: "But God will shoot them with arrows; suddenly they will be wounded." Their own venomous tongues trip them up and bring them crashing down, leaving every onlooker shaking their head in astonishment (64:7–8).

The closing two verses trace the effect of that judgment on two groups. All humanity will stand in awe, proclaim what God has done, and ponder His deeds. Meanwhile the righteous will rejoice, take refuge in the LORD, and everyone upright in heart will exult in praising Him (64:9–10).

## Key Takeaways

**Slander and whispering behind someone's back wound like ambush arrows.** David pictures bitter words fired from hiding at blameless targets (64:3–4). Damage inflicted by lies and character assassination lingers quietly yet cuts as deep as physical blows.

**No conspiracy stays hidden from God.** Schemers ask "Who can see them?" believing they have covered every track (64:5–6). In reality, the One who sees in secret turns their own malice back onto their heads (64:7–8).

**Sin routinely boomerangs on its maker.** "Their own tongues will bring them to ruin" (64:8) expresses a recurring biblical truth: the snare the wicked set for others ends up trapping their own feet.

## Deeper Meaning

**Being hidden by God from malicious plots brings peace to followers of Christ.** Dread of slander and hidden conspiracies (64:1–2) confronted both Jesus and His apostles repeatedly (Matthew 26:59; Acts 23:12–15). The New Testament assures believers that their life is hidden with Christ in God (Colossians 3:3), beyond the power of any scheme to derail His purpose.

**God exposing secret deeds points ahead to the day all hidden things come into the light.** Evildoers assume nobody sees their plots (64:5), yet Jesus taught that nothing is hidden that will not be disclosed and brought out into the open (Luke 8:17; 1 Corinthians 4:5).

## Notes & Nuances

**The poem creates razor-sharp symmetry by echoing the word *suddenly*.** In verse 4, the wicked *suddenly* ambush the blameless from hiding; in verse 7, God fires back and *suddenly* strikes them down (64:4; 64:7). That verbal mirror highlights how swiftly and decisively God's justice turns the tables.
