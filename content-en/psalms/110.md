# Psalms 110 — The King at God's Right Hand and Priest Forever

## Story

Attributed to David, Psalm 110 runs just seven verses long yet holds two of the loftiest divine oracles in the entire Old Testament. It opens with a heavenly declaration David overhears: "The LORD said to my Lord: 'Sit at My right hand until I make Your enemies a footstool for Your feet.'" Out of Zion the LORD stretches forth His mighty scepter, commanding this King: "Rule in the midst of Your enemies" (110:1–2).

On the day this King musters His power, His people will offer themselves freely; clad in holy splendor, His youthful volunteers appear countless and fresh as morning dew at dawn (110:3).

The second summit is an unbreakable oath sworn by the LORD Himself: "You are a priest forever in the order of Melchizedek." The very King seated at God's right hand is simultaneously installed as eternal Priest belonging to a priesthood higher than Aaron's line (110:4).

The final three verses sweep into decisive triumph. The Lord stands at the King's right hand shattering rival kings in the day of His wrath, judging nations, and crushing rulers across the wide earth. Pausing briefly on the march to drink from a wayside brook, the victorious King lifts His head high in unflagging conquest (110:5–7).

## Key Takeaways

**God's promised King is at once supreme Ruler and eternal Priest.** Under Mosaic law, kingship (tribe of Judah) and priesthood (tribe of Levi) stayed strictly separated—any king presuming to burn incense faced swift judgment (2 Chronicles 26:16–21). Psalm 110 breaks that old boundary by God's own oath, heralding One who unites both offices completely (110:1; 110:4).

**This King's subjects follow willingly, never by coercion.** Unlike earthly empires conscripting soldiers by whip, on His day of power His people volunteer gladly, fresh and radiant as morning dew (110:3).

**The triumph of God's kingdom stands guaranteed by divine oath.** Enemies still resist—the King rules right *in the midst* of His enemies (110:2)—yet the final outcome is settled: every hostile power will end beneath His feet (110:1; 110:5–6).

## Deeper Meaning

**The Old Testament psalm quoted and echoed more often in the New Testament than any other.** Right in the Gospels, Jesus quoted verse 1 to challenge the Pharisees: if the Messiah is merely David's physical descendant, why does David in the Spirit call Him *my Lord*? (110:1; Matthew 22:41–45; Mark 12:35–37). Standing trial before the Sanhedrin, Jesus explicitly testified they would see Him seated at the right hand of the Mighty One (Matthew 26:64).

**Cornerstone of apostolic preaching on Christ's ascension and reign.** In his Pentecost sermon, Peter cited verse 1 to prove Jesus rose, ascended to God's right hand, and reigns as both Lord and Christ (Acts 2:34–36). Paul quoted it regarding the resurrection and Christ placing every foe—death last of all—under His feet (1 Corinthians 15:25–26). Hebrews opens asking to which angel God ever spoke verse 1, because the right-hand throne belongs uniquely to the Son (Hebrews 1:13).

**Melchizedek and Christ's eternal priesthood in Hebrews.** Three full chapters (Hebrews 5–7) unpack verse 4 deeply (110:4). Melchizedek served as both king of Salem and priest of God Most High, blessing Abraham long before Levi's priesthood existed (Genesis 14:18–20). Anchored in God's oath in Psalm 110:4, Hebrews shows Jesus' priesthood surpasses Levi's: never cut short by death, He lives forever to intercede for believers after offering Himself once for all (Hebrews 5:6; 7:17; 7:21–27; 10:12–13).

## Notes & Nuances

**Why does verse 1 address the royal figure as *my Lord*?** In Hebrew, the first title is *YHWH* (the LORD), while the second is *Adoni* (my lord—a respectful address to a superior or sovereign). Since King David himself authored the psalm yet calls this figure *my Lord*, he points to a King towering far above David himself.

**Compressed poetic phrasing in verses 3 and 7 (110:3; 110:7).** Verse 3's dense Hebrew can picture either a vibrant army of youthful volunteers fresh as dew or the King Himself possessing eternal dew-fresh vigor. Verse 7's drink from a roadside stream depicts an unstoppable champion pausing only an instant before lifting His head in complete victory.
