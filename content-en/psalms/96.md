# Psalms 96 — Singing a New Song That the LORD Reigns

## Story

Continuing the *The LORD Reigns* group (Psalms 93–99), Psalm 96 opens calling the entire earth to sing a "new song" to the LORD. God's people do not keep praise indoors—they are sent out proclaiming His salvation day after day, declaring His glory and marvelous deeds among every nation and people (96:1–3).

The reason rings loud and clear: the LORD is great and far more worthy of awe than all other gods. All the gods of the nations are worthless idols, whereas the LORD made the heavens; splendor, majesty, strength, and beauty fill His sanctuary (96:4–6).

Accordingly, the psalmist invites all families of the nations to ascribe glory to the LORD's name, bring an offering into His courts, and worship Him in the splendor of holiness. Then the central proclamation rings across the world: "The LORD reigns!" The world stands firmly established and immovable, and He will judge the peoples with equity (96:7–10).

In the finale, all creation joins one massive choir: let the heavens rejoice, earth be glad, the sea resound, fields jubilate, and forest trees sing for joy before the LORD—"for He is coming—He is coming to judge the earth" in righteousness and truth (96:11–13).

## Key Takeaways

**True worship always fuels global mission.** Singing praise inside the temple and declaring God's salvation among every nation are inseparable halves of one calling (96:1–3; 96:7–10).

**The Maker of the heavens stands infinitely above handmade idols.** Every rival deity is an empty nothing; only the Creator of the skies deserves worldwide adoration (96:5).

**God's coming judgment is joyful news for creation.** Nature does not cower at God's arrival—it erupts in song, because His righteous rule heals a world scarred by sin and injustice (96:11–13).

## Deeper Meaning

**The *new song* and global proclamation culminate in the gospel.** Announcing salvation among all nations and welcoming every family of earth into God's courts (96:2–3; 96:7–8) leads straight to Jesus' Great Commission sending disciples to all nations (Matthew 28:19). In Revelation, redeemed believers from every tribe, tongue, people, and nation sing a *new song* before the Lamb's throne (Revelation 5:9).

**Creation rejoicing at God's coming echoes in Romans 8.** Skies, seas, fields, and trees bursting with joy at the LORD's righteous arrival (96:11–13) mirror creation groaning in eager expectation for liberation from bondage to decay (Romans 8:19–22).

## Notes & Nuances

**Close tie to 1 Chronicles 16.** Nearly all of Psalm 96 appears verbatim in the thanksgiving song King David handed Asaph when bringing the ark of the covenant into Jerusalem (1 Chronicles 16:23–33)—grounding this timeless missionary hymn right inside Israel's joyous worship history.

**Worshiping *in the splendor of holiness* (96:9).** The Hebrew phrase carries two equally rich nuances: approaching God clad in *holy beauty* of heart and sacred attire, or *trembling in awe* before the LORD's holy majesty.
