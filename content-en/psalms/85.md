# Psalms 85 — Where Mercy and Truth Meet Together

## Story

This psalm of the sons of Korah opens giving thanks for past deliverance: the LORD showed favor to His land, restored the fortunes of Jacob, forgave the iniquity of His people, covered all their sins, and turned back His fierce anger (85:1–3).

Yet present conditions still fall short of full peace, so at verse 4 the tone turns into earnest pleading: restore us completely, O God of our salvation, and lay aside Your displeasure. Asking whether God's anger will linger forever, the singer voices a celebrated prayer for revival: "Will You not revive us again, that Your people may rejoice in You?" He begs God to show His steadfast love and grant His salvation (85:4–7).

At verse 8, the poet falls quiet to listen for heaven's answer. He is sure the LORD will speak peace to His faithful people—paired with a sober caution: "but let them not return to folly." Salvation stands very near to those who fear Him, so glory may dwell once more in their land (85:8–9).

The song closes with a luminous vision of complete restoration: "Loving devotion and faithfulness have joined together; righteousness and peace have kissed." Truth springs up from the earth while righteousness looks down from heaven; the LORD gives what is good so the land yields its harvest, and righteousness marches ahead preparing the path for His footsteps (85:10–13).

## Key Takeaways

**Past redemption gives us courage to pray for fresh renewal today.** God's people had come home yet still wrestled with lingering hardship; recalling past pardon emboldens them to ask Him to revive them again (85:1–7).

**True revival always awakens joy in God Himself.** Praying to be revived is not merely wanting relief from hardship or poverty, but "that Your people may rejoice in You" (85:6).

**God's peace calls us not to slide back into old folly.** God eagerly forgives and speaks peace, yet lasting renewal requires revering Him and leaving former sinful habits behind (85:8–9).

**In God, mercy and justice never cancel each other out.** Human beings often bend justice to show leniency, or sacrifice compassion to enforce rules; in God's salvation, all four virtues—mercy, truth, righteousness, and peace—meet and embrace completely (85:10).

## Deeper Meaning

**Mercy and righteousness kissed completely at the cross of Jesus.** Verse 10 ranks among the deepest lines of the Old Testament (85:10). How can God pardon guilty sinners while upholding spotless truth and justice? The New Testament answers clearly: through Christ's atoning death, God displays both His mercy and His righteousness so that He is at once just and the justifier of those who trust in Jesus (Romans 3:25–26).

**Glory dwelling in the land points forward to the Incarnation.** The hope that "glory may dwell in our land" (85:9) where steadfast love and faithfulness meet (85:10) echoes in John's prologue: the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, full of grace and truth, and we beheld His glory (John 1:14).

## Notes & Nuances

**Post-exilic setting.** Verse 1 looks back to God restoring "the fortunes of Jacob," yet verses 4 through 7 still plead for revival and fruitful harvests (85:1; 85:4–7; 85:12). This fits closely the era right after Jewish exiles returned from Babylon to Jerusalem: freed to return home, yet facing ruined walls, poor crops, and low morale (cf. Haggai 1:6–11; Nehemiah 1:3).

**Vivid personification in verses 10 through 13 (85:10–13).** The poet brings four divine virtues—mercy, truth, righteousness, and peace—to life as messengers meeting, kissing, sprouting from soil, and gazing down from heaven, painting total harmony restored between heaven and earth.
