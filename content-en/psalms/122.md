# Psalms 122 — The Joy of Stepping Inside Jerusalem's Gates

## Story

Psalm 122 carries the heading of a Song of Ascents of David, marking the moment the pilgrim finally arrives at his destination. The poem bursts with gladness from its very first line: "I was glad when they said to me, 'Let us go to the house of the LORD.'" Now, after a long journey, their feet are standing right inside the gates of Jerusalem (122:1–2).

Gazing at the capital, the psalmist marvels at a city firmly built and closely bound together. Here all the tribes of Israel gather as decreed to give thanks to the name of the LORD, and here stand the thrones of righteous judgment belonging to the house of David (122:3–5).

From admiring the city, the song turns into an earnest prayer for Jerusalem's peace—a graceful wordplay, since the name *Jerusalem* echoes the word *shalom* (peace): "Pray for the peace of Jerusalem," wishing prosperity upon all who love her and praying for security within her walls and citadels. The poet gives two reasons for his love and blessing upon the city: first, for the sake of his brothers and companions there ("Peace be within you"); second, for the sake of the house of the LORD that dwells in her midst (122:6–9).

## Key Takeaways

**Shared worship is not a heavy duty, but the joy of drawing near to God together.** Verse 1 reveals the power of a community's invitation: hearing others invite him to go to God's house does not make the psalmist reluctant—it fills his heart with gladness (122:1).

**Peace and justice belong together with true worship.** Jerusalem is lovely not merely for her sturdy walls, but because the tribes unite there to thank God and because thrones of fair judgment stand within her (122:3–5). Without justice and brotherly love, there can be no genuine peace (122:6–8).

## Deeper Meaning

**From the earthly Jerusalem to the heavenly Jerusalem.** Just as Old Testament pilgrims rejoiced to stand inside the gates of Jerusalem where the temple and David's thrones stood (122:2; 122:5), the New Testament declares that believers in Christ have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem (Hebrews 12:22; Revelation 21:2).

**Praying for the peace of the Church for the sake of brothers and God's house.** The two motives in verses 8–9—the sake of brothers and friends, and the sake of the house of God (122:8–9)—mirror the very spirit of pursuing unity and peace in the Church to which the New Testament calls us (Ephesians 4:3; Romans 14:19).

## Notes & Nuances

**Wordplay on the *s-l-m* sound in Hebrew.** In verses 6–8, the Hebrew consonants *s-l-m* chime repeatedly through *Yerushalayim* (Jerusalem), *sha'alu* (pray/ask), *shalom* (peace), and *yishlayu* (prosper/be secure), creating a gentle, soothing cadence that sounds like the very peace it prays for.
