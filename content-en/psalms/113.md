# Psalms 113 — High Above the Heavens, Stooping to Lift the Lowly

## Story

Opening the *Hallel* collection (Psalms 113–118), Psalm 113 rings with buoyant summons: let every servant of the LORD praise His name from now until forevermore, unbound by clock or border—"From where the sun rises to where it sets, the name of the LORD is praised!" (113:1–3).

The middle three verses marvel at God's incomparable grandeur: exalted above every nation, His glory towering above the skies—"Who is like the LORD our God?" Enthroned so immeasurably high that He must stoop down simply to look upon both heaven and earth (113:4–6).

The closing three verses reveal what makes this God truly unmatched: towering height never makes Him cold toward the small. "He raises the poor from the dust," lifting the needy straight out of the ash heap to seat them beside princes of His people. He tenderly settles the barren woman in her home, turning social shame into the radiant joy of a mother surrounded by children. The hymn closes with a ringing *Hallelujah* (113:7–9).

## Key Takeaways

**God's true greatness shines where supreme height meets tender stooping.** Many pagan myths imagined exalted gods as aloof, or approachable gods as petty. The God of Psalm 113 towers above the skies yet bends right down into dust and ash heaps to lift the poor (113:4–7).

**God notices people society shoves to the margins.** A beggar sitting on an ash heap and a childless woman stood as twin emblems of social invisibility in the ancient world; the LORD steps in personally to transform their stories (113:7–9).

## Deeper Meaning

**Opening hymn of Passover sung by Jesus Himself.** In Jewish tradition, the *Hallel* (Psalms 113–118) graced the great annual feasts, especially Passover—with Psalms 113–114 sung before the meal and Psalms 115–118 afterward. On the night before the cross, after the Last Supper, Jesus and His disciples sang a hymn and went out to the Mount of Olives (Matthew 26:30)—almost certainly drawing from this very *Hallel*.

**Echoes linking Hannah's song to Mary's song.** Verses 7 and 8 echo Hannah's thanksgiving prayer almost word for word after God gave her Samuel (113:7–8; 1 Samuel 2:8). Centuries later, Mary sang the exact same melody upon learning she would bear the Savior: God looked on the humble state of His servant and exalted the lowly (Luke 1:48; 1:52).

**The Most High stooping deepest in the Incarnation.** God bending down to survey earth and lift the needy from dust (113:6–7) reached its absolute summit when Jesus emptied Himself, took the form of a servant, and died on a cross so sinners could be lifted up as God's own children (Philippians 2:6–8; 2 Corinthians 8:9).

## Notes & Nuances

**Opening the *Egyptian Hallel* (Psalms 113–118).** These six songs are titled *Hallel* (meaning *praise*) or the *Egyptian Hallel* for celebrating deliverance from Egypt—highlighted dramatically in Psalm 114 next door—and anchored Jewish celebrations of Passover, Pentecost, and Tabernacles.
