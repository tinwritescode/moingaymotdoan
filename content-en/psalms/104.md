# Psalms 104 — Song to the Creator Who Sustains All Life

## Story

Psalm 104 opens with the exact same self-summons as Psalm 103—"Bless the LORD, O my soul!"—yet where Psalm 103 celebrated redeeming grace, Psalm 104 lifts its eyes across the whole universe, marveling at the Creator clothed in splendor and majesty (104:1).

The poet paints sky and waters in breathtaking strokes: God wraps Himself in light like a cloak, stretches out the heavens like a tent curtain, lays the beams of His upper chambers on the waters, makes clouds His chariot, rides on the wings of the wind, and sends winds as messengers and flames of fire as attendants (104:2–4).

Next he recalls how God set earth firmly on its foundations. At first deep waters blanketed everything above the mountains; at God's rebuke and thunderous voice, the waters fled, mountains rose, valleys sank down, and God set an uncrossable boundary so the sea would never drown the earth again (104:5–9).

From awesome power, the scene warms into tender daily care. God sends springs gushing through valleys so wild animals and wild donkeys drink their fill while birds sing from branches overhead. Watering mountains from His upper chambers, He brings forth grass for cattle and plants for humanity: wine to gladden the heart, oil to make the face shine, and bread to sustain strength. Sap-filled cedars of Lebanon cradle nesting birds, cypresses house storks, high crags shelter mountain goats, and rocky cliffs hide rock badgers (104:10–18).

God also choreographs day and night. The moon marks the seasons and the sun knows its exact hour to set. Darkness falls and forest beasts prowl out, young lions roaring for prey as though asking God for their food. Sunrise sends them creeping back to their dens, clearing the daylight for humanity to step out to work until evening (104:19–23).

Overcome with awe, the poet bursts out admiring God's boundless wisdom filling the earth, then turns toward the vast ocean teeming with creatures large and small—where ships sail past and even mighty sea monster *Leviathan* is merely a playful creature God shaped to frolic in the waves (104:24–26).

Every creature looks expectantly to God for its meal in due time. When He opens His hand, they feast on good things; when He hides His face, they are dismayed; when He withdraws their breath, they die and return to dust; when He sends forth His Spirit, new life is created and the face of the earth is renewed (104:27–30).

The song ends in radiant blessing: may the LORD's glory endure forever and may He rejoice in His works—the One whose glance makes earth tremble and whose touch makes mountains smoke. Vowing to sing to God all his life, the poet adds one closing plea that sinners and the wicked vanish from this beautiful earth, before repeating his opening chorus: "Bless the LORD, O my soul! Hallelujah!" (104:31–35).

## Key Takeaways

**God did not build the universe and walk away—He actively sustains it every moment.** From valley springs for wild donkeys to lions' prey, from every breath drawn to renewing earth's surface season after season, all life depends directly on God's open hand (104:10–14; 104:27–30).

**Creation was designed not merely for bare survival, but for delight.** God supplies not only bread for energy, but wine that gladdens hearts and oil that makes faces glow; He crafts vast seas for creatures to play in and rejoices in His own handiwork (104:15; 104:26; 104:31).

**Nature is God's artwork, never rival gods to worship.** Ancient Near Eastern religions deified sun, moon, wind, fire, and sea, while dreading *Leviathan* as a chaos monster. Psalm 104 puts every single one in its rightful spot as obedient servant or playful creature in the LORD's hand (104:3–4; 104:19; 104:26).

## Deeper Meaning

**A lyrical twin to Genesis 1.** The psalm's imagery walks closely along the six days of creation: light and sky (104:2–4), separating sea and dry land (104:5–9), plants and trees (104:14–16), moon and sun (104:19), sea life, land animals, and humanity (104:20–26; cf. Genesis 1:3–31). Where Genesis proclaims creation in stately prose, Psalm 104 sings it in delighted wonder.

**Making winds His messengers and fire His servants.** Hebrews quotes verse 4 to highlight angels' role as ministering servants in sharp contrast to God's Son seated on His everlasting throne (104:4; Hebrews 1:7–8).

**The open hand feeding birds and the Spirit renewing the earth.** God feeding creatures right on time (104:27–28) echoes Jesus pointing disciples to birds that neither sow nor reap yet are fed by our heavenly Father (Matthew 6:26). And God's Spirit renewing the face of the earth (104:30) anticipates the new heavens and new earth where no sin or wickedness mars His good creation again (104:35; Revelation 21:1–5).

## Notes & Nuances

**Praying that sinners vanish in verse 35 fits the poem's beauty rather than clashing with it (104:35).** After thirty-four verses celebrating harmonious, generously cared-for creation, verse 35 names the sole discordant note spoiling that masterpiece—human wickedness. Longing for evil to disappear means longing for creation's original wholeness restored.

**The very first *Hallelujah* in the Psalter.** In Hebrew, *Hallelujah* means *Praise the LORD*. Appearing here for the first time at verse 35, that jubilant shout rings out again and again across the closing stretch of the Psalms.

**Parallels with an ancient Egyptian sun hymn.** Many scholars notice striking similarities between verses 20 through 26 and Pharaoh Akhenaten's fourteenth-century BC *Hymn to the Aten* (nighttime predators emerging, daytime human work, ships sailing seas). If Israel's poet knew that hymn, he deliberately redirected every shred of praise away from the sun disc to the Creator who made the sun and tells it when to set (104:19).
