# Psalms 100 — Enter His Gates with Thanksgiving

## Story

Just five verses long—and the only psalm specifically titled *A Psalm of Thanksgiving*—Psalm 100 crowns the royal-kingship psalms with seven rapid, joyful invitations. All the earth is summoned to shout joyfully to the LORD, serve Him with gladness, and come into His presence with singing (100:1–2).

Right in the center lies the sturdy bedrock of that gladness: "Know that the LORD is God. It is He who made us, and we are His; we are His people, and the sheep of His pasture" (100:3).

The closing two verses welcome everyone into the temple with grateful hearts: "Enter His gates with thanksgiving and His courts with praise." Gratitude and blessing rest not on fleeting moods, but on three unchanging truths about God's nature: "For the LORD is good, and His loving devotion endures forever; His faithfulness continues to all generations" (100:4–5).

## Key Takeaways

**Serving God belongs to gladness, never reluctant drudgery.** The call to "serve the LORD with gladness" reminds us God seeks not fearful slaves checking off chores, but joyful children delighting to serve their Father (100:2).

**Knowing we belong to God cures deep insecurity.** Verse 3 answers the ultimate identity question *Who am I?*: we did not invent ourselves; God made us, owns us, and cares for us as the sheep of His pasture (100:3).

**Thanksgiving is the doorway into God's presence.** Entering His gates with gratitude lifts our gaze off minor lacks onto His enduring goodness, steadfast love, and lifelong faithfulness (100:4–5).

## Deeper Meaning

**The entire earth becomes one flock under one Shepherd.** Opening with *all the earth* (100:1) and calling worshipers *the sheep of His pasture* (100:3), Psalm 100 points directly to Jesus—the Good Shepherd who lays down His life for His sheep and declares He has other sheep beyond Israel's fold whom He will gather into one flock under one Shepherd (John 10:11; 10:16).

**Unchanging goodness and faithfulness endure forever.** Verse 5's celebration of steadfast love lasting across every generation (100:5) carries directly into the New Testament assurance that Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever (Hebrews 13:8).

## Notes & Nuances

**Two perfectly balanced movements.** Compact as it is, Psalm 100 unfolds in twin matching stanzas: the first (100:1–3) pairs calls to joyful worship with theological grounds (He is God, Creator, and Shepherd); the second (100:4–5) pairs calls to enter with thanks with God's eternal character (good, loving, faithful forever).

**A charming textual nuance in verse 3 (100:3).** Ancient Hebrew manuscripts preserve two identically pronounced words (*lo*): one reads *not we ourselves* ("It is He who made us, and not we ourselves"), and the other reads *His* ("and we are His"). Both truths complement each other seamlessly: we are not self-made, and we belong wholly to our Maker.
