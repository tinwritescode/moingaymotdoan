# Psalms 3 — Sleeping Safely While Surrounded

## Story

The traditional superscription links this psalm to the time David fled from his own son Absalom (2 Samuel 15:13–30). David opens with an alarmed cry: his foes are multiplying, and countless people are rising up against him (3:1). Even more painful is the taunt ringing in his ears: people are saying there is no salvation for him in God (3:2).

Yet right at verse 3, the poem pivots to steady trust. David calls the LORD a shield around him, his glory, and the One who lifts his head (3:3). He recalls his own lived experience: whenever he cries aloud, the LORD answers him from His holy mountain (3:4).

Out of that confidence comes something remarkable right in the middle of danger: he lies down, falls asleep, and wakes up safely, because the LORD sustains him (3:5). Even with tens of thousands drawn up against him on every side, he refuses to be afraid (3:6).

In the closing lines, David pleads with God to arise and save him, because in the past He has struck his enemies and broken the teeth of the wicked (3:7). The poem finishes with a confession that rises above the king's personal crisis—"Salvation belongs to the LORD"—followed by a prayer of blessing upon all God's people (3:8).

## Key Takeaways

**The sharpest pain in trouble is the feeling that God has abandoned you.** David's enemies are not merely numerous; what cuts deepest is their claim that God will no longer come to his rescue (3:1–2).

**Real peace does not wait until every enemy disappears.** David does not wait for the siege to lift before getting rest. He lies down and sleeps right through a dangerous night, convinced that God is keeping watch over him (3:5–6).

**Genuine faith still remembers others.** Even while pursued by his own son and deserted by much of the nation, David ends his prayer by asking God's blessing upon all His people (3:8).

## Deeper Meaning

**Psalm 3 is the first personal prayer of lament in the Book of Psalms.** After two introductory chapters setting out big themes, Psalm 3 steps into the real life of faith—which is not always smooth, and often involves betrayal and encirclement.

**The confession "Salvation belongs to the LORD" becomes a golden thread across Scripture.** Jonah later repeats nearly the exact same sentence from inside the great fish (Jonah 2:9), and the vast multitude in heaven in Revelation proclaims that salvation belongs to our God and to the Lamb (Revelation 7:10).

**Peaceful sleep in the midst of danger foreshadows Jesus Himself.** On the Sea of Galilee, while furious waves swamped the boat and the disciples panicked, Jesus slept calmly in the stern (Mark 4:37–38). David's quiet rest and Jesus' unshakable peace flow from the same source: complete trust in the heavenly Father.

## Notes & Nuances

**The phrase *lifts my head* in verse 3 carries a vivid historical picture.** When David fled Jerusalem, he walked barefoot, weeping, with his head covered in grief (2 Samuel 15:30). For God to lift his head means both emotional comfort and the restoration of honor to a humiliated king (3:3).

**The plea to *strike the jaw* and *break the teeth* of the wicked portrays enemies like wild predators (3:7).** In Old Testament poetry, breaking a lion's or beast's teeth means stripping violent oppressors of their power to harm, not endorsing personal vengeance.

**Psalm 3 is traditionally known as a morning prayer.** Because verse 5 says, "I lie down and sleep; I wake again" (3:5), tradition reads Psalm 3 upon waking, paired with Psalm 4 as an evening prayer (Psalms 4:8).
