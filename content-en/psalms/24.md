# Psalms 24 — Open the Gates for the King of Glory

## Story

Psalm 24 reads like a grand antiphonal processional hymn—likely sung as the ark of the covenant was carried up into Jerusalem (2 Samuel 6). Its opening two verses sound a cosmic declaration: "The earth is the LORD's, and the fullness thereof, the world and all who dwell therein," for He founded it firmly upon the seas and established it upon the waters (24:1–2).

Halting at the foot of the holy hill, the procession asks: "Who may ascend the hill of the LORD? Who may stand in His holy place?" (24:3). The reply demands integrity inside and out: the one with "clean hands and a pure heart," who does not lift his soul to worthless idols or swear deceitfully (24:4). That worshiper will receive blessing and righteousness from the God of his salvation—such is the true generation of those who seek His face (24:5–6).

The final four verses erupt into a stirring call-and-response before the ancient city gates. Voices ring out: "Lift up your heads, O gates! Be lifted up, O ancient doors, that the King of glory may come in!" (24:7). From within the gateway echoes the question: "Who is this King of glory?" And the procession answers in two rising crescendos: it is the LORD, strong and mighty in battle, the LORD of Hosts—He alone is the King of glory (24:8–10).

## Key Takeaways

**Everything on earth belongs to the Creator.** Human beings are guests and stewards inside the LORD's world, so genuine worship always begins by acknowledging His supreme ownership (24:1–2).

**True worship unites outward conduct with inward purity.** "Clean hands" point to upright actions, while a "pure heart" points to honest motives—having one without the other is never enough (24:4).

**The righteousness of those who seek God is ultimately God's own gift.** Verse 5 states clearly that the worshiper ascending the hill "will receive blessing from the LORD and vindication from the God of his salvation" (24:5).

## Deeper Meaning

**The apostle Paul quotes verse 1 verbatim in 1 Corinthians to teach Christian freedom and gratitude (24:1).** Discussing everyday food, Paul reminds believers that the earth is the Lord's and everything in it, so every good gift of creation may be received with thankfulness (1 Corinthians 10:26).

**The triumphant entry of the "King of glory" has long been read by the church in light of Jesus' ascension.** Having conquered sin and death at the cross (Psalm 22) and risen as the Good Shepherd (Psalm 23), Christ alone—the One with utterly clean hands and pure heart—ascended to the Father's right hand as the King of glory (Acts 1:9; 1 Corinthians 2:8).

## Notes & Nuances

**Psalms 22, 23, and 24 form a celebrated trilogy noted by many interpreters.** Psalm 22 portrays the cross (past redemption), Psalm 23 portrays the Shepherd's present care, and Psalm 24 unveils the crown and royal majesty of the coming King.

**The *ancient doors* originally referred to the old stone gateways of Jerusalem (24:7; 24:9).** Personifying the gates and telling them to *lift up their heads* conveys royal awe—as though the city's arches stood too low for the towering majesty of the King about to enter.
