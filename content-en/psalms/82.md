# Psalms 82 — Putting Unjust Rulers on Trial

## Story

Asaph's brief, razor-sharp poem opens like a heavenly tribunal. God takes His stand in the great assembly and renders judgment among the *gods*—rulers and judges commissioned to uphold His justice on earth. His opening question strikes straight at their conscience: how long will you judge unjustly and show partiality to the wicked? (82:1–2).

Next comes the exact job description God requires of every ruler: defend the weak and the fatherless, uphold the rights of the afflicted and oppressed, and rescue the poor and needy from the grip of the wicked (82:3–4).

Instead, these officials remain blind—knowing nothing, understanding nothing, stumbling around in darkness until "all the foundations of the earth are shaken" (82:5). Therefore God pronounces verdict: although He once called them "gods" and "sons of the Most High" because of their exalted office, they will die like ordinary mortals and fall like every other prince (82:6–7).

The closing verse voices Asaph's crisp prayer to the sole true Judge: "Arise, O God, judge the earth, for all the nations are Your inheritance" (82:8).

## Key Takeaways

**Every earthly ruler answers to God's courtroom.** No matter how lofty a leader's seat appears, a higher court watches overhead evaluating how power gets used (82:1–2).

**Justice is measured by how a society treats its most vulnerable.** God evaluates governments not by grand monuments or GDP, but by whether orphans, the poor, and the helpless find real protection from exploiters (82:3–4).

**Injustice shakes the bedrock of the whole world.** When courts grow crooked, not only do the poor suffer—the entire moral fabric of society cracks from underneath (82:5).

**High status saves no one from mortality or accountability.** Earthly authority is temporary; corrupt power brokers fall and die like any ordinary mortal (82:6–7).

## Deeper Meaning

**Jesus quoted verse 6 directly when challenged by Jewish opponents (82:6).** Accused of blasphemy for calling Himself the Son of God, Jesus cited this very line—"I have said, 'You are gods'"—to answer them (John 10:34–36). His logic was airtight: if Scripture itself called human recipients of God's word *gods* as His representatives, how much more rightful is it for the One set apart and sent into the world by the Father to call Himself God's Son!

**The righteous Judge of all nations is Christ Himself.** Praying for God to arise, judge the earth, and claim all nations as His inheritance (82:8) echoes the Father's promise giving the nations to His Son (Psalms 2:8) and anticipates the day Jesus judges the world in flawless righteousness (Acts 17:31).

## Notes & Nuances

**Who are the *gods* in verses 1 and 6 (82:1; 82:6)?** The Hebrew word *elohim* can mean God, heavenly beings, or human judges acting as God's representatives (cf. Exodus 21:6; 22:8–9). Many interpreters view this psalm as addressing earthly kings and judges directly—matching both Jesus' citation (John 10:35) and the verdict that they will "die like men" (82:7). Other modern scholars see a poetic scene of a heavenly council of spiritual beings assigned over nations, proclaiming God's supremacy over every unseen power. Either way, the core message shines unambiguously: God condemns oppression and stands as sole rightful Judge of all peoples.
