# Psalms 15 — Who May Enter God's House?

## Story

David opens this compact five-verse psalm with two parallel questions: "O LORD, who may abide in Your tent? Who may dwell on Your holy mountain?" (15:1).

The rest of the poem delivers a strikingly concrete answer—saying nothing about elaborate rituals and going straight into everyday character. The one who may dwell near God walks blamelessly, does what is right, and speaks truth from the heart (15:2). On the tongue, this person does not slander, does no harm to a neighbor, and casts no slur against a friend (15:3).

In relationships and commitments, this person despises vile behavior yet honors those who fear the LORD, keeping an oath even when it hurts (15:4). With money, this person refuses to lend at exploitative interest to the poor or accept a bribe against the innocent. The poem ends with a sturdy promise: "He who does these things will never be shaken" (15:5).

## Key Takeaways

**True worship can never be separated from everyday integrity.** Approaching God's sanctuary means proving our reverence for Him in our speech, our promises, and how we handle money with our neighbors (15:1–5).

**Truthfulness begins on the inside.** Verse 2 demands more than technically accurate words; it requires one who "speaks truth from his heart"—free of self-deception and double-mindedness (15:2).

**Keeping your word when it costs you measures true character.** Anyone can keep a convenient promise, but the person who belongs to God keeps an oath "even when it hurts" and does not change (15:4).

## Deeper Meaning

**The high standard of Psalm 15 exposes human imperfection and points straight to Christ.** Measured against every line—never slandering, always truthful in heart, never breaking a vow—no human being meets the mark completely except Jesus. Through His flawless righteousness, believers are granted open access into the Most Holy Place (Hebrews 10:19–22).

**The closing promise "will never be shaken" echoes in the Sermon on the Mount and in 2 Peter (15:5).** Jesus likens the one who hears and obeys His words to a builder setting a house on solid rock that no storm can topple (Matthew 7:24–25), while Peter urges believers to cultivate virtue so they will never stumble and will receive a rich welcome into the eternal kingdom (2 Peter 1:5–11).

## Notes & Nuances

**Psalm 15 belongs to the genre of *entrance liturgies*, much like the question-and-answer exchange later in the Psalter (Psalms 24:3–6).** Scholars suggest that as pilgrims climbed Mount Zion, they sang the question in verse 1 and priests answered with the ethical qualifications of verses 2 through 5 (15:1–5).

**The ban on lending money *at interest* stems from Mosaic laws protecting the poor within Israel (15:5; Exodus 22:25; Leviticus 25:35–37).** In the ancient world, borrowing occurred mainly when impoverished families hit rock bottom; profiting off their desperation was considered predatory injustice.
