# Psalms 75 — It Is God Who Judges, Bringing One Down and Exalting Another

## Story

This crisp ten-verse psalm arrives like an authoritative answer to the anguished "How long?" of Psalm 74 right before it. The congregation opens in unified gratitude, giving thanks to God because His name is near and people are recounting His wondrous deeds (75:1).

At once God's own voice rings out in a solemn oracle: "When I choose a time, I will judge fairly." Even if the earth and all its inhabitants dissolve in upheaval, God Himself holds its pillars steady (75:2–3).

Next comes a blunt warning to the arrogant and wicked: stop boasting, stop lifting up your horn high, stop speaking with a stiff neck. Promotion and real authority come neither from the east, nor the west, nor the desert south—God alone decides: "It is God who judges: He brings one down and exalts another." In the LORD's hand sits a foaming cup of spiced judgment wine; when He pours it out, every wicked person on earth must drain it right down to the dregs (75:4–8).

The final two verses shift to the psalmist's—or representative king's—resolute pledge: he will proclaim and sing praise forever to the God of Jacob, carrying out justice to cut off every horn of the wicked while lifting high the horns of the righteous (75:9–10).

## Key Takeaways

**God has an *appointed time* to judge—He neither forgets nor panics.** Watching evil strut unchecked makes God seem slow, yet verse 2 assures us He acts right on His chosen schedule with flawless equity (75:2).

**Every earthly office and honor comes from God—and returns to Him.** Nobody keeps themselves on top forever; "He brings one down and exalts another" (75:7). Remembering that keeps leaders humble and gives the oppressed quiet hope.

**Amid a trembling world, God holds earth's pillars steady.** Even when social order looks ready to melt away (75:3), creation never falls apart because the Creator's own hand braces its foundations.

## Deeper Meaning

**The *cup* of God's wrath runs from Old Testament prophecy into the New Testament.** The foaming cup of judgment awaiting evildoers (75:8) recurs across the prophets (Isaiah 51:17; Jeremiah 25:15) and in Revelation (Revelation 14:10; 16:19). Yet the Bible's most staggering good news unfolds in Gethsemane: Jesus voluntarily drank that cup of wrath down to the dregs in place of sinful humanity at the cross, so everyone who trusts Him receives forgiveness and the cup of salvation (Matthew 26:39; John 18:11).

**God humbling the proud and lifting the humble resounds in Mary's song.** The truth that God "brings one down and exalts another" (75:7) echoes directly in Mary's Magnificat when carrying Jesus: "He has brought down rulers from their thrones, but has exalted the humble" (Luke 1:52; James 4:6).

## Notes & Nuances

**The *horn* in verses 4–5 and 10 is a standard biblical emblem of strength and power (75:4–5; 75:10).** Picturing a strong wild ox tossing its horns high signified proud might. "Lifting up your horn" means arrogant strutting, while "cutting off all the horns of the wicked" means completely dismantling oppressive power (1 Samuel 2:10).

**Why does verse 6 mention east, west, and south without naming the north (75:6)?** Many commentators note that north of Judah lay both Mount Zion (or the highway of invading empires like Assyria and Babylon); poetically leaving out one compass point before immediately answering in verse 7 that promotion comes from God above spotlights heaven—not any earthly quarter—as the sole source of authority.
