# Psalms 87 — Zion, Homeland of All Nations

## Story

This brief, startling song of the sons of Korah opens celebrating Jerusalem: the LORD founded her upon holy mountains, loving the gates of Zion more than all the other dwellings of Jacob. Glorious things are spoken of the city of God (87:1–3).

Then comes the poem's jaw-dropping surprise—spoken by God Himself. He calls the roll of historic Gentile nations, including Israel's fiercest longtime adversaries: Rahab (a poetic name for Egypt), Babylon, Philistia, Tyre, and Cush. Yet God counts them among those who know Him and declares something astonishing: "This one was born in Zion" (87:4).

When the LORD opens His celestial registry of the peoples, the Most High Himself will establish Zion and record in heaven's census that citizens from every one of those nations count as native-born children of Zion. The poem ends amid music and dancing: "All my springs are in you" (87:5–7).

## Key Takeaways

**God loves Zion not to wall off the world, but to welcome all nations in.** Jerusalem was established not as a closed ethnic fortress, but as the gathering home for every people coming to know God (87:1–4).

**God's grace turns former enemies into native-born citizens.** Egypt and Babylon once enslaved Israel and Philistia fought her for generations, yet God enters their names on Zion's birth register—not as second-class newcomers, but as full native children (87:4–6).

**Every true spring of life and joy rises in God's city.** The closing song declares that wherever God dwells lies the fountain sustaining every soul (87:7).

## Deeper Meaning

**Zion's citizenship roll foreshadows the worldwide church.** Psalm 87's astonishing vision stretches far beyond ancient Israel's borders: people from every nation can be *born in Zion*. In the New Testament, Paul calls the Jerusalem above *our mother* (Galatians 4:26), and Hebrews tells believers from every nation that they have come to Mount Zion, the city of the living God, where the assembly of the firstborn is enrolled in heaven (Hebrews 12:22–23).

**New birth in Christ fulfills being *born in Zion*.** The way someone from Babylon or Cush becomes a native child of Zion is through being born again by the Spirit (John 3:3–5)—vividly pictured when the Ethiopian official believes in Jesus and enters God's family (Acts 8:27–38).

## Notes & Nuances

**Who is *Rahab* in verse 4 (87:4)?** Not Rahab of Jericho, but a poetic nickname for Egypt evoking a proud sea monster (cf. Isaiah 30:7; Psalms 89:10). Naming Rahab alongside Babylon spans both great superpowers—west and east—that once dominated Israel.
