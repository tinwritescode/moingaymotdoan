# Psalms 12 — When Lies Reign Supreme

## Story

David opens with a brief yet piercing cry for help: the godly are gone, and the faithful seem to have vanished from among humanity (12:1). Everywhere around him, neighbors lie to one another with flattering lips and double hearts (12:2).

He asks the LORD to cut off every flattering lip and boastful tongue—those who brag that by their own tongues they will prevail, claiming, "We own our lips—who can be our master?" (12:3–4).

Into that suffocating atmosphere, the LORD Himself speaks at verse 5. Seeing the poor plundered and hearing the groaning of the needy, He announces that He will arise right now to place them in the safety for which they long (12:5). Immediately David contrasts that divine word with human falsehood: the words of the LORD are completely flawless, like silver refined seven times with every trace of impurity removed (12:6). Even though the wicked still strut freely on every side whenever vileness is exalted, David trusts that God will guard the needy from this generation forever (12:7–8).

## Key Takeaways

**When truth decays, speech becomes an instrument of oppression.** Arrogant people wield flattery and deceit, boasting "We own our lips—who can be our master?" in order to crush the vulnerable (12:2–4).

**God's word is utterly trustworthy in a world full of deceit.** Over against double-hearted human talk stands God's word, pure as silver refined seven times (12:6).

**God will not stay silent forever while the oppressed groan.** Right at the heart of the lament, God's own voice breaks in with a promise to arise and rescue (12:5; 12:7).

## Deeper Meaning

**The sin of the boastful tongue in verses 3 and 4 is explored further in the letter of James (12:3–4).** James compares the tongue to a tiny spark capable of setting an entire forest ablaze and corrupting the whole body (James 3:5–6).

**The image of God's word as silver refined sevenfold undergirds confidence in Scripture's complete reliability (12:6).** Jesus prayed to the Father that His word is truth (John 17:17), declaring that His words will never pass away (Matthew 24:35).

## Notes & Nuances

**Verse 6 uses the number *seven* in its classic Hebrew sense of completeness (12:6).** The poem's point is that God's word holds not the slightest alloy of error or broken promise.

**The ending in verse 8 is refreshingly realistic and refuses easy polish (12:8).** Even after hearing God's promise of protection in verses 5 and 7, David faces squarely the fact that wicked people still prowl freely whenever society prizes what is base; faith stands firm right inside an unredeemed reality.
