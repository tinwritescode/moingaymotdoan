# Psalms 48 — The City of the Great King

## Story

The hymn opens with ringing praise centered on Jerusalem: "Great is the LORD, and greatly to be praised in the city of our God, His holy mountain." Mount Zion is celebrated as the city of the great King and the joy of the whole earth, because right within her citadels God has revealed Himself as an unshakable fortress (48:1–3).

Next the singer replays a breathtaking rescue like a fast-moving scene. Allied kings march together to besiege the city, yet the instant they catch sight of it they freeze in shock, panic, tremble like a woman in labor, and bolt in terror—shattered as easily as great ships of Tarshish smashed by God's east wind. What God's people had previously only heard told in stories, they have now seen with their own eyes right in the city of their God (48:4–8).

Inside the temple, the congregation quietly meditates on God's steadfast love. As wide as His name reaches, so far His praise rings to the ends of the earth. Mount Zion and the towns of Judah rejoice over His righteous judgments (48:9–11).

The closing stanza invites worshipers to walk around Zion's walls, count her towers, and inspect her ramparts and citadels standing intact after danger has passed so they can tell the next generation. Yet the city's true defense is never thick stone walls, but her Resident King: this God is their God forever and ever, and He will guide them to the very end (48:12–14).

## Key Takeaways

**Zion's true worth comes from the One dwelling inside her.** Jerusalem stood unbreached not because of geography or masonry, but because God Himself stood within her as a fortress (48:3; 48:8). Walking around to count towers and ramparts (48:12–13) really means marveling at God's faithful protection.

**Faith moves from hearsay to firsthand witness—and then passes the story on.** Verse 8 captures that vividly: "As we have heard, so we have seen in the city of the LORD of Hosts" (48:8). Experiencing God's deliverance today carries the calling to "tell the next generation" (48:13).

## Deeper Meaning

**Jesus echoed the title *the city of the great King* in the Sermon on the Mount (48:2).** Teaching truthfulness in speech, Jesus warned against swearing by Jerusalem because it is the city of the great King (Matthew 5:35). Yet He also revealed that true worship would no longer be tied to one geographical hill, but offered in Spirit and truth (John 4:21–24).

**Unshakable Mount Zion points forward to the heavenly Jerusalem.** Hebrews reminds believers that through Jesus they have already drawn near to Mount Zion, the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem (Hebrews 12:22)—a holy city no adversary can ever overthrow.

## Notes & Nuances

**The dramatic rout of allied kings in verses 4 through 6 is often linked to Jerusalem's rescue under King Hezekiah** (2 Kings 19:35–36) or the miraculous deliverance under King Jehoshaphat (2 Chronicles 20). Whatever the exact historical crisis, the poem turns that remembered rescue into lasting praise for God's eternal guardianship.
