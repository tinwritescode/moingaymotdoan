# Psalms 34 — Taste and See That the LORD Is Good

## Story

According to the traditional heading, David composed this song after feigning madness before Abimelech (a royal title for King Achish of Gath) to escape alive while on the run (1 Samuel 21:10–15). He opens with a vow to bless the LORD at all times—not alone, but inviting the humble to join him in exalting God's name together (34:1–3).

He then shares his personal testimony: he sought the LORD, and God delivered him from all his fears. This poor man cried out and the LORD saved him; those who look to Him grow radiant and are never put to shame. The angel of the LORD encamps around those who fear Him to rescue them (34:4–7).

Out of firsthand rescue comes a warm invitation: "Taste and see that the LORD is good; blessed is the man who takes refuge in Him!" Even strong young lions grow weak and hungry, yet those who seek the LORD lack no good thing (34:8–10).

The second half shifts into the gentle voice of a teacher instructing children in the fear of the LORD. Whoever longs for life and good days must keep the tongue from deceit, turn from evil, do good, and diligently pursue peace (34:11–14).

David finishes by contrasting the destinies of the righteous and the wicked. The LORD's eyes watch the righteous and His ears hear their cry; above all, "The LORD is near to the brokenhearted; He saves the contrite in spirit." The righteous still face many afflictions, yet the LORD rescues them out of them all and guards every single bone so not one is broken. By contrast, evil itself slays the wicked, while those who take refuge in God are redeemed and never condemned (34:15–22).

## Key Takeaways

**Knowing God happens through lived experience, not theory alone.** David does not say *listen to a debate about God*; he invites us, "Taste and see that the LORD is good!" (34:8). Just as you truly know honey's sweetness only by tasting it, you discover God's goodness most clearly when you hand over your fears to Him (34:4).

**Fearing God shows up immediately in everyday speech and conduct.** Verses 11 through 14 keep reverence grounded in real life: guarding your tongue from lies, stepping away from harm, doing good, and chasing peace (34:11–14). Authentic faith never detaches from daily integrity.

**God never promises exemption from trouble, yet promises nearness and rescue.** Verse 19 speaks with bracing candor: "Many are the afflictions of the righteous, but the LORD delivers him from them all." Precisely when our hearts feel shattered, God stands nearest of all (34:18).

## Deeper Meaning

**The promise that not one bone will be broken is cited in the New Testament directly of Jesus' death (34:20).** When Roman soldiers came to break the legs of those crucified to hasten death, they found Jesus already dead and left His legs untouched. The apostle John notes this fulfilled Scripture—"Not one of His bones will be broken" (John 19:36)—braiding Psalm 34:20 together with the Passover lamb rule prohibiting broken bones (Exodus 12:46).

**The apostle Peter borrows Psalm 34's exact counsel to guide suffering churches.** Encouraging believers facing slander and persecution, Peter quotes nearly all of verses 12 through 16 about restraining the tongue, doing good, and pursuing peace because the Lord's eyes watch the righteous (1 Peter 3:10–12), having earlier echoed verse 8 when reminding them they have tasted that the Lord is good (1 Peter 2:3).

## Notes & Nuances

**Psalm 34 is an alphabetic acrostic following the Hebrew alphabet.** Successive verses (with minor ancient variations) begin with consecutive letters from start to finish, helping ancient Israelites memorize its rich wisdom on the fear of the LORD.

**Verse 18's tenderness toward the *brokenhearted* and *contrite in spirit* highlights how God treasures humility (34:18).** Coming right out of David's most humiliating escape—acting mad to get away from Gath—he learned that God does not despise the shattered and weak, but rushes close to save them.
