# Psalms 67 — Bless Us So All Nations May Know Your Ways

## Story

This concise seven-verse hymn opens with the familiar priestly blessing: asking God to be gracious, bless His people, and make His face shine upon them. Immediately, however, the aim of that blessing widens to embrace the entire world—not hoarded for Israel alone, but "that Your ways may be known on earth, Your salvation among all nations" (67:1–2).

Right away rings out an eager refrain longing for every people to praise God. The heart of the poem stands right in its middle verse (67:4): summoning all nations to rejoice and sing for joy, because God judges the peoples with equity and guides every nation on earth. The refrain of global praise then sounds a second time (67:3–5).

The final two verses tie harvest gratitude to global mission: the earth has yielded its harvest, proving that God is blessing His people. And the ultimate fruit of that blessing ripples to the horizon: "all the ends of the earth will fear Him" (67:6–7).

## Key Takeaways

**God blesses His people so they become channels of blessing to the nations.** The link between verse 1 and verse 2 is unmistakable: Israel asks God's face to shine upon them *so that* every nation sees His salvation (67:1–2). God's favor is never meant to dead-end in selfishness.

**God's righteous reign is good news for the whole world.** People often dread judgment, yet verse 4 urges the nations to "be glad and sing for joy" when God judges with fairness, because His rule dismantles oppression and establishes true peace (67:4).

**God's visible goodness among His people bears witness to the world.** When believers live beneath God's blessing (67:6–7), that tangible fruit draws people across the earth to revere the living God.

## Deeper Meaning

**This psalm bridges God's covenant promise to Abraham straight to Jesus' Great Commission.** God pledged to bless Abraham so that all families of the earth would be blessed through him (Genesis 12:2–3). Psalm 67 turns that promise into a missionary prayer for all nations, and Jesus fulfills that vision when commissioning His disciples to make disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:19–20; Revelation 7:9–10).

## Notes & Nuances

**Verse 1 directly adapts Aaron's priestly blessing (67:1).** In Numbers, God instructed Aaron to bless Israel asking that the LORD bless them, keep them, and make His face shine upon them (Numbers 6:24–26). Psalm 67 takes that cherished Israelite blessing and transforms it into an intercession for the whole world.

**The psalm forms a lovely symmetrical design often compared to a seven-branched lampstand.** Seven verses balance neatly around the central peak of verse 4: verses 1 and 7 highlight God's blessing, verses 2 and 6 speak of the earth and nations, verses 3 and 5 form matching refrains, and verse 4 crowns the center celebrating God's just rule over all peoples.
