# Psalms 94 — Does He Who Formed the Eye Not See?

## Story

Tucked right inside the *The LORD Reigns* psalms, Psalm 94 faces a thorny question head-on: if God truly reigns, why do bullies run wild? The psalmist cries out to the LORD—"O God of vengeance" and "Judge of the earth"—urging Him to rise and repay the proud. Wicked oppressors pour out arrogant boasts, crush God's people, murder widows, foreigners, and orphans, and smirk smugly that the LORD neither sees nor notices (94:1–7).

Turning directly on those senseless fools, the poet fires two razor-sharp questions: "Does He who implanted the ear not hear? Does He who formed the eye not see?" The One who disciplines nations and teaches humanity knowledge knows well that human schemes are mere vapor (94:8–11).

Next the tone softens into tender comfort for the righteous. Blessed is the person God disciplines and teaches from His law, granting inward calm during evil days until justice takes its course. The LORD will never abandon His people; eventually judgment will align again with righteousness (94:12–15).

In the closing section, the writer shares deeply personal testimony: who will stand up for him against evildoers? Without the LORD's help, his soul would swiftly have sunk into death's silence. Yet the moment he cries "My foot is slipping," God's steadfast love holds him up; when "anxiety multiplies within me," God's comfort delights his soul. A corrupt throne that frames mischief by statute can never partner with God; the LORD remains the psalmist's stronghold and rock of refuge, bringing evildoers' own wickedness crashing back upon their heads (94:16–23).

## Key Takeaways

**God is neither blind nor deaf to human injustice.** Assuming God neither notices nor cares is the oppressor's foolish illusion; the Maker of eyes and ears hears and sees every tear shed by the vulnerable (94:7–9).

**God's teaching through Scripture steadies the heart amid dark days.** Rather than spiraling into rage or despair, someone instructed in God's law finds quiet inward rest while awaiting justice (94:12–13).

**God's steadfast love and comfort meet us right as our feet slip and our minds race.** Verses 18 and 19 offer deeply relatable comfort: right when we feel ready to collapse amid swirling anxieties, God's steadying hand and consolation grow clearest (94:18–19).

**Injustice disguised in legal dress never fools God.** The psalm unmasks the subtlest tyranny: corrupt courts "framing mischief by statute" to condemn innocent blood (94:20–21). Before heaven's Judge, legal veneers conceal nothing.

## Deeper Meaning

**The apostle Paul cites Psalm 94 twice in the New Testament.** Reminding Corinthian believers that proud worldly cleverness is folly before God, Paul quotes verse 11 directly: "The Lord knows that the thoughts of the wise are futile" (94:11; 1 Corinthians 3:20). And affirming that God has not cast off His people Israel, he quotes verse 14 verbatim: "God has not rejected His people" (94:14; Romans 11:2).

**Leaving vengeance to God breaks the cycle of personal retaliation.** Calling God "O God of vengeance" (94:1; cf. Deuteronomy 32:35) underpins Paul's call for believers never to avenge themselves, leaving room for God's just wrath (Romans 12:19).

**Legalized condemnation of innocent blood reached its darkest hour at Jesus' trial.** Verses 20 and 21 depict officials misusing law to sentence the innocent (94:20–21)—exactly what religious leaders and courts did to Jesus (Matthew 27:4; 27:24). Yet God overturned their crooked verdict through His resurrection.

## Notes & Nuances

**Understanding *God of vengeance* (94:1).** In everyday modern speech, *vengeance* sounds like petty personal spite. In Scripture, *God's vengeance* never means blind fury, but full, impartial justice defending widows, orphans, and innocent victims crushed underfoot (94:6; 94:21).
