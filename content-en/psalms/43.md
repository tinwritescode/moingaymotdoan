# Psalms 43 — Light and Truth Leading Back to God's Altar

## Story

Picking up straight from the emotional arc of Psalm 42, the writer pleads with God to vindicate him and defend his cause against an ungodly nation and deceitful, unjust people. He voices the familiar bewildered question: since God is his stronghold, why does it feel as though God has rejected him, leaving him mourning under the enemy's oppression? (43:1–2).

Rather than staying stuck in pain, his prayer brightens into longing to be guided home: "Send out Your light and Your truth; let them lead me. Let them bring me to Your holy mountain, and to the place where You dwell." He pictures the day he steps up once more to God's altar—not merely for ritual's sake, but to meet the One who is "God, my exceeding joy"—and praises Him with the harp (43:3–4).

The poem concludes with the third appearance of the refrain linking it directly to Psalm 42 (Psalms 42:5; 42:11), rallying his soul one more time to shake off despair and anchor hope in God (43:5).

## Key Takeaways

**God Himself—not circumstances or rituals—is the believer's highest joy.** In verse 4, the psalmist approaches the altar not merely to perform offerings, but to come to "God, my exceeding joy" (43:4). When God is our ultimate gladness, hope survives even before outward circumstances change.

**God's light and truth are the two guides leading us out of darkness.** Surrounded by liars and oppressors (43:1), his first request is not vengeance, but God's guiding light and truth bringing him home to worship (43:3).

## Deeper Meaning

**The prayer for *Your light and Your truth* in verse 3 finds complete fulfillment in Jesus (43:3).** In John's Gospel, Jesus declares Himself the light of the world (John 8:12) and the way, the truth, and the life—no one comes to the Father except through Him (John 14:6). Christ Himself is the light and truth leading believers straight into God's dwelling place (Hebrews 10:19–22).

## Notes & Nuances

**Psalm 43 completes the paired unit of Psalms 42 and 43.** Where Psalm 42 leans heavily toward grief and memories of the past, these five crisp verses turn forward in hopeful petition and joyful harp-filled worship (43:4), rounding out the threefold refrain encouraging the soul (Psalms 42:5; 42:11; 43:5).
