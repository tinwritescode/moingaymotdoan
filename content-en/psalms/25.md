# Psalms 25 — Teach Me Your Paths

## Story

David begins his prayer with a gesture of complete surrender: "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul" (25:1). Surrounded by enemies, he places his trust in God and asks not to be put to shame or let his foes gloat over him; he is sure that no one who waits on God will ever be put to shame—only those who are treacherous without cause will face disgrace (25:2–3).

The next section offers a humble plea for two basic needs: guidance and forgiveness. He asks God to show him His ways and lead him in His truth all day long (25:4–5). Then he appeals to God to remember His ancient mercy and steadfast love, rather than the sins of his youth and his rebellious ways (25:6–7).

From verse 8 through verse 14, David pauses his petitions to meditate on the LORD's good character. Because the LORD is good and upright, He gladly instructs sinners in the way and guides the humble in what is right (25:8–10). Right in the middle of that meditation breaks out a brief yet profound prayer: "For the sake of Your name, O LORD, forgive my iniquity, for it is great" (25:11). He then returns to celebrate the blessings of the person who fears the LORD: receiving guidance on the path to choose, resting in prosperity, and above all this—"The LORD confides in those who fear Him, and reveals His covenant to them" (25:12–14).

The closing lines return to heavy reality: his eyes stay fixed on the LORD to pull his feet out of the net, for he feels lonely, afflicted, deeply troubled in heart, and hated fiercely by many enemies (25:15–19). He pleads for forgiveness of all his sins, asks God to guard his soul in integrity and uprightness, and concludes with intercession for the entire nation: "Redeem Israel, O God, from all its distress" (25:20–22).

## Key Takeaways

**Sinners can still hope for God's guidance—not because they deserve it, but because God is good.** Verses 8 and 11 ground that hope directly in God's own nature: He teaches sinners because He is good and upright; He pardons great guilt for the sake of His own name (25:8; 25:11).

**Humility is the prerequisite for learning God's ways.** God guides "the humble"—those willing to admit they lack the wisdom to steer their own lives (25:4–5; 25:9).

**Fearing the Lord opens the door to intimate friendship with Him.** The summit of relationship with God is not distant terror, but being taken into His confidence and shown the richness of His covenant (25:14).

## Deeper Meaning

**The promise that "none who wait for You will be put to shame" becomes a core gospel assurance in Romans (25:3).** Drawing on parallel wording from Isaiah, Paul declares that everyone who believes in Christ will never be put to shame (Romans 9:33; 10:11).

**Close friendship between God and those who fear Him reaches its fullness in Jesus' words to His disciples (25:14).** On His final night, He told them He no longer called them servants but *friends*, because everything He learned from His Father He had made known to them (John 15:15).

## Notes & Nuances

**Psalm 25 is a nearly complete alphabetic acrostic following the twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet.** Successive verses begin with consecutive letters (omitting two, repeating one, and adding a closing prayer for Israel in verse 22) (25:22). Because it follows the alphabet, themes of petition, reflection, confession, and trust weave back and forth rather than moving in a strictly linear story.

**The plea "forgive my iniquity, for it is great" flips ordinary human logic upside down (25:11).** People usually ask forgiveness by minimizing their offense as minor; David states bluntly that his guilt is massive—so massive that only the LORD's great name and mercy are big enough to cover it.
