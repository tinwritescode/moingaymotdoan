# Psalms 150 — Let Everything That Has Breath Praise the Lord

## Story

Psalm 150 is the radiant finale of the closing *Hallel* group and of all one hundred and fifty psalms. Compressed into six short verses, the poem pours every sound of a full orchestra into a single rising crescendo—the verb *praise* rings out thirteen times without pause.

Verse 1 answers *where* to praise: "Hallelujah! Praise God in His sanctuary. Praise Him in His mighty heavens!"—stretching from the earthly temple straight up to the vast dome of the sky (150:1).

Verse 2 answers *why* to praise: "Praise Him for His mighty acts; praise Him for His surpassing greatness!"—honoring God both for what He has done and for the sheer majesty of who He is (150:2).

The next three verses answer *how* to praise, summoning a lively ensemble of wind, strings, percussion, and movement: blast the trumpet, play the harp and lyre, strike the tambourine and dance, sound the strings and flute, clash the resounding cymbals and loud cymbals (150:3–5).

The final verse answers *who* should praise, throwing open every boundary to seal the entire book of Psalms with a call embracing all life: "Let everything that has breath praise the LORD! Hallelujah!" (150:6).

## Key Takeaways

**The destination of the whole book of Psalms is unclouded praise.** The Psalter holds plenty of tears, loneliness, raw questions, and midnight laments. Yet the collection does not end in grief or bitterness; it arrives at a pure symphony where only praise remains.

**Praise God for both His deeds and His character.** Verse 2 places two reasons side by side: we thank Him for the *mighty acts* He has performed in history and in our lives, and deeper still, we adore Him simply for His *surpassing greatness*.

**Every single breath is a reason to praise.** Verse 6 reminds us of a simple, profound truth: the breath inside our lungs is God's own gift (Genesis 2:7), so using that breath to bless Him is the truest purpose of being alive.

## Deeper Meaning

**The grand chorus of all creation in Revelation.** The closing summons, "Let everything that has breath praise the LORD!" (150:6), finds its complete answer in Revelation 5:13, where John hears every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth, and on the sea—and all that is in them—crying out blessing, honor, glory, and power to Him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb forever and ever.

**Praising with our whole lives in Christ.** When physical breath meets the breath of the Spirit in the New Testament, worship is no longer limited to instruments inside the Jerusalem temple; it becomes the believer's daily living sacrifice (Romans 12:1; Hebrews 13:15).

## Notes & Nuances

**Four questions packed into six verses.** Psalm 150 is brief yet tightly built: *where* (150:1), *why* (150:2), *how* (150:3–5), and *who* (150:6). Every family of Old Testament instruments appears: the ram's horn (*shofar*) blown on solemn festivals, the harps and lyres of the temple Levites, the tambourines and dancing of ordinary crowds in the streets, and crashing cymbals—symbolizing that every artistic gift and human emotion has a welcome place in worship.

## What Commentators Say

**Charles Spurgeon**, nineteenth-century English pastor, compared Psalm 150 to the final triumphant trumpet fanfare closing the entire symphony of the Psalter. After walking through so many valleys of tears and battlefields in earlier psalms, the soul at last steps onto a summit where lament disappears and only *Hallelujah* echoes across heaven and earth. *(The Treasury of David, on Psalm 150)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: recalls Spurgeon's summary of Psalm 150 as the culminating peak of the entire Psalter -->
