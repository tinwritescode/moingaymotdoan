# Psalms 62 — In God Alone My Soul Finds Rest

## Story

This entire psalm breathes an uncommon, unhurried calm. Rather than opening with frantic pleading, David begins with serene assurance: "In God alone my soul finds rest; my salvation comes from Him." He declares that God alone is his rock, his salvation, and his fortress, so he will never be greatly shaken (62:1–2).

Next he confronts attackers teaming up to push him over like a leaning wall or tottering fence. Their sole aim is to topple him from his place of honor; with their mouths they bless sweetly while inwardly pouring out curses and lies (62:3–4).

Instead of trading blows with them, David speaks straight to his own soul in a refrain echoing his opening almost word for word—with one subtle upgrade: no longer saying "I will not be greatly shaken," he declares firmly, "I will not be shaken." Every ounce of his salvation, honor, mighty strength, and refuge rests wholly in God (62:5–7).

Widening from personal experience to the whole nation, he calls out warmly: "Trust in Him at all times, O people; pour out your hearts before Him. God is our refuge." He warns them never to anchor trust in flimsy supports: human beings—lowborn or highborn alike—weigh lighter than a breath on the scales; and wealth, especially gain seized through extortion or robbery, must never claim their heart even if it multiplies (62:8–10).

The poem concludes with two bedrock truths David has clearly heard from God: "power belongs to God" and "loving devotion belongs to You, O Lord." Power and steadfast love belong together, guaranteeing that God will fairly repay every person according to what they have done (62:11–12).

## Key Takeaways

**The deepest peace flows from the word *alone*.** The word *alone* (or *only*) rings like a bell throughout this poem (62:1–2; 62:5–6). Dividing our trust between God and backup crutches leaves us shaky; resting in God alone settles the soul.

**Leaning on people or riches rests on something lighter than air.** David does not despise honest work or standing, yet warns us never to "set your heart upon them" when riches increase (62:10). Human favor shifts and money evaporates; God alone stands unmoved (62:9).

**Trusting God means pouring out your entire heart before Him.** Resting silently before God in verses 1 and 5 does not mean stoically bottling up emotions (62:1; 62:5). Verse 8 pairs quiet rest with "pouring out your hearts before Him"—spilling every fear and frustration honestly into the safest shelter there is (62:8).

**Power without mercy terrifies, and mercy without power cannot save—in God both unite.** The closing lines join God's twin pillars: power mighty enough to rescue and steadfast love tender enough to care, paired with impartial justice toward every life (62:11–12).

## Deeper Meaning

**The warning against setting your heart on wealth echoes in Jesus and Paul.** "If your riches increase, do not set your heart upon them" (62:10) resonates with Jesus' reminder that where your treasure is, your heart will be also (Matthew 6:21), and Paul's charge to the wealthy never to grow arrogant or set hope on uncertain riches, but on God Himself (1 Timothy 6:17).

**The truth that God repays each person according to their deeds is quoted directly in the New Testament.** Jesus echoes verse 12 when speaking of His return (62:12; Matthew 16:27), and Paul cites it verbatim in Romans (Romans 2:6). God's fair judgment never contradicts grace—it affirms that how we truly live matters deeply before Him.

## Notes & Nuances

**Saying "Once God has spoken; twice I have heard this" uses a classic Hebrew wisdom idiom (62:11).** Much like Job and Proverbs (Job 33:14; Proverbs 30:15–31), this stepped numerical pattern (*once... twice*) does not mean mechanical repetition, but underscores that the truth about to follow is settled, unmistakable, and worthy of engraving on the heart.

**The shift from "not be greatly shaken" (62:2) to "not be shaken" (62:6) is wonderfully subtle.** Many readers notice that as David preaches rest in God to his soul a second time, his faith grows firmer still: first he allows he might wobble slightly without falling, and halfway through he is certain he will not be shaken at all.
