# Psalms 35 — Plead My Cause Against False Accusers

## Story

David opens this psalm blending battlefield and courtroom imagery. He calls on the LORD to contend with those who contend with him, take up shield and buckler, and brandish spear and javelin to block his pursuers. Right amid the clash of arms, what he longs to hear most is a gentle inward assurance: "Say to my soul, 'I am your salvation'" (35:1–3).

He prays that those who hid a net and dug a pit for him without cause will be put to shame, scattered like chaff before the wind, slipping in darkness and tumbling straight into their own trap. When God steps in, David's whole being will rejoice and praise the One who rescues the weak from those too strong for them (35:4–10).

Then David uncovers his rawest wound: betrayal by people repaying kindness with cruelty. Malicious witnesses stand up grilling him over charges he knows nothing about. Yet earlier, when those very people lay sick, David wore sackcloth, fasted, and bowed his head in mournful prayer for them as though grieving a brother or his own mother. Now that he has stumbled, they gather in glee to mock him, gnashing their teeth and tearing at him relentlessly (35:11–16).

He cries out asking how long the Lord will simply look on, pleading for God to snatch his life from ravaging lions so he can give thanks before the great congregation (35:17–18).

The closing section pleads with God to stay silent no longer. Enemies hate him without cause, scheme against quiet neighbors, and gloat as though they have swallowed him whole. David urges God to awake and judge his cause righteously—putting foes to shame while letting defenders of justice rejoice and exalt the LORD, as David's own tongue proclaims God's righteousness all day long (35:19–28).

## Key Takeaways

**Betrayal by those we once loved stings most bitterly.** David's grief comes not merely from danger, but from recognizing his accusers as the very people for whom he once fasted and wept in sickness (35:12–14). This psalm invites believers to pour out that raw hurt directly to God rather than swallowing resentment in silence.

**Believers hand vengeance over to God instead of retaliating themselves.** Intense as verses 4 through 8 sound, David never sets counter-traps for his foes; he submits the entire case to God's intervention (35:1; 35:23–24). His ultimate longing is to see God's righteousness and glory celebrated before the assembly (35:18; 35:27–28).

## Deeper Meaning

**Being hated without cause and accused by false witnesses foreshadows Jesus Himself.** David laments "malicious witnesses" rising up (35:11) and foes who "hate me without cause" (35:19). At the Last Supper, Jesus cited *They hated Me without reason* as Scripture fulfilled in Himself (John 15:25; Psalms 69:4), and before the Sanhedrin false witnesses stood up leveling fabricated charges against Him (Mark 14:56–57).

**Jesus lived out love for future betrayers to perfection.** David fasted and mourned for people who later turned on him (35:13–14); Jesus went further still—washing Judas's feet, healing the ear of a servant sent to arrest Him, and praying for the Father to forgive those nailing Him to the cross (Luke 23:34).

## Notes & Nuances

**Psalm 35 belongs to the group of *imprecatory psalms*—prayers calling for divine judgment on evildoers.** Pleas for foes to be chased down or caught in their own snare can sound jarring to New Testament ears. Remember that this is the anointed king asking God to defend justice against unprovoked violence, not reckless personal spite; and in New Testament light, believers pray for persecutors' repentance while still entrusting every injustice to the righteous Judge (Matthew 5:44; 1 Peter 2:23).
