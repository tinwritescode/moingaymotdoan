# Daniel 12 — Resurrection and the Promise of the End

## Story

The heavenly messenger brings the prophecy begun in chapter 11 to its climactic summit. At that time, Michael—the great prince who stands guard over Daniel's people—will arise. A time of distress will erupt unlike any since nations first existed; yet everyone among Daniel's people whose name is found written in *the book* will be delivered (12:1).

Then rings out the chapter's grandest promise: "Many who sleep in the dust of the earth will awake—some to everlasting life, but others to shame and everlasting contempt." Those who are wise will shine like the brightness of the heavens, and those who lead many to righteousness like the stars forever and ever. The angel instructs Daniel to shut up the words and seal the scroll until the time of the end; many will roam to and fro, and knowledge will increase (12:2–4).

Looking out across the river, Daniel sees two other figures standing on opposite banks. One asks the linen-clad figure standing above the waters how long it will be until these astonishing events conclude. Raising both right and left hands toward heaven, the figure swears by Him who lives forever that it will be for "a time, times, and half a time"; when the power of the holy people has finally been shattered, all these things will be completed (12:5–7).

Daniel hears the words yet still cannot grasp them, so he asks plainly: "My lord, what will be the outcome of these things?" The angel replies gently, "Go on your way, Daniel," for the words are closed and sealed until the end. Many will be purified, made spotless, and refined; the wicked will keep acting wickedly with no understanding, while the wise will understand. The angel adds two concrete time markers: from the abolition of the daily sacrifice and the setting up of the abomination of desolation, there will be 1,290 days; and "Blessed is he who waits and reaches the end of the 1,335 days!" (12:8–12).

The entire book of Daniel closes with a deeply personal, warm farewell spoken to the aging prophet: "As for you, go on your way until the end. You will rest, and will arise to your inheritance at the end of the days" (12:13).

## Key Takeaways

**Death is not the final period for the faithful.** In chapter 11, many wise believers loyal to God fell by sword and flame. Chapter 12 directly answers that agonizing loss: they are only *sleeping in the dust*, destined one day to wake into everlasting life, while the wicked who thought they had triumphed will wake to everlasting shame (12:2).

**The highest reward belongs to those who turn others toward righteousness.** In a world obsessed with scheming and conquest, lasting greatness never lies in building empires like Nebuchadnezzar or winning wars like the northern kings. The wise who guide many back to righteousness are the stars shining undimmed forever (12:3).

**We do not need every puzzle of the future solved in order to live faithfully today.** Even Daniel—entrusted with staggering visions—frankly admits at life's close, "I did not understand" (12:8). The angel does not unlock every number for him, simply telling him to walk his path faithfully to the end and rest in peace (12:9; 12:13).

**Blessing belongs to those who wait patiently for God.** Right through history's darkest crisis, God guards His people's names securely in His book (12:1). The book's final beatitude belongs to those who refuse to quit midway, persevering until God completes His word (12:12).

## Deeper Meaning

**The clearest statement of resurrection in the Old Testament.** Before Daniel 12:2, the Old Testament offered only glimmers of hope reaching past the grave (Job 19:25–27; Isaiah 26:19). Daniel 12:2 speaks unmistakably of a resurrection for both destinies—everlasting life and everlasting shame. In the New Testament, Jesus echoes this exact structure when declaring that an hour is coming when all in the tombs will hear His voice and come out: those who did good to the resurrection of life, and those who did evil to the resurrection of judgment (John 5:28–29). Likewise, the righteous shining like stars (12:3) resonates in Jesus' promise that the righteous will shine like the sun in their Father's kingdom (Matthew 13:43).

**Unparalleled tribulation in Matthew 24 and Mark 13.** The prediction in 12:1 of a distress greater than any since nations began is quoted almost word for word by Jesus in His Olivet Discourse (Matthew 24:21; Mark 13:19).

**The sealed scroll and the unsealed scroll in Revelation.** A cascade of Daniel 12 images steps directly into Revelation: Michael rising to battle (Revelation 12:7), the book recording the names of the saved (Revelation 20:12; 21:27), and the mighty angel standing over the waters lifting his hand to swear by Him who lives forever (Revelation 10:5–6). Yet one crucial contrast stands out: Daniel is told to "seal the scroll until the time of the end" (12:4; 12:9), whereas John—writing after Jesus' incarnation, cross, and resurrection—receives the opposite command: "Do not seal up the words of prophecy in this book, because the time is near" (Revelation 22:10–11).

## Notes & Nuances

**What do the 1,290 days and 1,335 days (12:11–12) signify?** These two final numbers spark intense curiosity, running slightly past the three-and-a-half-year benchmark (1,260 days, or *a time, times, and half a time* in verse 7) by 30 and 75 days respectively. Commentators propose several readings. Under a Maccabean historical reading, scholars note that 1,290 days closely approximates the temple's desecration under Antiochus IV (factoring in an intercalary Jewish month), while 1,335 days reflects a slight extension of expectation before deliverance (or until news of Antiochus's death arrived). Under a futurist end-times reading, many interpreters propose that following three and a half years of tribulation (1,260 days), an extra 30 days bring judgment and cleansing and 45 more days establish Christ's peaceful reign—making those who endure to day 1,335 blessed. Many others read the numbers symbolically: even when suffering appears to stretch past every bearable limit, it remains under God's exact timetable and ends in blessing.

**Does "Many who sleep in the dust of the earth will awake" imply only *some* rise?** In Hebrew idiom, *many* does not exclude others, but emphasizes a *vast multitude* (cf. Isaiah 53:11–12; Matthew 20:28). The verse affirms a great resurrection embracing both the righteous and the wicked, which the New Testament later clarifies as universal (John 5:28–29; Acts 24:15).

**"Many will roam to and fro, and knowledge will increase" (12:4).** Popular modern readers often apply this verse to jet travel and exploding digital technology. Within Daniel's own context, however, *roaming to and fro* commonly portrays people searching eagerly for God's word or scanning a prophetic scroll back and forth (cf. Amos 8:12), while *knowledge* increasing refers to understanding this very prophecy as its events unfold at the end.

After twelve chapters packed with bizarre dreams, a fiery furnace, lions, terrifying beasts, and clashing empires, the book does not end with a blaring trumpet, but with a gentle personal note to a weary old man: go on your way, rest, and on the last day stand up for your inheritance.
