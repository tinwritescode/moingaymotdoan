# Galatians 6 — Boasting Only in the Cross

## Story

Paul paints what walking by the Spirit looks like in everyday community life. If someone is caught in a trespass, those of you who walk by the Spirit should restore that person gently, watching yourselves closely lest you too be tempted. "Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ." (6:1–2). The agitators wanted to load Mosaic law onto their shoulders; Paul sets before them a different law—the law of Christ's love.

Anyone who thinks he is something when he is nothing fools himself. Each person should test his own work; then any ground for satisfaction rests in himself alone rather than in comparing himself with someone else, for each one will have to carry his own load (6:3–5). Verse 2 commands carrying one another's burdens, while verse 5 says each carries his own load. There is no contradiction: some crushing weights exceed any single person's strength, while each of us still answers personally for our own stewardship.

Whoever receives instruction in the word must share all good things with the teacher. Do not be deceived: God is not mocked, "for whatever a person sows, that he will also reap." Whoever sows to his own flesh will reap corruption from the flesh, whereas whoever sows to the Spirit will reap eternal life from the Spirit. Let us not grow weary of doing good, because at the proper season we will reap if we do not give up. Therefore, whenever opportunity arises, let us do good to everyone, and especially to those belonging to the household of faith (6:6–10). Free grace never cancels the law of sowing and reaping; it transforms the field in which we sow.

At this point Paul takes the pen directly into his own hand: "See with what large letters I am writing to you with my own hand!" (6:11). Ancient authors typically dictated letters to a secretary—as Tertius wrote down Romans—before signing a few personal closing lines to verify authenticity (Romans 16:22). Those bold strokes may reflect weak eyesight or deliberate emphasis; either way, every closing word flows straight from Paul's own hand.

He strips bare the agitators' true motives. Those pressing circumcision merely want to make an impressive outward showing in the flesh, solely to avoid persecution for the cross of Christ. Not even those who accept circumcision actually keep the law themselves; they simply want your flesh circumcised so they can boast over your bodies (6:12–13). Every circumcised Gentile served as another trophy for their scoreboard.

"But far be it from me to boast except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world!" Neither circumcision nor uncircumcision counts for anything; what matters is a new creation. Peace and mercy rest upon all who walk by this rule, and upon the Israel of God (6:14–16). Readers still discuss whether "the Israel of God" designates the whole Church of Jews and Gentiles together, or believing Jews within it.

Paul closes with a weathered plea: from now on let no one cause him trouble, because he bears on his body the brand marks of Jesus. Finally he prays that the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with their spirit (6:17–18; 2 Corinthians 11:23–25). The agitators demanded a physical mark proving covenant belonging. Paul carries physical marks of his own—the scars of rods and stones endured for preaching the cross.

## Key Takeaways

**Restore the fallen with gentleness.** People genuinely led by the Spirit do not hunt for others' mistakes to feel superior; they "restore them in a spirit of gentleness" (6:1). The Greek verb for "restore" also describes setting a broken bone: the aim is healing, never punishment.

**Carry others' heavy weights while owning your own pack.** "Bear one another's burdens" (6:2) stands comfortably beside "each will carry his own load" (6:5). Crushing crises call for brothers and sisters stepping under the weight together, while personal responsibility cannot be outsourced to anyone else.

**You reap what you sow.** "God is not mocked, for whatever one sows, that will he also reap" (6:7). Grace never erases moral cause and effect. Living for self harvests decay; investing in the Spirit harvests eternal life (6:8).

**Keep doing good without burning out.** Harvest can take time, yet it arrives without fail (6:9). Seize every open door to show kindness to everyone, beginning with—never stopping at—the family of faith (6:10).

**Boast solely in the cross.** While rival teachers tally circumcised bodies as badges of pride (6:13), Paul boasts only in the cross where the world died to him and he died to the world (6:14). What matters is never an outward mark on flesh, but "a new creation" (6:15).

## Deeper Meaning

**The law of Christ.** After arguing tirelessly against living under Mosaic law, Paul speaks warmly of "the law of Christ" (6:2)—pointing straight to Jesus' new commandment: "Just as I have loved you, so you also must love one another" (John 13:34). In 1 Corinthians Paul likewise describes himself as "under the law of Christ" (1 Corinthians 9:21), and James calls neighbor-love "the royal law" (James 2:8).

**Sowing wind, reaping whirlwind.** Agricultural harvest imagery runs deep through the Old Testament: "Whoever sows injustice reaps calamity" (Proverbs 22:8), and unfaithful Israel "sow the wind and reap the whirlwind" (Hosea 8:7). Paul picks up that ancient wisdom and adds a fresh dimension: we can now sow directly "to the Spirit."

**New creation.** The phrase "new creation" in 6:15 echoes Paul's parallel word in 2 Corinthians: "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation" (2 Corinthians 5:17). Isaiah's sweeping promise has already begun breaking into history: "Behold, I create new heavens and a new earth" (Isaiah 65:17).

**True circumcision of the heart.** Paul never discards the deeper meaning of circumcision; he relocates it inside the heart: "True circumcision is of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the letter" (Romans 2:29). To Philippi he boldly writes, "We are the true circumcision" (Philippians 3:3)—echoing Moses' own promise that God would circumcise His people's hearts to love Him (Deuteronomy 30:6).

**Peace upon Israel.** The closing blessing in 6:16 echoes the final refrain of two psalms: "Peace be upon Israel!" (Psalms 125:5; Psalms 128:6). Paul seals a fierce letter debating Torah observance with Israel's own beloved benediction.

## Notes & Nuances

**Who is "the Israel of God"?** Verse 6:16 sparks lively debate among interpreters, yielding three main readings: first, the entire Church—Jews and Gentiles united in Christ—named the true Israel; second, specifically Jewish believers in Christ; third, historic Israel as a whole, for whose mercy Paul still intercedes as in Romans 9–11. Although the first view is most widespread, nowhere else does Paul flatly call the Church "Israel," while he does remark that "not all who are descended from Israel belong to Israel" (Romans 9:6).

**Why such large letters?** Writing in oversized script (6:11) might point to poor eyesight (cf. 4:15), unfamiliarity with professional scribal calligraphy, or bold visual emphasis—like writing in capital letters or underlining. We simply cannot tell for sure.

**The marks of Jesus.** The Greek word *stigmata* in 6:17 referred to brands or tattoos stamped onto slaves to identify their owner. Paul almost certainly refers to real scars left by beatings and stoning (2 Corinthians 11:23–25; Acts 14:19), rather than miraculous nail-wound stigmata attested in later medieval devotion.

**Supporting teachers financially.** Verse 6:6 is no self-serving fundraiser; Paul famously declined financial support from churches he planted (1 Corinthians 9:12–18). He sets down a general principle that congregations care materially for those teaching them God's word, echoing Jesus' principle that "the worker deserves his wages" (Luke 10:7).
