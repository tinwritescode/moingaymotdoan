# Matthew 24 — The Olivet Discourse: The Temple's Fall and the Coming of the Son of Man

## Story

As Jesus left the temple, His disciples came up to point out its magnificent buildings. He answered: do you see all these things? Truly not one stone here will be left on another; every one will be thrown down (24:1–2).

Later, as He sat on the Mount of Olives, the disciples approached Him privately: tell us when these things will happen, and what will be the sign of Your coming and of the end of the age? (24:3).

He replied: watch out that no one deceives you. Many will come in My name claiming, "I am the Christ." You will hear of wars and rumors of wars; do not be alarmed, for such things must happen, yet the end is still to come. Nation will rise against nation, and there will be famines and earthquakes in various places. All these are merely the beginning of birth pains (24:4–8).

Then they will hand you over to tribulation and put you to death, and you will be hated by all nations because of My name. Many will fall away, betray one another, and hate one another. Many false prophets will rise and deceive many. Because lawlessness will multiply, the love of most will grow cold. "But the one who perseveres to the end will be saved." And this gospel of the kingdom will be preached throughout the whole world as a testimony to all nations, and then the end will come (24:9–14).

When you see "the abomination of desolation," spoken of by the prophet Daniel, standing in the holy place, let those in Judea flee to the mountains. Let no one on the housetop go down to take anything out of the house; let no one in the field turn back for a cloak. How dreadful those days will be for pregnant women and nursing mothers! Pray that your flight will not take place in winter or on a Sabbath. For there will be great distress, unequaled from the beginning of the world until now and never to be equaled again; if those days had not been cut short, no one would survive, yet for the sake of the elect those days will be shortened (24:15–22).

At that time, if anyone tells you the Christ is here or there, do not believe it. False christs and false prophets will arise performing great signs and wonders so as to lead astray, if possible, even the elect. If people say He is out in the desert, do not go out; if they say He is in inner rooms, do not believe it. For just as lightning flashes from the east and shines as far as the west, so will be the coming of the Son of Man (24:23–28).

Immediately after the distress of those days, the sun will be darkened, the moon will not give its light, and the stars will fall from the sky. The sign of the Son of Man will appear in heaven; all the tribes of the earth will mourn and see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven with power and great glory. He will send out His angels with a loud trumpet call to gather His elect from the four winds (24:29–31).

Learn this lesson from the fig tree: as soon as its twigs grow tender and sprout leaves, you know summer is near. In the same way, when you see all these things, know that He is near, right at the doors. Truly this generation will not pass away until all these things have taken place. "Heaven and earth will pass away, but My words will never pass away" (24:32–35).

Yet concerning that day and hour no one knows—not even the angels of heaven, nor the Son, but the Father alone. Just as it was in Noah's days, people were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage right up to the day Noah entered the ark, oblivious until the flood swept them all away—so will be the coming of the Son of Man. Two men will be in the field: one will be taken and the other left; two women grinding at the mill: one will be taken and the other left (24:36–41).

Therefore keep watch, because you do not know on what day your Lord is coming. If the homeowner had known what watch of the night the thief was coming, he would have stayed awake and kept his house from being broken into. You too must be ready, because the Son of Man will come at an hour you do not expect (24:42–44).

Who then is the faithful and wise servant whom the master put in charge of his household to give them food at the proper time? Blessed is that servant whose master finds him doing so when he returns; he will put him in charge of all his possessions. Yet if a wicked servant tells himself his master is delayed and starts beating his fellow servants and carousing with drunkards, the master will return on a day he does not expect and an hour he does not know, punish him severely, and assign him a place with the hypocrites, where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth (24:45–51).

## Key Takeaways

**Even the grandest temple will fall.** The disciples admire the stonework, while Jesus announces not one stone will remain stacked on another (24:2). Everything built by human hands—no matter how sacred—eventually passes away. Only Christ's words endure forever (24:35).

**The greatest danger is deception.** Jesus' opening command is not "calculate dates," but "watch out that no one deceives you" (24:4). Wars, famines, and earthquakes are real, yet they do not mean the end has arrived. Self-proclaimed messiahs and rumors of secret appearances are the true trap.

**In hard times, endure and keep love alive.** Jesus warns that the love of many will grow cold (24:12). The deadliest threat of dark seasons is not only persecution from outside, but growing cold-hearted within. "The one who perseveres to the end will be saved" (24:13).

**Nobody knows the day or the hour.** Not even the Son knows—only the Father (24:36). Anyone claiming to have cracked the date of Christ's return is claiming to know what Jesus explicitly said nobody knows.

**Waiting means staying faithful at your post.** The praised servant is simply the one found feeding the household on schedule when the master arrives (24:46). Keeping watch does not mean staring idly at the sky, but quietly doing the daily work Christ entrusted to us.

## Deeper Meaning

**Daniel's abomination.** Daniel foretold "the abomination that causes desolation" set up when daily sacrifices ceased (Daniel 12:11; Daniel 9:27). Jewish memory immediately recalled the Greek ruler Antiochus erecting a pagan altar inside the temple around 167 BC. Jesus warns that a similar desecration lies ahead.

**The Son of Man on the clouds.** Coming on the clouds of heaven draws straight from Daniel's vision: "one like a son of man, coming with the clouds of heaven" (Daniel 7:13). In Daniel, that figure approaches the Ancient of Days to receive an everlasting kingdom.

**Darkened sun and falling stars.** Old Testament prophets routinely used cosmic collapse imagery to depict the fall of empires. Isaiah described Babylon's overthrow: stars ceasing to shine, the sun darkened, the moon withholding its light (Isaiah 13:10). It is the dramatic vocabulary of heaven and earth shaking when God intervenes in history.

**The nations mourning.** Zechariah envisioned a day when Jerusalem's inhabitants "will look on Me, the One they have pierced" and weep as for an only child (Zechariah 12:10). Matthew weaves that imagery into the Son of Man's unveiling.

**Like a thief, like Noah's day.** Paul likewise says the Day of the Lord will arrive like a thief in the night (1 Thessalonians 5:2). People in Noah's generation were not judged for eating or getting married, but for living as if God would never hold them to account (Genesis 7:7). Peter explains why the day seems delayed: God is patient, wanting everyone to reach repentance (2 Peter 3:9).

**God's unshakeable word.** "Heaven and earth will pass away, but My words will never pass away" (24:35). Isaiah had declared, "The grass withers and the flowers fall, but the word of our God stands forever" (Isaiah 40:8). Jesus sets His own words squarely alongside the eternal word of God.

## Notes & Nuances

**Two questions woven together.** The disciples asked both when the temple would fall and what signs would mark His coming and the age's end (24:3). Jesus' answer braids both horizons together, making it tricky to separate which verse points to which event. Many readers see the opening portion fulfilled when Rome destroyed Jerusalem in AD 70 and the later verses looking toward Christ's final return. Others read nearly the entire chapter as fulfilled in AD 70, while still others view the whole chapter as still future.

**Who is "this generation"?** Jesus states that "this generation will not pass away until all these things have taken place" (24:34). Some understand it as His contemporaries—fitting the temple's destruction roughly forty years later. Others take it to mean the Jewish nation, or the future generation alive when end-time signs unfold. Albert Schweitzer and later critics argued Jesus mistakenly expected the end right away, a conclusion many other scholars reject.

**Who is "taken"?** Popular modern prophecy readings often treat 24:40–41 as believers being secretly raptured out of the world. Yet in the immediately preceding sentence, the flood "swept them all away" in judgment (24:39). Many commentators therefore read the one "taken" as swept away into judgment while the one left behind is spared. Either way, the passage offers no blueprint for a detailed end-times timetable.

**How could the Son not know?** From early centuries, verse 24:36 puzzled readers since the Son is God. Church Fathers commonly explained that Jesus spoke according to the human nature He freely assumed. However theologians explain the mystery, one practical point is crystal clear: nobody is permitted to set dates.

## What Commentators Say

**Josephus**, the first-century Jewish historian, witnessed the Roman siege of Jerusalem firsthand and wrote a gripping account of it. He describes appalling famine inside the walls, self-styled prophets promising miraculous rescue, and flames consuming the temple in AD 70. **Eusebius**, bishop of Caesarea and early fourth-century church historian, reports that before the war broke out, Jerusalem Christians received an oracle warning them to flee across the Jordan to Pella. Many readers see this as obedience to Jesus' instruction in 24:16, "let those who are in Judea flee to the mountains." *(Church History, book 3, chapter 5)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: the flight of Jerusalem believers to Pella in Church History book 3 chapter 5 is well known, though Eusebius cites a prophetic revelation rather than explicitly linking it to 24:16 -->

**Augustine**, bishop of Hippo in North Africa in the late fourth century, replied to a fellow bishop eager to calculate the end of the world. Believers should wait and keep watch, Augustine wrote, never calculate dates, because the Lord declared nobody knows. Admitting his own ignorance, he remarked that it is far better to confess we do not know than to pretend we do. *(Letter 199, to Hesychius)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: confident Augustine wrote to Hesychius refusing to calculate the end date; recalled as Letter 199, though letter number is unverified -->

**The Talmud**, the collection of rabbinic commentary, pronounces a blunt curse on end-time calculators: may the bones of those who compute the end rot away. The reason given is pastoral: once a predicted date passes without the Messiah arriving, disillusioned people stop hoping altogether. Both Jewish sages and Jesus guard believers against date-setting. *(Babylonian Talmud, Sanhedrin 97b)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: the curse on end-time calculators in Sanhedrin 97b is widely cited -->

**N. T. Wright**, the contemporary Anglican bishop and New Testament scholar, contends that most of this chapter speaks of Jerusalem and the temple's fall. Drawing on Daniel, he reads "coming on the clouds" as the Son of Man's vindication and enthronement in heaven rather than a downward flight through the sky, and views darkened sun and falling stars as prophetic language for historic catastrophe. *(Jesus and the Victory of God)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: this reading of Mark 13 and Matthew 24 is a signature argument of Wright in this volume -->
