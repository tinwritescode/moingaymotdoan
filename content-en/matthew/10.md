# Matthew 10 — Sending Out the Twelve Apostles

## Story

Jesus called His twelve disciples to Him and gave them authority to drive out unclean spirits and to heal every disease and sickness (10:1). Matthew records their names: Simon who is called Peter, and his brother Andrew; James and John, sons of Zebedee; Philip and Bartholomew; Thomas, and Matthew "the tax collector"; James son of Alphaeus, and Thaddaeus; Simon the Zealot, and Judas Iscariot, who betrayed Him (10:2–4).

He sent them out with instructions: do not go among the Gentiles or enter any town of the Samaritans, but go rather to "the lost sheep of the house of Israel." Proclaim that the kingdom of heaven is near. Heal the sick, raise the dead, cleanse the lepers, drive out demons. "Freely you have received; freely give" (10:5–8).

Take no gold, silver, or copper in your belts, no travel bag, no extra tunic, sandals, or staff, for the worker is worthy of his provisions. Whatever town you enter, look for a worthy person and stay at their house until you leave. If any home or town refuses to welcome you or listen to your words, shake the dust off your feet as you leave; on the day of judgment it will be more bearable for Sodom and Gomorrah than for that town (10:9–15).

He does not hide what awaits them: I am sending you out like sheep among wolves, so be "as shrewd as snakes and as innocent as doves." People will hand you over to local councils, flog you in their synagogues, and bring you before governors and kings on My account. When they arrest you, do not worry about what to say, for the Spirit of your Father will speak through you (10:16–20).

Brother will betray brother to death, and a father his child. You will be hated by everyone because of My name, "but the one who perseveres to the end will be saved." When persecuted in one town, flee to the next. "A disciple is not above his teacher, nor a servant above his master"; if they have called the head of the house Beelzebul, how much more the members of his household (10:21–25).

Yet do not be afraid. Nothing is concealed that will not be disclosed; what I tell you in the dark, speak in the daylight. Do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul; rather, fear the One who can destroy both soul and body in hell. Are not two sparrows sold for a penny? Yet not one of them falls to the ground apart from your Father. Even the hairs of your head are all numbered. "So do not be afraid; you are worth more than many sparrows" (10:26–31).

Whoever acknowledges Me before men, I will also acknowledge before My Father in heaven; whoever disowns Me, I will disown before My Father (10:32–33). Do not assume I have come to bring peace to the earth; I have come to bring a sword. I have come to turn a man against his father, a daughter against her mother, and a person's enemies will be members of his own household (10:34–36).

Anyone who loves father or mother, son or daughter more than Me is not worthy of Me. Anyone who does not take up his cross and follow Me is not worthy of Me. "Whoever finds his life will lose it, and whoever loses his life for My sake will find it" (10:37–39).

He finishes with a promise for those who welcome His messengers: whoever welcomes you welcomes Me, and whoever welcomes Me welcomes the One who sent Me. Even anyone who gives one of these little ones just a cup of cold water because he is a disciple will certainly not lose his reward (10:40–42).

## Key Takeaways

**God sends ordinary people.** The list of twelve includes fishermen, a tax collector working for Rome, an anti-Roman Zealot, and the very man who will betray Him (10:2–4). Jesus does not wait for flawless recruits; He shares His own authority with flawed ones (10:1).

**Freely received, freely given.** The disciples are not allowed to sell healing or pack excess wealth for the road (10:8–10). They must rely on the hospitality of those who listen, trusting God to provide for them.

**Following Jesus invites opposition.** Jesus never promises smooth sailing. He speaks plainly: you are sheep among wolves, you will be flogged, dragged into court, and betrayed even by family members (10:16–21). If the Master was called the prince of demons, disciples should not expect kinder treatment (10:25).

**Do not fear, because the Father counts every hair.** Three times He says "do not be afraid" (10:26; 10:28; 10:31). Human opponents can only touch the body. Meanwhile the Father notices a single cheap sparrow. Fear is cured not by pretending danger isn't real, but by knowing how precious we are to our Father.

**Jesus claims first place.** Loving parents and children is good, yet loving them more than Christ puts even good love out of order (10:37). Losing our life for His sake is the only way we truly find it (10:39).

## Deeper Meaning

**Twelve men for twelve tribes.** Ancient Israel consisted of twelve tribes descended from Jacob's twelve sons (Genesis 49:28). By choosing exactly twelve apostles, Jesus signals that He is regathering a renewed Israel.

**First to Israel, then to all nations.** This initial mission is restricted to "the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (10:6). At the close of the Gospel, after the resurrection, Jesus sends the disciples to every nation (28:19). Paul follows the same order: first for the Jew, then for the Greek (Romans 1:16).

**Shaking off the dust.** Paul and Barnabas enacted this very instruction when driven out of Pisidian Antioch (Acts 13:51). It is not a spiteful curse, but a solemn gesture leaving responsibility with those who chose to reject the message.

**The Spirit supplies the words.** The promise of verses 19–20 came true when Peter, "filled with the Holy Spirit," stood before the Jewish rulers to defend the gospel (Acts 4:8). The book of Acts is full of moments like this.

**A sword dividing households.** Verses 35–36 quote Micah, where the prophet laments that a person's enemies are members of their own house (Micah 7:6). Micah described a time of social breakdown. Jesus says His own arrival forces a choice so decisive that it can divide even a family.

## Notes & Nuances

**Verse 10:23 is one of Matthew's hardest sayings.** Jesus says the disciples will not finish going through the towns of Israel before the Son of Man comes. Interpreters offer several views: He refers to His resurrection; to the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70; or to His final return, with the mission to Israel still unfinished until then. Some scholars think Jesus expected the end of the age to arrive much sooner.

**"A sword" is not a call to violence.** Luke records the parallel saying using the word "division" (Luke 12:51). The sword here is an image of separation, not an endorsement of weapons. Jesus Himself later tells Peter to put his sword away (26:52).

**Carry a staff or not?** Matthew reports taking no staff, while Mark allows a single staff (Mark 6:8–9). Some reconcile this by reading Matthew as forbidding acquiring an extra staff for the trip while Mark lets them keep the one already in hand. Either way, both stress traveling light and leaning on God.

**Who is "the One who can destroy both soul and body"?** A few readers think this refers to the devil. Most commentators agree it means God Himself, since Scripture never instructs believers to fear Satan (10:28). Nor does the verse paint God as a tyrant, because the very next sentence speaks of sparrows held safely in the Father's care (10:29).

## What Commentators Say

**John Chrysostom**, bishop of Constantinople in the late fourth century, preached on "like sheep among wolves." He remarked: as long as we remain sheep, we conquer, even surrounded by thousands of wolves. The moment we turn into wolves ourselves, we are defeated, because we forfeit the Shepherd's help. *(Homilies on Matthew, Homily 33)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: the saying "as long as we remain sheep we conquer" in Homily 33 is very widely quoted -->

**Albert Schweitzer**, the Franco-German theologian and medical missionary, placed 10:23 at the center of his famous book on the historical Jesus. He argued that Jesus genuinely expected the end of the world to arrive before the twelve returned from their tour. When it did not, Jesus resolved to step into suffering Himself to force the kingdom to come. Most scholars reject Schweitzer's conclusion, yet the question he raised is still debated today. *(The Quest of the Historical Jesus, 1906)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: the pivotal role of 10:23 in Schweitzer's thesis is widely cited -->

**Thomas of Celano**, a thirteenth-century friar who wrote the first biography of Francis of Assisi, tells how Francis heard the Gospel passage instructing disciples to carry no gold or silver, no bag, no extra tunic, sandals, or staff. Francis cried out with joy: this is what I want to do with all my heart. He took off his shoes, dropped his staff, kept a single tunic, and went out preaching repentance. **Dietrich Bonhoeffer**, the German pastor executed by the Nazis in 1945, devoted a chapter to Matthew 10. He emphasizes that the messengers bring nothing of their own—neither force nor human strategy. Their sole authority is God's word, and they are forbidden to coerce anyone into accepting it.
