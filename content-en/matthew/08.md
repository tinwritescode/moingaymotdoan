# Matthew 8 — Authority Over Sickness, Storms, and Demons

## Story

When Jesus came down from the mountain, large crowds followed Him. A man with leprosy came and knelt before Him: "Lord, if You are willing, You can make me clean." Jesus reached out His hand and touched him: "I am willing. Be clean!" Immediately his leprosy was cleansed. Jesus told him not to tell anyone, but to go show himself to the priest and offer the gift Moses commanded, as a testimony to them (8:1–4). He touched the very person the Law forbade anyone to touch.

In Capernaum, a Roman centurion came asking for help: my servant lies paralyzed at home in terrible suffering. Jesus said: I will go and heal him. The centurion replied, "Lord, I am not worthy to have You come under my roof. But just say the word, and my servant will be healed." He too was a man under authority with soldiers under him; he tells one to go and he goes, another to come and he comes (8:5–9).

Jesus marveled and said to those following Him: I have not found anyone in Israel with such great faith. Many will come from east and west and recline at table with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven, while those who were meant to be sons of the kingdom will be thrown into outer darkness. Then He told the centurion: go, let it be done for you as you have believed. And the servant was healed at that very hour (8:10–13).

Entering Peter's house, Jesus saw Peter's mother-in-law lying in bed with a fever. He touched her hand, the fever left her, and she got up and began to serve Him (8:14–15). That evening, people brought many who were demon-possessed; with a single word He drove out the spirits and healed all who were sick. Matthew recalls Isaiah's words: "He Himself took on our infirmities and carried our diseases" (8:16–17).

Seeing the crowd around Him, Jesus gave orders to cross to the other side of the lake. A scribe came up and said: Teacher, I will follow You wherever You go. Jesus answered, "Foxes have dens and birds of the air have nests, but the Son of Man has no place to lay His head." Another disciple asked to go first and bury his father. Jesus said, "Follow Me, and let the dead bury their own dead" (8:18–22).

He got into the boat and His disciples followed Him. Suddenly a furious storm swept down on the lake so that waves swamped the boat, yet Jesus was sleeping. The disciples woke Him: "Lord, save us! We are perishing!" He replied: why are you afraid, you of little faith? Then He rose and rebuked the winds and the sea, and there was a great calm. They marveled, asking, "What kind of man is this? Even the winds and the sea obey Him!" (8:23–27).

Across the lake, in the region of the Gadarenes, He met two demon-possessed men coming out of the tombs, so violent that no one dared pass that way. They shouted: what do You want with us, Son of God? Have You come here to torture us before the appointed time? (8:28–29).

Some distance away a large herd of pigs was feeding. The demons begged Him: if You drive us out, send us into the herd of pigs. He said to them, "Go!" The demons came out of the two men, entered the pigs, and the whole herd rushed down the steep bank into the sea and drowned (8:30–32).

The herdsmen ran off into the town and reported everything. The whole town came out to meet Jesus. Yet when they saw Him, they did not ask Him to stay; they begged Him to leave their region (8:33–34).

## Key Takeaways

**Teaching is backed by action.** Chapters 5–7 show Jesus teaching with authority. Chapters 8–9 show Him acting with that same authority—over sickness, demons, and even wind and waves. When He speaks, it happens.

**Jesus touches the untouchable.** A leper was barred from coming near anyone. Jesus could have healed him with a single word, yet He chose to reach out and touch him (8:3). For someone nobody had touched in years, that hand on his skin was already part of the healing.

**Great faith can come from unexpected places.** The person Jesus praises for having the greatest faith is an officer in the occupying army (8:10). He grasped what many within Israel had not yet grasped: Jesus only needs to give the word, and everything must obey.

**Following Jesus carries a cost.** Jesus never promises comfort or security to would-be followers (8:20). And His call claims priority even over the most sacred family obligations of Jewish life, like burying a father (8:22).

**"What kind of man is this?"** That is the disciples' question after the storm, and it is the question of the entire chapter (8:27). The sick, the centurion, the demons, and the wind and waves all give the same answer. Only the townspeople of Gadara choose to ask Him to leave (8:34).

## Deeper Meaning

**Leprosy under the Law of Moses.** The Law required someone with leprosy to tear their clothes, cry out "Unclean! Unclean!" and live alone outside the camp (Leviticus 13:45–46). Anyone who touched them became unclean too. Here the flow reverses: Jesus touches him, and His cleanness spreads to the sick man.

**The banquet of the nations.** Isaiah foresaw a day when the Lord would prepare on His mountain "a banquet of rich food" for all peoples (Isaiah 25:6). Jesus says people from east and west will come and sit at table with Abraham (8:11). The centurion is the first guest through the door.

**The Servant bearing our sicknesses.** Matthew quotes Isaiah 53, the chapter about the Servant who suffers on behalf of the people (Isaiah 53:4). Jesus' healings are the opening act of a deeper carrying of human suffering that culminates at the cross.

**Only God stills the sea.** The Psalms picture sailors caught in a tempest crying out to the Lord, and "He calmed the storm to a whisper" (Psalms 107:29). In the Old Testament, rule over the raging sea belongs to God alone (Psalms 89:9). That is why the disciples are not merely relieved after the storm—they are awestruck by the One sitting in their boat.

**A greater Jonah.** Jonah also slept below deck during a storm while sailors had to wake him (Jonah 1:5–6). Yet Jonah stilled the sea by being thrown overboard. Jesus stills the sea with a word. Later, Jesus Himself will point to "the sign of the prophet Jonah" (12:39–41).

## Notes & Nuances

**Matthew counts two demon-possessed men; Mark and Luke count one.** The region's name differs too: Gadarenes here, Gerasenes in Mark (Mark 5:1). Some suggest Mark and Luke focus on the more prominent man. The place names likely differ because Gadara was the major city of the area, while Gerasa referred to a village or broader district. Matthew regularly mentions pairs where other Gospels mention one (cf. 20:30).

**Was the loss of the pigs unfair?** Some critics, including the philosopher Bertrand Russell, fault Jesus for letting a whole herd of pigs perish. Commentators respond in several ways: the demons themselves asked to enter the pigs and destroyed them; a human life is worth far more than a herd of animals; and the episode exposes how demons always seek destruction. Even so, the story leaves a genuine question about the owners' economic loss.

**"Let the dead bury their own dead" sounds jarring.** Some think the father had not yet died and the son wanted to stay home until his father passed away. Others think he wanted to wait for the secondary burial of the bones a year later, a custom of that time. Either way, the core point is that the call of Jesus cannot wait. The first "dead" likely refers to those spiritually dead.

**Does 8:17 promise immediate healing for every illness today?** Some teachers argue that because Christ died for us, every believer has a right to physical healing right now. Yet Paul had a thorn in the flesh that was not removed, and Timothy suffered frequent stomach ailments. Most commentators understand that complete healing is guaranteed, yet arrives in full only when Christ returns.

## What Commentators Say

**Geza Vermes**, a Hungarian-born Jewish scholar who taught at Oxford, places Jesus alongside other Jewish miracle-workers in Galilee. He cites a Talmudic account of Rabbi Hanina ben Dosa: asked to pray for Gamaliel's son, he prayed from afar and then told the messengers the boy was well, and at that exact hour the fever broke. **Martin Hengel**, a German New Testament scholar, wrote an entire monograph around the saying "let the dead bury their own dead." For Jews of that era, burying a parent was such a supreme obligation that it even excused one from reciting daily prayers. No teacher of the Law would have dared tell a pupil to walk away from it. Jesus' command makes sense only if He claimed an authority standing above the Law itself. *(The Charismatic Leader and His Followers, 1968)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: the entire book begins from 8:22 and this thesis is its centerpiece -->

**Augustine**, bishop in North Africa over 1,600 years ago, preached on the centurion. He noted that the officer declared himself unworthy for Christ to enter his house, and precisely that humility made him worthy for Christ to enter his heart. The centurion stands as an image of the Gentiles, who believe without seeing Christ with their eyes. *(Sermons)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: confident Augustine highlights the centurion's humility in his sermons; uncertain of the exact sermon number -->

**John Calvin**, the sixteenth-century Reformer in Geneva, observes that Isaiah 53 speaks primarily of sin, whereas Matthew applies it to bodily illnesses.
