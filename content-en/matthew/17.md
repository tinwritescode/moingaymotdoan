# Matthew 17 — The Transfiguration on the Mountain

## Story

Six days later, Jesus took Peter, James, and James's brother John and led them up a high mountain by themselves. There He was transfigured before them: His face shone like the sun, and His clothes became as white as the light. Suddenly Moses and Elijah appeared, talking with Him (17:1–3). The Law and the Prophets stand beside the One who fulfills them both.

Peter spoke up: Lord, it is good for us to be here; if You wish, I will put up three shelters—one for You, one for Moses, and one for Elijah. While he was still speaking, a bright cloud enveloped them, and a voice from the cloud declared: this is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; "Listen to Him!" (17:4–5).

Hearing this, the disciples fell facedown to the ground, terrified. Jesus came and touched them: "Get up. Do not be afraid." When they looked up, they saw no one except Jesus alone (17:6–8).

Coming down the mountain, He instructed them not to tell anyone what they had seen until the Son of Man had risen from the dead. They asked: why then do the scribes say that Elijah must come first? He replied: Elijah is indeed coming and will restore all things; yet Elijah has already come, and they did not recognize him, but did to him whatever they wished; in the same way the Son of Man is going to suffer at their hands. Then the disciples understood He was speaking to them about John the Baptist (17:9–13).

Returning to the crowd, a man came up and knelt before Him: Lord, have mercy on my son, for he suffers from seizures and is in terrible distress, often falling into fire or water. I brought him to Your disciples, yet they could not heal him (17:14–16).

Jesus exclaimed, "O unbelieving and perverse generation," how long shall I stay with you and put up with you? Bring the boy here to Me. He rebuked the demon, it came out of the boy, and he was healed from that very hour (17:17–18).

The disciples came to Him privately and asked: why couldn't we drive it out? He replied: because of your little faith. If you have faith as small as a mustard seed, you can say to this mountain, "Move from here to there," and it will move, and nothing will be impossible for you. He added: this kind does not go out except by prayer and fasting (17:19–21).

While they were gathering in Galilee, He told them again: the Son of Man is about to be delivered into the hands of men; they will kill Him, and on the third day He will be raised to life. And the disciples were filled with grief (17:22–23).

Arriving in Capernaum, the collectors of the temple tax approached Peter: doesn't your teacher pay the temple tax? Yes, He does, Peter answered. When Peter entered the house, Jesus spoke first: what do you think, Simon—from whom do the kings of the earth collect duty and taxes, from their own sons or from others? From others, Peter replied. Then the sons are exempt, Jesus said (17:24–26).

Yet so that we may not offend them, He told Peter to go to the sea and throw out a line; take the first fish you catch, open its mouth, and you will find a silver coin; take it and give it to them for Me and for you (17:27).

## Key Takeaways

**Glory is revealed before the cross.** Six days earlier, Jesus spoke of His death and the cost of following Him (16:21–24). Now on the mountain, three disciples see His true glory (17:2). They need this glimpse before having to watch Him crucified.

**"Listen to Him!"** Peter wants to build three shelters, putting Jesus on the same level as Moses and Elijah. The voice from heaven interrupts him (17:4–5). In the end, the disciples "saw no one except Jesus alone" (17:8). The Law and the Prophets point to Him, then step aside.

**From the mountaintop down to the valley.** Right at the foot of the mountain wait a tormented child, a desperate father, and powerless disciples (17:14–16). Mountaintop glory is not meant to be hoarded on the summit, but carried down into suffering.

**Small faith that is genuine is enough.** The disciples failed not because their faith was too small in quantity, but because it was weak and divided (17:20). Faith no bigger than a mustard seed, placed in God, can move mountains. The power lies in the One trusted, not in the size of the believer.

**The Son is free, yet chooses not to cause offense.** As Son of the Lord of the temple, Jesus owes no temple tax (17:26). Even so, He pays it so as not to put a stumbling block in anyone's way (17:27). Real freedom knows how to yield out of love for others.

## Deeper Meaning

**A new Mount Sinai.** Moses climbed Mount Sinai, cloud covered the mountain for six days, and then God called to him out of the cloud (Exodus 24:16). When Moses came down, his face was radiant (Exodus 34:29). Here, "after six days," on a high mountain, come cloud and voice, and Jesus' face shines like the sun (17:1–5). Yet Moses' face merely reflected light; Jesus radiates light from Himself.

**A prophet like Moses.** Moses promised God would raise up a prophet like him and commanded, "You must listen to him" (Deuteronomy 18:15). The command "Listen to Him!" spoken from the cloud reveals who that Prophet is.

**Peter never forgot.** Years later, 2 Peter recalls: we ourselves heard this voice borne from heaven when we were with Him "on the sacred mountain" (2 Peter 1:17–18). The writer appeals to eyewitness sight and hearing as proof that the gospel is no clever myth.

**Elijah has already come.** Malachi foretold that Elijah would come to turn the hearts of fathers to their children before the great day of the Lord (Malachi 4:6). Jesus explains that Elijah came in John and was killed (17:12). The fate of the forerunner previews the fate of the One following behind him.

**Tax for the Father's house.** The Law of Moses required every Israelite to pay half a shekel as "an offering to the LORD" for temple service (Exodus 30:13). Jesus quietly implies: I am the Son of the One worshiped inside that temple.

## Notes & Nuances

**What did three shelters mean?** Peter likely had the Feast of Tabernacles in mind, when Jews lived in temporary booths for seven days to commemorate the wilderness journey (Leviticus 23:42). He wanted to freeze that glorious moment in place. Yet the age of permanent dwelling had not yet arrived; first lay the road to the cross.

**Verse 17:21 is absent from many early manuscripts.** Traditional Bibles include "But this kind does not go out except by prayer and fasting." Many modern translations place this verse in a footnote because it is missing from the oldest Greek manuscripts and was likely copied in from Mark 9:29.

**Mountain-moving faith is not magic.** Speaking of telling a mountain to move was a common Jewish figure of speech for overcoming the impossible (17:20). Jesus is not promising we can get anything we fancy, but assuring us that nothing is too hard for God when we trust Him.

**The coin in the fish's mouth.** Matthew does not actually narrate Peter going fishing and finding the coin (17:27). Most readers understand a quiet miracle took place. The point of the episode is not the fish, however, but Jesus' stance toward the temple tax.

## What Commentators Say

**Joseph Ratzinger**, later Pope Benedict XVI, reads the Transfiguration alongside the Feast of Tabernacles. He notes that the detail "after six days" and Peter's desire to pitch tents both point to that festival. Peter rightly sensed that the Messianic age had dawned, yet erred in trying to stay put prematurely. The true dwelling place of God with humanity will be Jesus' own body. *(Jesus of Nazareth, volume 1, chapter 9)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: the chapter connecting the Transfiguration to the Feast of Tabernacles is a prominent section of volume 1 -->

**Gregory Palamas**, the fourteenth-century Eastern Orthodox archbishop of Thessalonica, argued that the light on the mountain was no created light. It was the uncreated light of God Himself, always present in Christ; on the mountain, the disciples' eyes were simply opened to perceive it. Contemplative monks in the Orthodox tradition believe believers in deep prayer can likewise behold that divine light. *(Triads in Defense of the Holy Hesychasts)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: the uncreated light of Mount Tabor is the central thesis of Palamas in the Triads -->

**John Chrysostom**, bishop of Constantinople in the late fourth century, asks why Moses and Elijah specifically appeared. He gives several reasons: crowds wondered if Jesus was Elijah or another prophet, so He displays both men as His servants; critics accused Him of breaking the Law, so Moses the lawgiver stands beside Him; and because Moses died while Elijah was taken up alive, Jesus is shown to be Lord of both the dead and the living. *(Homilies on Matthew, on Matthew 17)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: confident Chrysostom enumerates these reasons; uncertain of the exact homily number -->

**John Calvin**, the sixteenth-century Reformer in Geneva, draws a lesson about Christian liberty from the temple tax. Jesus had every right to claim exemption, yet voluntarily waived His right to avoid causing weak observers to stumble. Christians should do the same, Calvin writes: know our freedom, yet exercise it in love rather than flaunting it to confuse others. *(Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists, on Matthew 17)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: confident Calvin draws the lesson of yielding one's rights for others' sake; uncertain of exact phrasing -->
