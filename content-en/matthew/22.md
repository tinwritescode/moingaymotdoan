# Matthew 22 — The Wedding Banquet and the Trap Questions

## Story

Jesus tells another parable: the kingdom of heaven is like a king who prepared a wedding banquet for his son. He sent his servants to summon those invited, yet they refused to come. He sent other servants with word: I have prepared my dinner, my oxen and fattened cattle have been slaughtered, everything is ready; come to the wedding feast! Still they paid no attention—one went off to his farm, another to his business, while the rest seized his servants, mistreated them, and killed them. Enraged, the king sent his troops, destroyed those murderers, and burned their city (22:1–7).

The king told his servants: the wedding feast is ready, yet those invited were not worthy. Go out to the crossroads and invite to the banquet anyone you find. The servants went into the streets and gathered everyone they found, both bad and good, and the wedding hall filled with guests (22:8–10).

Coming in to look at the guests, the king spotted a man not wearing wedding clothes and asked, "Friend, how did you get in here without wedding clothes?" The man was speechless. The king ordered his attendants to bind him hand and foot and throw him into outer darkness. "For many are called, but few are chosen" (22:11–14).

The Pharisees plotted how to trap Jesus in His own words. They sent their disciples along with the Herodians, opening with flattery: Teacher, we know You are a man of integrity who fears no one. Then they asked: is it lawful to pay taxes to Caesar or not? (22:15–17). Say "yes" and lose the people; say "no" and invite Roman arrest.

Knowing their malice, He replied: you hypocrites, why are you testing Me? Show Me the coin used for paying the tax. They brought Him a denarius. He asked, "Whose image is this, and whose inscription?" Caesar's, they answered. He declared, "Then give to Caesar what is Caesar's, and to God what is God's." Amazed, they left Him and went away (22:18–22).

That same day the Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, approached Him with a riddle: Moses taught that if a man dies childless, his brother must marry the widow to raise up offspring for him. Seven brothers in turn married the same woman and all died childless; finally the woman died too. At the resurrection, whose wife will she be of the seven? (22:23–28).

Jesus replied: you are in error because you do not know the Scriptures or the power of God. At the resurrection people neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are like angels in heaven. As for the resurrection of the dead, have you not read what God spoke to you: I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob? He is not the God of the dead, but of the living. Hearing this, the crowds were astonished at His teaching (22:29–33).

Hearing He had silenced the Sadducees, the Pharisees regrouped. An expert in the Law tested Him: Teacher, which is the greatest commandment in the Law? He answered, "Love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind." This is the first and greatest commandment. And the second is like it: "Love your neighbor as yourself." All the Law and the Prophets hang on these two commandments (22:34–40).

Then Jesus turned and questioned the Pharisees: what do you think about the Christ—whose son is He? The son of David, they replied. He asked: how is it then that David, speaking by the Spirit, calls Him "Lord," saying, "The Lord said to my Lord, 'Sit at My right hand'"? If David calls Him "Lord," how can He be his son? No one could answer Him a word, and from that day on nobody dared ask Him any more questions (22:41–46).

## Key Takeaways

**God's invitation is an invitation to a celebration.** The kingdom of heaven is compared not to a courtroom or a lecture hall, but to a wedding feast (22:2). The tragedy is never a shortage of good food on God's table, but invited guests too busy with private affairs to care (22:5).

**The doors stand open to everyone, yet guests must wear wedding clothes.** The king invites both bad and good off the crossroads (22:10). Nobody enters by earning an invitation. Yet the guest without wedding clothes warns us: approaching God while refusing to let Him change us is as good as staying away.

**Give back to whoever bears the stamp.** The coin carries Caesar's portrait, so give it back to Caesar (22:21). Human beings carry God's image, so our whole selves belong to God. Jesus neither dismisses civic duty nor places it anywhere near our duty to God.

**God is the God of the living.** The Sadducees crafted a silly puzzle to ridicule resurrection. Jesus exposes their double ignorance: they understand neither Scripture nor God's power (22:29). The age to come is not merely this life stretched longer, but a transformed new life.

**Two commandments hold the entire Law together.** Love God completely, and love your neighbor as yourself (22:37–39). Every other command hangs on those two pegs. We cannot truly love God while hating people, nor love people completely while forgetting God.

## Deeper Meaning

**The wedding supper of the Lamb.** Isaiah envisioned the banquet God prepares for all nations on His mountain (Isaiah 25:6). Revelation brings that story home: "Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb!" (Revelation 19:9). Jesus' parable bridges promise and fulfillment.

**Garments of salvation.** Isaiah rejoiced because the Lord "clothed me with garments of salvation" and wrapped him in a robe of righteousness like a bridegroom on his wedding day (Isaiah 61:10). Many readers see the wedding garment in this light: clothing not stitched by our own merit, but supplied by God.

**God's image stamped on humanity.** When Jesus asked, "Whose image is this?" (22:20), Scripture-literate listeners instantly remembered that God created humanity "in His own image" (Genesis 1:27). Paul later tells believers to pay taxes to whom taxes are due (Romans 13:7), while reserving worship for God alone.

**The God of Abraham.** Jesus quotes God's words to Moses at the burning bush (Exodus 3:6), deliberately drawing from the five books of Moses because the Sadducees accepted only those five books as Scripture. God did not say "I was" the God of the patriarchs, but "I am." Relationship with the living God does not end at the grave.

**Two commandments from two books of the Law.** The first comes from Israel's daily confession: "Love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your strength" (Deuteronomy 6:5). The second comes from Leviticus (Leviticus 19:18). John insists the two cannot be separated: anyone claiming to love God while hating a brother is a liar (1 John 4:20).

**David's son and David's Lord.** Psalms 110 is the Old Testament verse quoted most frequently in the New Testament (Psalms 110:1). At Pentecost, Peter uses this very line to proclaim that Jesus has been exalted to the Father's right hand (Acts 2:34–36).

## Notes & Nuances

**Does the burning of the city allude to AD 70?** The detail of the king dispatching troops to burn the murderers' city (22:7) reminds many readers of Rome's burning of Jerusalem in AD 70. Some think Matthew wrote after that disaster; others see either prophecy or standard ancient imagery for royal vengeance.

**What does the wedding garment symbolize?** A popular explanation claims ancient kings supplied robes to guests, making refusal a deliberate insult—though solid historical proof for that custom is slim. Commentators variously interpret the garment as God-given righteousness, repentance, or a transformed life of good works. The shared point is clear: being freely invited does not mean treating the King casually.

**"Like angels" does not mean humans turn into angels after death.** Jesus simply says that in the resurrection there is no marriage (22:30). He neither claims believers become angels nor implies that deep bonds of love between spouses vanish.

**Does "give to Caesar" establish a strict separation of church and state?** The saying is often cited to wall religion off from public life. For Jesus, however, nothing stands outside God's sovereignty—including Caesar. The line both acknowledges legitimate taxation and draws a sharp boundary: Caesar has zero claim over what belongs exclusively to God.

## What Commentators Say

**Tertullian**, a theologian in Carthage, North Africa, around AD 200, reflected on 22:21. Give back to Caesar the image of Caesar stamped on the coin, he wrote, and give back to God the image of God stamped on the human soul. **The Talmud**, the collection of rabbinic commentary, preserves numerous debates between Pharisees and those who denied the resurrection. Rabbis searched the five books of Moses for proof of life after death. One argument appealed to God's oath promising the land to the patriarchs personally: since they died without possessing it, they must rise again to receive it. Jesus' reasoning in 22:32 moves along closely related lines. *(Babylonian Talmud, Sanhedrin 90b)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: proofs of the resurrection from Torah, including the land promise to the patriarchs, appear in Sanhedrin 90b -->

**Rabbi Akiva**, the famed early second-century Jewish sage, declared that "love your neighbor as yourself" is the supreme governing principle of Torah, preserved in the rabbinic commentary on Leviticus. Identifying a single core summary of the Law was therefore a familiar Jewish pursuit. What distinguishes Jesus is fusing two commandments from two separate books into one inseparable pair. *(Sifra, on Leviticus 19)* <!-- confidence: high | verified: no | reason: Akiva's statement on Leviticus 19:18 in Sifra is widely cited -->

**Martin Luther**, the sixteenth-century German Reformer, wrote a treatise on the limits of civil government drawing extensively on 22:21. God established two modes of rule, he explained: civil authorities wield the sword to preserve outward order, while God's word rules the heart. Governments hold authority over body and property, never over the soul.
