# Song of Solomon 7 — The Wife's Beauty and an Invitation to the Countryside

## Story

Chapter 7 flows straight out of the call to the Shulammite at the end of chapter 6. The man—or, in several translations, the chorus admiring her at first—gazes on her and praises her from her feet upward to the crown of her head. Her sandaled feet move with royal grace, the curves of her thighs gleam like jewels shaped by a master craftsman, and her waist and belly radiate warmth like a rounded goblet never lacking spiced wine and a mound of wheat encircled by lilies (7:1–2).

His gaze climbs upward: her breasts are soft as twin fawns of a gazelle, her pale neck stately as an ivory tower, her eyes deep and luminous like the pools by the gate of Heshbon, her nose regal as the tower of Lebanon overlooking Damascus (7:3–4). Her head rises proudly like Mount Carmel, and her dark hair shimmers with royal purple sheen so captivating that a king himself is held captive in its tresses (7:5).

After praising her form, the man's voice becomes warmer and more direct: he exclaims how lovely and delightful she is in her love. Comparing her slender stature to a date palm, he longs to embrace her, savoring breath sweet as apples and kisses smooth as the finest wine (7:6–9). Right midway through verse 9, many translations hear the woman's tender voice gently finish his sentence, reserving that sweet wine wholly for her beloved (7:9).

The woman then declares their unshakable bond: "I belong to my beloved, and his desire is for me" (7:10). Taking the lead, she invites him to leave the city behind, head out into the countryside, and spend the night among the villages. Early next morning they will walk out to the vineyards to see if the vines have budded, the blossoms have opened, and the pomegranates are in flower—and in that peaceful retreat she will give him her love (7:11–12). She adds that the mandrakes are giving off their fragrance, and right at their door wait every kind of choice fruit, both new and old, saved up exclusively for him (7:13).

## Key Takeaways

**Love cherishes the whole person without embarrassment.** Far from dismissing the body, the poem celebrates a wife's beauty with rich, joyful imagery from sandaled feet up to eyes and hair (7:1–5). Inside marriage, physical delight walks hand in hand with royal honor—addressing her as "daughter of the prince" (7:1).

**Knowing we are deeply wanted quiets insecurity.** For the third time in the Song, the woman affirms their mutual belonging—and this time adds: "his desire is for me" (7:10). When a wife knows her husband's heart turns wholly toward her, fear and jealousy melt away, freeing love to grow confident and joyful.

**Love needs unhurried retreats to stay fresh.** The woman invites him away from noise and crowds out into the countryside, waking early to watch vineyards and pomegranates bloom (7:11–12). Even the deepest love thrives when couples carve out quiet spaces reserved strictly for each other.

**Lasting love weaves treasured memories with fresh delight.** The "delicacies, new as well as old" she saves for him (7:13) capture the beauty of mature marriage: honoring shared history and faithfulness while always offering fresh affection.

## Deeper Meaning

**Healing the fracture of Eden.** The declaration "his desire is for me" (7:10) deliberately echoes the exact rare Hebrew word spoken after the Fall in Genesis, where the woman's desire was tangled up with the husband's harsh rule over her (Genesis 3:16). Here in Song of Solomon 7:10, that curse is reversed: dominance and power struggle give way to a husband's affectionate longing directed joyfully toward his wife.

**Self-giving, cherishing love in Christian marriage.** The husband's admiration and wholehearted devotion in chapter 7 reflect Paul's charge to husbands to love their wives as their own bodies, nourishing and cherishing them just as Christ cares for the church (Ephesians 5:28–29).

**The flourishing land as love's backdrop.** Landmarks stretching across Israel—Mount Carmel, Lebanon, and Heshbon (7:4–5)—weave the wife's beauty directly into the lush Promised Land God gifted His people. Human love and God's good creation sing together in harmony.

## Notes & Nuances

**Praising from feet to head (7:1–5).** Unlike chapter 4 (which moves from head downward), chapter 7 sweeps upward from sandaled feet to flowing hair. Reversing the direction adds lively motion—many scholars link this poem with the joyful wedding dance referenced right before it in 6:13.

**Mandrakes (7:13).** In the ancient Mediterranean world, *mandrakes* were aromatic spring plants associated in folklore with romance and fertility—appearing earlier in the story of Rachel and Leah (Genesis 30:14–16). Here their scent signals springtime vitality and marital warmth.

**Purple-sheened hair (7:5).** Her hair is not literally dyed violet; *purple* evokes the glossy, blue-black luster of costly royal fabric, highlighting how majestic she looks in her beloved's eyes.
