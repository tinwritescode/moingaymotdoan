# Deuteronomy 27 — Inscribed Stones and Twelve Curses on Mount Ebal

## Story

Moses and the elders instruct Israel on a solemn ceremony to carry out as soon as they cross the Jordan into the Promised Land. They must set up large stones on Mount Ebal, coat them with plaster, and write all the words of this law very clearly upon them. Right at that same spot, they must build an altar to the LORD from uncut stones untouched by any iron tool, offer burnt offerings and peace offerings, and eat joyfully in God's presence (27:1–8).

Next, Moses and the Levitical priests hush all Israel to listen carefully: this very day they have become the people of the LORD their God, so they must obey His voice and follow every command He gives (27:9–10).

On that same day, Moses arranges a dramatic ceremony between two facing mountains in the region of Shechem. Six tribes—Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Joseph, and Benjamin—will stand on Mount Gerizim to bless the people. The remaining six tribes—Reuben, Gad, Asher, Zebulun, Dan, and Naphtali—will stand on Mount Ebal to pronounce the curses (27:11–13).

The Levites will shout out twelve curses in a loud voice, and after each one all the people must respond in unison, "Amen"—meaning *so be it*. The curses target sins usually committed in secret where human courts rarely catch them: crafting an idol and hiding it in a secret place; dishonoring father or mother; secretly shifting a neighbor's boundary marker; deliberately misleading a blind person on the road; denying justice to foreigners, orphans, and widows; incest and bestiality; striking down a neighbor in secret; and accepting a bribe to murder an innocent person. The twelfth curse sweeps across everything: "Cursed is he who does not put the words of this law into practice!" And once more all the people answer together, "Amen!" (27:14–26).

## Key Takeaways

**God's law stands openly in daylight, never hidden as elite secrets.** Writing the entire law plainly on whitewashed stones atop a mountain meant every ordinary Israelite could see and know how to live (27:2–8). Biblical faith never locks God's word inside a privileged circle, but displays it openly before the whole community.

**God sees even the sins committed in darkness.** What unites the first eleven curses is striking: nearly all describe wrongs difficult for human courts to catch—hidden idols, shifting boundary stones at night, tricking the blind who cannot see your face, ambushing a neighbor with no witnesses, or pocketing secret bribes (27:15–25). Human justice can be evaded; God cannot.

**Saying *Amen* binds the community's own conscience.** No one forced Israel to say *Amen* after each curse. By answering in unison, they publicly acknowledged that God's righteous standards are true and willingly placed themselves under His judgment if they broke faith (27:15–26).

**The altar and joyful meal stand right on the mountain of cursing.** Here is a startling detail brimming with grace: the uncut stone altar for burnt offerings and peace offerings stands on Mount Ebal—the mountain of curses (27:4–7). Right where sin is exposed, God places a sacrifice of reconciliation and a meal of joyful fellowship.

## Deeper Meaning

**Paul quotes 27:26 to show humanity cannot save itself by keeping the law.** The twelfth curse demands complete obedience to every word of the law—a standard no sinful human can achieve. In Galatians, Paul quotes 27:26 directly to conclude that all who rely on works of the law stand under a curse; yet Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us on a tree, so that Abraham's blessing might flow to all nations through faith (Galatians 3:10–14).

**Joshua carried out this ceremony in full upon entering Canaan.** Right after capturing Jericho and Ai, Joshua gathered the whole nation in the valley between Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal, built the uncut stone altar, inscribed the law on stones, and read every blessing and curse aloud before men, women, children, and resident foreigners (Joshua 8:30–35). History confirms chapter 27 did not stay on paper; it became Israel's spiritual inauguration inside the Promised Land.

## Notes & Nuances

**Why does this chapter list twelve curses without listing corresponding blessings?** Although verse 12 assigns six tribes to Mount Gerizim for blessing, chapter 27 records only the twelve curses. Many scholars note that the formal blessings immediately open chapter 28 (28:1–14), balancing the long curses that follow. It is also likely the actual Shechem ceremony recited both blessings and curses as Joshua 8:33–34 describes, while chapter 27 highlights the curses to expose hidden sins that quietly rot a covenant from within.

**The division of tribes between the two mountains was deliberate.** All six tribes standing on Mount Gerizim (blessing) descended from Jacob's two primary wives, Leah and Rachel. Standing on Mount Ebal (cursing) were the four tribes descended from the maidservants Bilhah and Zilpah, joined by Reuben (who forfeited his birthright through sin with his father's concubine) and Zebulun (Leah's youngest son). The valley between these two peaks forms a natural amphitheater whose acoustics carry voices crisply across the slopes.

**Were the words painted or carved on the plastered stones?** In ancient Egypt and the Near East, scribes commonly coated monumental stones with lime plaster and wrote on them in bold ink, or incised letters deeply into plastered stone so the script stood out legibly (27:2–3; 27:8). Both practices underscore the command that the law be displayed very plainly and durably.

**Peter C. Craigie**, Canadian Old Testament scholar, observes that the twelve curses of chapter 27 target the *blind spots* human courts cannot easily police. By placing hidden offenses under public covenant curses answered with communal *Amen*, the Mount Ebal liturgy awakens individual conscience before an all-seeing God. *(The Book of Deuteronomy, NICOT, 1976)* <!-- confidence: medium | verified: no | reason: emphasizing the hidden nature of the offenses in 27:15–25 is a familiar point in Craigie's commentary; exact page numbers not verified -->
